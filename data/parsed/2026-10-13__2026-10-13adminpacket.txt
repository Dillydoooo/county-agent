In the Matter of Continuing the Suspension of 
)
Acceptance of Applications for Mineral
)
Order No. 2026-059
Exploration Permits and Mining Leases 
)
WHEREAS, by Board Order 2026-040, the Board of County Commissioners suspended 
the acceptance and processing of applications for mineral exploration permits, mining leases, 
and mining permits for a period of one year, until October 8, 2026; and
WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners intends to update Chapter 5.15 of the 
Josephine County Code which pertains to mining; and
WHEREAS, the consideration and approval of mineral exploration permits and mining 
leases costs public resources including staff time and consulting fees; and
WHEREAS, the Forestry Program should focus entirely on preparing and offering timber for 
sale as a steady and reliable source of revenue for the County; 
NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that the suspension of the acceptance 
and processing of applications for mineral exploration permits, mining leases, and mining permits  
be continued for an additional period of one (1) year, effective October 8, 2026; and
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that any applications that are currently pending are to be 
returned to the applicant along with a full refund of money paid by the applicant to the County; 
and 
IT IS FURTHER ORDERED that this suspension will remain in effect until October 8, 
2027, unless it is revoked or extended by a formal act of the Board of Commissioners.

RE:  Support for Continued Secure Rural Schools Funding
Dear Senators Wyden, Merkley, Crapo and Risch and Congressman Bentz:
On behalf of Josephine County, we write to express our strong support for continuation of the 
Secure Rural Schools and Community Self-Determination Program beyond federal fiscal year 
2026.
Josephine County has a substantial amount of federally owned, tax-exempt land. SRS funding 
helps provide stability for rural counties like ours by supporting roads, schools, public safety, 
emergency response, wildfire preparedness and other essential services that would otherwise be 
supported by a broader property-tax base.
We appreciate the bipartisan effort now underway to extend SRS for an additional three years. The 
October 1 legislation introduced by Senators Wyden, Merkley, Crapo and Risch is an important 
step toward preventing another lapse in funding.
Recent experience demonstrates why timely action is so important. SRS previously expired in 
2023, and counties were required to operate for an extended period without certainty regarding 
future payments before Congress ultimately restored the program through FY2026.

Item 3b
For county governments, uncertainty itself has consequences. We develop budgets, maintain roads 
and infrastructure, provide emergency services, and make long-term financial commitments. 
Retroactive funding may ultimately restore revenue, but it does not provide the predictability 
necessary for responsible budgeting and planning.
We respectfully urge Congress to:
1. Reauthorize SRS beyond FY2026 without another lapse in authorization;
2. Support passage of the current three-year extension; and
3. Continue working toward a permanent or substantially longer-term solution that provides 
dependable funding for counties containing large amounts of federally owned land.
Josephine County recognizes the important role federal lands play in our community and our 
economy. At the same time, those lands are exempt from local property taxation, limiting the 
revenue available to provide the services residents rely upon. SRS has long helped address that 
imbalance.
We appreciate your continued work on behalf of Oregon’s rural counties and welcome the 
opportunity to work with Oregon’s congressional delegation, the Association of Oregon Counties, 
and other federal-land counties toward a sustainable long-term solution.
Respectfully submitted,
