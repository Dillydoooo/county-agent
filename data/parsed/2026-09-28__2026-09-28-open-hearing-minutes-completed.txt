APPROVED ON OCTOBER 7, 2026 
BY THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS 
AT THE WEEKLY BUSINESS SESSION 
 
Open Session: September 28, 2026 
10:00 a.m. - Board of Commissioners Conference Room 
Attendance: Commissioners Ron A. Smith; Colene M. Martin; Gary L. Richardson; and Nikole Guzman, 
Recorder 
Chair Ron A. Smith convened the Board in Open Session at 10:00 a.m. 
1. OPEN SESSION 
a. To review and evaluate the employment-related performance of the chief executive 
officer of any public body, a public officer, employee or staff member who requests an 
open hearing. 
Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe, Interim Human Resources Director, explained that the purpose of the hearing was 
to provide the Commissioners an opportunity to ask questions about the previously issued job-performance 
concerns and to allow Facilities Director Ryan Johnson to respond. Mr. Johnson confirmed that he requested 
an open hearing and was prepared to address the matters identified in the notice. Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe stated 
that the Commissioners, rather than Human Resources, would conduct the questioning. 
Commissioner Martin began with the County communications system and asked why the condition and 
urgency of the radio infrastructure had not been more fully communicated to the current Board. Mr. Johnson 
stated that he had presented the matter during budget discussions, had spoken individually with 
Commissioners, and had participated in earlier discussions involving the Sheriff, Public Works and Finance. 
He described the system as obsolete and said a 2024 Motorola proposal and earlier efforts to obtain funding 
documented the longstanding need for replacement. Commissioners Martin and Richardson emphasized the 
need for a documented, forward-looking plan, including project priorities, costs and supporting information, 
to enable Board planning and funding decisions. 
Commissioner Martin questioned Mr. Johnson about his absence from the April 2 meeting with the Sheriff 
and Day Wireless and the status of the Board-approved equipment assessment. Mr. Johnson stated that he 
was home ill, learned of the meeting shortly beforehand, and had previously expressed concern that a 
roughly $32,000 assessment would duplicate information already available about the aging system. 
Commissioner Martin disagreed that the assessment was unnecessary and stated that it supplied information 
about equipment and facility conditions. Both Commissioners questioned why existing proposals and 
maintenance information had not been provided to them when the Board sought an assessment. Mr. Johnson 
acknowledged that he should have furnished the Motorola proposal, while maintaining that he had verbally 
communicated his concerns and that the County already had relevant information. 
Commissioner Richardson then reviewed his July request for an estimated cost for work at the Animal 
Shelter and questioned the time taken to provide planning information. Mr. Johnson stated that he attempted 
to obtain cost information, that a reliable estimate for a project exceeding $3 million would require work by 
design and construction professionals, and that project funding had not been identified. The Commissioners 
discussed his subsequent use of inflation-adjusted figures from a 2024 estimate and their expectations for 
timely updates. Mr. Johnson disputed the reference in his notice to a levy for capital upgrades, stating that 
the Animal Shelter levy funded operations. Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe clarified that the document supplied to Mr. 
Johnson was a notice of investigation, not findings of an investigation. 
The Board also discussed written communication and management-meeting attendance. Mr. Johnson 
acknowledged that he did not regularly attend general management meetings, explaining that he prioritized 
topics directly involving Facilities and sought specific documentation of concerns. Commissioners Martin 
and Richardson emphasized that directors should share information across departments and plan 
proactively. Mr. Johnson stated that Facilities operates subject to Board priorities and available funding.

Commissioner Martin asked about election-day access to the Clerk's Office. Mr. Johnson described 
automated door schedules and one manual unlocking assignment, and acknowledged a prior staffing 
oversight and an automated-system malfunction. The Board also questioned Facilities contracting and 
security planning, including Treasury and the courthouse. Mr. Johnson discussed purchasing thresholds, 
earlier projects, existing security plans and budget constraints. The parties disagreed about the extent and 
timing of information that should reach the Board. 
At 10:50 a.m., Chair Smith called a five-minute recess. 
2. EXECUTIVE SESSION - NOT HELD 
a. To review and evaluate the employment-related performance of the chief executive 
officer of any public body, a public officer, employee or staff member who does not 
request an open hearing - ORS 192.660(2)(i). 
An Executive Session was listed on the agenda but was not convened. Mr. Johnson requested an open 
hearing, and all discussion on the matter occurred in Open Session. 
3. OPEN SESSION CON'D 
a. Continuation of the requested open hearing after recess. 
The Board reconvened in Open Session at 10:55 a.m. Commissioner Martin questioned the Facilities 
maintenance process and the condition of older plumbing at the Fairgrounds, including the buildings used 
by 4-H. Mr. Johnson explained that the Fairgrounds plumbing had been modified over many years and that 
the allocation of major projects and building responsibilities was ultimately determined by the Board. 
Commissioner Martin again requested clearer documentation of prospective maintenance needs, including 
roofing and heating, ventilation and air-conditioning systems. 
Mr. Johnson stated that service needs are received by telephone or email, entered into Asset Essentials, 
prioritized and assigned to technicians. He said safety and security are primary considerations, that recurring 
preventive-maintenance work is generated through the system, and that other repairs are addressed 
according to available staffing and funds. In response to questions about building conditions and delayed 
maintenance, he described a maintenance budget of approximately $300,000 for County facilities and said 
that urgent failures frequently determine the order of work. Commissioners Martin and Richardson 
emphasized the importance of documented priority lists and planning ahead for repair and replacement 
needs. 
Commissioner Martin asked about the response to power outages and communications with departments 
and County tenants. Mr. Johnson stated that he contacts the utility, dispatches Facilities staff, and prioritizes 
critical operations such as Dispatch and the courts. Commissioner Martin described a tenant's difficulty 
reaching Facilities during a previous outage and requested at least an acknowledgment or status message. 
Mr. Johnson said he had returned the call after speaking with the utility and that emergency-response calls 
must be prioritized. In response to questions regarding ordinary communications, he stated that he attempts 
to answer emails and voicemail the same day when possible and also responds to calls outside normal 
working hours. 
Mr. Johnson stated that he believed he had responded to the concerns, referred to supporting correspondence 
from other department heads, requested removal of the notice from his employment record, and asked to 
receive any additional findings or allegations. Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe explained that the Commissioners would 
review his responses and meet subsequently to consider next steps. No personnel findings, disciplinary 
determination or other formal action were made at this hearing. 
There being no further business, Chair Smith adjourned the meeting at 11:11 a.m.
