1. Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest  
The Josephine County Board of Commissioners reviewed today’s agenda and determined there were no potential 
conflicts of interest.  
      2. Ordinance No. 2026-xxx; An Ordinance Amending Chapter 2.05 of the County Code Relating to the  
          Meetings of the Board of Commissioners 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel presented a draft ordinance amending Chapter 2.05 of the Josephine County Code 
relating to meetings of the Board of Commissioners. Hicks reviewed the ordinance adoption process required 
under the Josephine County Charter, including the requirement for two readings conducted at least thirteen days 
apart and opportunities for public comment prior to adoption. Hicks explained that the proposed amendment 
would simplify existing provisions relating to executive sessions by referencing applicable Oregon Public 
Meetings Law and Charter requirements rather than retaining more prescriptive local language.  
Hicks explained that the proposed revision was intended to provide greater flexibility as laws, court decisions, 
and administrative practices regarding executive sessions continue to evolve. Commissioners discussed recent 
challenges associated with executive session procedures and expressed support for updating the code to maintain 
consistency with current legal requirements while preserving transparency and compliance with Oregon law. The 
Board reached consensus to proceed with placing the ordinance on a future Weekly Business Session agenda. 
 
3. Administrative Policy Revisions: 
a. A-04; Contract Signing Authority 
b. D-02; Position Requisitions 
c. D-03; Personnel Actions 
d. A-07; Advisory Bodies 
Legal Counsel reviewed proposed revisions to several administrative policies previously discussed by the Board. 
Discussion focused on contract signing authority, personnel requisition procedures, personnel actions, advisory 
body appointments, and the role of commissioner liaisons within County departments. Hicks explained that many 
of the revisions were intended to update policy language, clarify responsibilities, and improve administrative 
efficiency. 
 
Commissioners discussed whether formal liaison assignments remained necessary for all departments and noted 
that some departments may benefit from commissioner involvement while others operate effectively without 
designated liaisons. Discussion occurred regarding leadership development, communication between department 
heads and the Board, and potential opportunities for departments to provide regular updates during Administrative 
Workshops. Commissioners generally expressed support for maintaining flexibility and assigning commissioner 
involvement on an as-needed basis rather than through a formal countywide liaison structure. 
 
The Board also discussed contract signing authority and acknowledged previous concerns regarding 
administrative inefficiencies created by requiring Board approval for routine transactions. Commissioners 
expressed support for allowing department heads additional flexibility to conduct business within approved 
budgets while maintaining appropriate oversight and accountability measures. Hicks indicated he would proceed 
with preparing formal orders to implement policy revisions following additional review.

Discussions additionally occurred regarding advisory body appointments and removals. Commissioners 
expressed support for maintaining flexibility while preserving opportunities for public participation and due 
process when appropriate. Hicks agreed to incorporate Board feedback into future policy drafts. 
4. Personnel Policy Revisions: 
e. Article 5; Section 5; Workplace; Service or Working Animals 
b. Article 8; Section 1; Travel 
Legal Counsel reviewed proposed revisions to personnel policies concerning workplace service animals and 
employee travel. Discussion occurred regarding compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act and 
ensuring County policies remain consistent with applicable federal and state laws governing service animals in 
the workplace. Commissioners discussed balancing legal requirements with operational considerations and public 
accessibility concerns. 
 
The Board also reviewed proposed travel policy revisions. Commissioners discussed the value of in-person 
training opportunities, networking, and professional development while also recognizing the cost-saving benefits 
of remote attendance when available. Discussion occurred regarding whether County policy should express a 
preference for remote participation when feasible while still allowing travel when justified by operational needs 
and educational value. Hicks agreed to further refine the proposed language based upon Board feedback. 
 
Commissioners additionally discussed obtaining information regarding County travel and training expenditures 
and emphasized the importance of maintaining reasonable oversight while supporting necessary professional 
development opportunities for County employees. 
5. Other:  
The Board discussed procedures relating to agenda posting requirements and consideration of resolutions. Hicks 
advised that resolutions intended for Board action should be specifically identified on the published agenda and 
available for public inspection prior to consideration. Discussion occurred regarding a recently approved 
resolution and the need to revisit the item through a properly noticed agenda process to ensure compliance with 
public meeting requirements. Commissioners discussed potential options for bringing the matter back for 
consideration at a future Weekly Business Session. 
 
Additional discussion occurred regarding organizational structure within Emergency Management and Airports. 
Commissioners discussed the process for reestablishing separate departmental leadership positions and the 
personnel actions necessary to implement such changes. Hicks advised that personnel requisitions and personnel 
action forms could be utilized to formally separate the functions into distinct departments if directed by the 
Board. 
6. Additional Subject Matters from Commissioners/Announcements 
Commissioners discussed current wildfire conditions throughout Josephine County and noted that fire danger 
levels had been elevated to Moderate. Discussions occurred regarding public awareness efforts, evacuation 
zones, and the importance of residents enrolling in emergency notification systems. Commissioners encourage 
community members to become familiar with evacuation information and preparedness resources as wildfire 
season progresses. 
 
 
Meeting adjourned at 10:53 a.m.
