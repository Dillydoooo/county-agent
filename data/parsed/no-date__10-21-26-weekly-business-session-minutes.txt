Smith opened the floor to public testimony (each person given three (3) minutes to speak, on this matter only):
Mike Pelfrey, Josephine County resident, spoke in opposition to the annexations. Describing himself as a financial
professional of 37 years, he stated that property owners who annex into the Library District are asking to be taxed more

withdraw her property from the Library District, which the Board had declined, questioned who is encouraging property
owners to petition for annexation, and asked why owners may opt into the district while she cannot opt out.

4. REQUESTS/COMMENTS FROM CITIZENS: (Each person will be given three (3) minutes to speak)
Stephen Fitch, Josephine County resident, stated that he is circulating a petition for an independent oversight committee so
that criminal complaints are investigated and referred to the District Attorney for charging, and cited 18 U.S.C. $ 1519,
which makes the destruction, alteration or falsification of records with intent to obstruct a federal matter a federal crime.
He asserted that the Board has an obligation to uphold constitutional rights and the power to remove other officials, and
that failing to act on criminal complaints would expose the Commissioners to personal civil liability not covered by County
insurance. He demanded that all County agencies operate in full compliance with the constitution and the law.
Mike Pelfrey, Josephine County resident, recommended that County department heads attend Board meetings on the dais,
asserting that they direct the Board's actions. He repeated his request that the County adopt a proclamation declaring that it
does not wish to be a sanctuary county, describing it as a simple statement that breaks no laws, and stated that it would be
well received by constituents and that the State would not withhold funds over it. He urged the Commissioners to lead and
to act on their constituents' requests.
Dave Blakely, Grants Pass resident, echoed Mr. Palfrey and asked the Board to stop allocating funds to the Sheriff and the
District Attorney until, in his view, they perform their duties. He stated that the Sheriff had told him personally that he
would not make an arrest within the City of Grants Pass on jurisdictional grounds, which Mr. Wakely disputed as a former
law enforcement officer, and that the District Attorney had declined to pursue what he characterized as three state law
violations by a sitting County Commissioner, citing a conflict of interest. He stated that citizens are frustrated, that the
Board works for the public, and that the Board should use its authority over funding to bring the elected officials into
compliance.
5. BOARD MAy REVTEW PUBLTC COMMENTS/QUESTTONS:
Commissioner Richardson addressed the Library District comments, explaining that state special district law allows
individual property owners to petition to join a district but provides no equivalent right for an individual property to
withdraw; the method available under the law is to gather petitions for a ballot measure to dissolve the district. He responded
to Mr. Fitch that he does not believe the Board has authority to remove other elected officials, who are not County
employees, and asked for documentation supporting that view. He told Mr. Wakely that he was not familiar with the matters
raised and asked for details so he could look into them.
Commissioner Martin concurred with Commissioner Richardson, stating that the Board must follow Oregon law regarding
the Library District, that the Board pursued that fight for three years, and that it now lies with the community. She stated that
the Board recently adopted a resolution asking the State to be accountable and do its job, that it is not a sanctuary
declaration, and that the election in 13 days offers voters a chance to change direction at the State level; she noted that the
federal government has already reduced one County grant to a third of its prior funding and that jeopardizing State grants is
not a risk she is willing to take. She explained that the Board does not control elected officials or their departments, though it
oversees their budgets, facilities and position counts, and that cutting the Sheriff s funding would affect nearly 100
employees rather than one official. She stated that department heads do not direct the Board and asked anyone who believes
otherwise to identifi the department head so she can discuss it.
Chair Smith stated that under its charter the County is first an agent of the State, which binds the Board to state statutes and
rules and has stripped the County of authority it held when it was established in 1856, before statehood. He noted that
roughly 68 percent of the County's land was placed under federal management around the turn of the twentieth century, land
that once fully funded the Sheriff and County government, and that the County now has three of the poorest zip codes in the
state and a budget that is about 60 percent grants and fees. He stated that he has fought the federal land issues for much of
his life, wished the County had its original authority, and thanked the speakers for their comments.
