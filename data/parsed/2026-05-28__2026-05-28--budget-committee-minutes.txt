1) Open Meeting – Gary Richardson, Budget Committee Chair 
Commissioner Richardson opened the meeting at 9:01 a.m.  
2) Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest 
Budget Committee members reviewed the agenda and declared no potential conflicts of interest related to 
matters on the agenda. 
3) Department Presentations: 
a. Public Works and Fleet – Richard Pike and Shannon Doak Exhibit 1 PowerPoint 
Presentation 
Richard Pike and Shannon Doak, Public Works Managers, presented the proposed Public Works and Fleet 
budgets. Staff reviewed the various funds administered through Public Works, including the Public Works 
Fund, County Fleet Fund, Road and Bridge Fund, Solid Waste operations, North Valley Industrial Park, and 
equipment reserve funds. Discussion focused on the department's responsibility for maintaining 
approximately 570 miles of county roads, numerous bridges and structures, culverts, gravel sources, and fleet 
facilities without reliance on General Fund property tax revenues.  
Staff reviewed major revenue sources, including state fuel taxes, federal forest revenues, transportation block 
grant funding, permits, and interfund reimbursements. Discussions occurred regarding operational costs, 
staffing levels, fleet replacement planning, chip seal programs, storm response activities, bridge 
maintenance, and ongoing infrastructure projects. Public Works reported plans to complete approximately 33 
miles of chip seal work during the upcoming construction season and reviewed several bridge replacement 
and road improvement projects currently underway or in design.  
Committee members discussed fleet replacement schedules, reserve funding, vehicle replacement costs, 
county vehicle usage, bridge conditions, deferred infrastructure needs, funding limitations, and the impacts 
of transportation funding uncertainty on future maintenance projects. Staff explained the methodology used 
to establish vehicle replacement rates, maintain fleet sustainability, and prioritize road and bridge 
improvements throughout the county. Exhibit 1 PowerPoint Presentation 
b. Finance- Arthur O’Hare Presentation  
Arthur O’Hare, Budget Officer, presented the proposed Finance Department budget and reviewed staffing 
levels, operational responsibilities, and budget changes from prior fiscal years. O'Hare explained that the 
department currently operates with six budgeted positions and is responsible for countywide financial 
reporting, budget preparation, accounting oversight, and financial management functions.  
Discussion occurred regarding professional service expenditures, software maintenance costs, and audit 
services. O'Hare reported that the County's fiscal year ending June 30, 2024 audit had been completed; 
however, work associated with subsequent audits remained unfinished. Committee members discussed the 
status of outstanding audit work, financial reporting requirements, and anticipated efforts to complete audit 
activities and establish a timeline for future audit completion. Additional discussion occurred regarding 
financial reporting tools, departmental support provided throughout the County, and opportunities to improve 
access to financial information for decision-makers and the public 
c. Human Resources – Michelle Shaw-Exhibit 2 PowerPoint Presentation  
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources Director, presented the proposed Human Resources budget and reviewed 
departmental responsibilities, including recruitment, onboarding, employee benefits administration, labor 
relations, policy compliance, workers' compensation management, and employee support services. Shaw 
reported that the department currently supports approximately 400 County employees and anticipates 
processing approximately 1,850 employment applications during the upcoming fiscal year.  
Discussion occurred regarding staffing levels, workers' compensation claims, labor negotiations, employee 
recruitment efforts, and legal expenditures associated with employment matters. Shaw explained that one of 
the department's goals is reducing employment-related legal costs through proactive employee relations, 
policy compliance, and training initiatives. The Committee also discussed countywide employee counts, 
staffing trends, and plans to expand employee training opportunities, including CPR, first aid, and AED 
instruction. Exhibit 2 PowerPoint Presentation 
d. Information Technology – Ken Clever Exhibit 3 PowerPoint Presentation  
Ken Clever, Information Technology, presented the proposed budgets for Information Technology, 
Geographic Information Systems (GIS), and Cybersecurity. Clever reviewed departmental responsibilities, 
including support of countywide computer systems, software applications, servers, networking infrastructure, 
cybersecurity operations, GIS mapping services, telephone systems, and technology support for County 
departments. Clever reported that the department currently supports approximately 570 users and more than 
600 managed devices throughout the organization.  
The presentation included discussion regarding website improvements, accessibility enhancements, 
evaluation of emerging artificial intelligence tools, cybersecurity initiatives, GIS services, and long-term 
technology planning. Clever reviewed performance metrics related to system uptime, network availability, 
multi-factor authentication coverage, phishing prevention efforts, ticket response times, and cybersecurity 
readiness. Committee members discussed technology training, cybersecurity investments, staffing needs, 
county communications systems, and opportunities to improve technology services provided to County 
departments and the public.  
Clever requested funding to restore a previously authorized Help Desk Support Technician position. Staff 
explained that filling the position would improve customer support, reduce ticket backlogs, increase patch 
compliance, and allow senior technology staff to focus on infrastructure, security, and long-term planning 
responsibilities. Ken Clever Exhibit 3 PowerPoint Presentation 
e. Legal Counsel – Wally Hicks Exhibit 4 PowerPoint Presentation 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, presented the proposed Legal Counsel budget and reviewed services provided 
to the Board of Commissioners, County departments, elected officials, and advisory bodies. Discussion 
occurred regarding legal representation, contract review, labor and employment matters, litigation 
management, public records issues, risk management, and legal support provided throughout County 
operations. Committee members discussed outside legal services, litigation costs, and ongoing efforts to 
provide timely and effective legal guidance while managing expenditures responsibly. Exhibit 4 
PowerPoint Presentation 
f. Forestry – Dave Streeter Exhibit 5 PowerPoint Presentation 
Dave Streeter, Forestry, proposed Forestry budget and reviewed management of County forestlands, timber 
resources, forest health initiatives, and stewardship activities. Discussion included timber harvest planning, 
forest management objectives, reserve funding, revenue generation, wildfire mitigation efforts, and long-
term sustainability of County Forest resources. Committee members discussed market conditions, forest 
health concerns, regulatory requirements, and strategies intended to balance responsible resource 
management with future revenue opportunities. Exhibit 5 PowerPoint Presentation 
4) Budget Officer answers questions/provides requested information 
Budget Officer Arthur O'Hare responded to questions raised throughout the departmental presentations and 
provided clarification regarding budget assumptions, staffing allocations, internal service fund charges, 
reserve accounts, operational expenditures, audit costs, and long-term financial planning considerations. 
Discussion occurred regarding funding methodologies, departmental cost allocations, vehicle replacement 
reserves, technology investments, and projected future obligations. 
5) Public Comment (limit of three minutes each) 
No public testimony was provided. 
6) Confirm next scheduled meeting.  
Chair Richardson confirmed that the Budget Committee would reconvene for continued review of the Fiscal 
Year 2026–2027 Budget and consideration of additional budget matters. 
The meeting adjourned at 12:16 p.m.

Public Works
Purpose of Program
Public Works strives to make living and traveling in Josephine County safe and convenient. The 
revenue received from state motor vehicle fuel tax and a portion of the timber receipts from U.S. 
Forest Service lands are designated by law to be used by the county for road purposes.  The 
Department does not receive any county property taxes to fund its operations.  Designated work 
efforts on major maintenance programs include chip sealing, asphalt patching and overlays, ditch 
cleaning and culvert replacement, storm response, and vegetation management.
Public Works is responsible for managing and maintaining:
•
570 miles of county-maintained roads
•
170 bridges and structures
•
13,000 road and driveway culverts
•

Sharpening Our Chain
Why maintaining our tools and skills is part of the work
Two loggers competed for the same job — cut the most wood in a day. 
One worked nonstop. The other paused every hour. At day's end, the 
one who paused had cut far more.
“I wasn't resting — I was sharpening my saw.”
For IT, sharpening the saw is the work that doesn't close tickets but 
makes all the cutting possible:
■  Backup and recovery procedures
■  Debugging and troubleshooting skills
■  Software, compiler, and tooling currency
■  License management and renewals
■  Training and documentation
A team cutting nonstop never gets to sharpen — and over time, the blade dulls.

Budget Goal 1 - Improve Efficiency, Transparency, and Public Engagement
Enhanced Service Initiative: Improve citizen access, engagement, or service quality (e.g., digital tools, public dashboards, response-time standards, expanded outreach, or 
staff development); 
Forestry is accessible via phone, in person visit or via Josephine County website. Questions and concerns are always answered in a timely manner when 
received. Additionally, all information on timber sales and firewood opportunities are advertised in the local newspaper and posted online at the Josephine 
County website along with information regarding mining.
Efficiency Initiative: Reduce costs or improve operational efficiency while maintaining or enhancing service levels (e.g., process improvements, automation, benchmarking, or 
alternative service delivery models). 
Forestry is working to enhance efficiency through measures to ensure seedling survival during initial planting. Forestry is continually looking at improved 
seedling stock, planting timing, planting quality to ensure success during initial planting which decreases costs of replanting due to seedling mortality. Forestry 
continues to improve our access to technology that assists in improving efficiency from data collection and the use of drone technology. Additionally, continued 
education and stakeholder interviews are used to produce sales that are in high demand by purchasers and produce the maximum return of stumpage for the 
County by offering highly sought after sales.
Budget Goal 2 -Ensure Long-Term Financial Sustainability of Core Services
Develop and maintain a multi-year financial plan to support all mandated and essential County programs, with a focus on balancing infrastructure needs and public safety services.
•
Require annual departmental reviews with clearly defined costed action plans. 
o
Forestry will report annually on progress of cost savings plans in silviculture, and actual to budgeted costs.
•
Prioritize sustainable funding strategies that reduce reliance on one-time or temporary revenue sources; and 
o
Forestry is a self-supporting department that does not rely on any outside funding. Additionally, there is a Forestry Reserve Fund that supports the program during years of poor log 
markets so that the program doesn’t draw resources from the general fund.
•
Align expenditures with long-term fiscal capacity and community priorities. 
o
A majority of Forestry’s expenditures are through contract supplies and services. Products and services are procured through the formal and informal bid process with the best return to 
the County (cost and quality) in all considerations.  
Budget Goal 3 -Strategic Investment in Public Safety and Community Infrastructure
Ensure responsible and sustainable allocation of financial resources to support public safety, infrastructure, and community priorities through thoughtful planning and long-term fiscal strategies.
Maintain balanced and coordinated funding for core public safety services, including the Sheriff, Jail, and District Attorney. 
Revenues generated by Forestry help support Juvenile Justice and Veterans Services, as dictated by the BCC.
Prioritize funding strategies that support recruitment and retention of critical public safety personnel. 
Forestry is self-supporting with no draw of resources from other general fund departments.
Plan for capital improvements and infrastructure upgrades, including communications systems and essential public facilities. 
Forestry has no plans for capital improvements in the near future.
Evaluate and pursue fiscally responsible funding options for major capital projects, including potential bond financing where appropriate. 
Forestry does not anticipate any major capital projects in the near future. If any are determined at a later date, proposals would include payment by revenues earned.
Identify and address infrastructure needs that impact public health and safety through long-term financial planning.
Addressed above in the third bullet point. 
Support community-enhancing programs and events through appropriate and sustainable funding mechanisms.
Forestry has conducted our annual Ross Roe Memorial Youth Tree Plant for 36 years, we will continue this educational experience for youth with funds derived from revenues earned, additionally we 
will look for grant funding to offset expenses.
