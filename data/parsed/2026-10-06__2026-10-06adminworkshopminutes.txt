2. Department Business 
a. Legal Counsel 
1. Resolution No. 2026-037 – In the Matter of Fair Housing in Josephine County 
Helene Lulich, Property Manager, presented Resolution No. 2026-037 renewing the County's fair housing 
resolution. She explained that the action is required as part of the County's Community Development Block 
Grant (CDBG) fair-housing activity and will assist with closing out the current two-year grant. The current 
grant totals $500,000 and has primarily supported home-repair assistance for low-income residents. The next 
$500,000 award includes approximately $400,000 for qualifying home repairs and $100,000 specifically for 
mobile homes located in mobile-home parks outside the City of Grants Pass. The CDBG funds originate with 
HUD and are administered through the State of Oregon. Home Bridging performs the repair work, and the 
County submits eligible costs to the State for reimbursement. 
Ms. Lulich advised that, unless the Board designated another representative, she would serve as the County's 
fair housing representative for the required public notice. Without objection, the Board directed that 
Resolution No. 2026-037 be placed on the October 14, 2026 Weekly Business Session agenda, with Ms. 
Lulich to provide a public overview of the requirement and program. 
b. Juvenile Justice 
1. Cancellation of the Child Nutrition Program Sponsor Agreement – Request for 
authorization for Community Justice Director Scott Hyde to provide a 60-day 
cancellation notice to the Child Nutrition Program, with cancellation effective no 
later than December 2026 
Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, requested authorization to discontinue participation in the Child 
Nutrition Program Sponsor Agreement for the detention and residential programs. He reported that fiscal year 
2025-26 food reimbursement totaled $13,748 against approximately $23,618 in food costs, while staff 
estimated that administration of the program required substantially more in personnel time, training/travel, 
and periodic audit preparation. Mr. Hyde also explained that program rules restrict portion sizes and 
purchasing options, limiting flexibility to provide additional healthy food and to select more cost-effective 
food sources. The department budgets approximately $36,000 annually for food and can absorb the food costs 
while redirecting staff time to other program needs. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to authorize Community Justice Director Scott Hyde to provide a 60-day 
cancellation notice for the Child Nutrition Program Sponsor Agreement, with cancellation effective no later 
than December 2026. Commissioner Martin seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson 
- Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0.

d. Sheriff 
1. Unclaimed Property – Request to credit $57,100.39 in unclaimed currency and 
unclaimed property auction proceeds to the Sheriff’s Office equipment reserve 
account (482935) for the purchase of replacement Tasers (Josephine County Code 
2.80.060) 
Sheriff Dave Daniel requested that $57,100.39 in unclaimed currency and unclaimed-property auction 
proceeds be credited to the Sheriff's Office equipment reserve account for future replacement Tasers. Sheriff 
Daniel explained that the existing Tasers are becoming obsolete and increasingly difficult to support, and that 
a full replacement package is expected to cost approximately $500,000 for about 50 units under a multi-year 
technology contract. The Sheriff's Office has also received a $30,000 grant from the Cow Creek Band of 
Umpqua Tribe of Indians/Seven Feathers toward the replacement effort and is building the reserve so the 
County can plan for the eventual purchase. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the request to credit $57,100.39 in unclaimed currency and 
unclaimed-property auction proceeds to the Sheriff's Office equipment reserve account (482935) for 
replacement Tasers pursuant to Josephine County Code 2.80.060. Commissioner Richardson seconded the 
motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - 
Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
2. BJA FY 2026 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) Program – 
Local Formula – Approval to submit a grant application for $13,114.00 the full local 
allocation, to fund Deputy overtime for community engagement activities; the grant 
requires Board review and an opportunity for public comment 
Sheriff Dave Daniel presented the BJA FY 2026 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) 
Program local allocation in the amount of $13,114. The Sheriff intends to use the funding for overtime 
associated with renewed resident-deputy/community-engagement activities, including evening and weekend 
events. He described a plan to reestablish a resident-deputy style program with deputies serving Cave Junction, 
the northern portion of the County, and the southern portion of the County. The grant requires Board review 
and an opportunity for public comment. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve moving forward with the BJA FY 2026 Edward Byrne Memorial 
Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) Program application for the full local allocation of $13,114. Commissioner 
Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - 
Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. The Board directed that the grant be placed on the 
October 14, 2026 Weekly Business Session agenda to provide the required opportunity for public review and 
comment.

2. Personnel Actions 
a. IT – Technology Director – Promotion and Title Change

3. Telework Request 
a. Legal – Temporary Teleworking Approval 
Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe, Interim Human Resources Director, presented the listed position requisitions. The 
IT System Support Specialist position was approved in the July budget and is now ready to be filled following 
completion of the Technology Director hiring process. The Animal Shelter request adds one 0.7 FTE Shelter 
Technician supported by levy funding. Human Resources also requested an updated job description and 
compensation study for the Public Health Nurse Practitioner position serving the jail clinic; the description 
had not been updated since the County transitioned the jail medical staffing model from contracted services 
to County employment in 2023. In response to a Board question, Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe said she was not aware 
of a completed cost-effectiveness review of that change and that Public Health would be the appropriate 
department to address the program history. 
Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe also presented personnel actions for the IT Technology Director and the Public Health 
Program Specialist - Solid Waste. She explained that the IT action completes the hiring process and transitions 
the position from interim status to the permanent director role, subject to the six-month trial-service period in 
County policy. The Solid Waste candidate is a former County employee with direct solid-waste and code-
enforcement experience; Public Health requested a higher starting rate based on that prior experience. The 
Board also discussed that solid-waste revenues will be directed to the solid-waste program rather than the 
general fund so that program staffing and related costs can be supported by those revenues. 
For the Legal Counsel telework request, Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe explained that the office HVAC system is not 
functioning adequately and the workspace has no operable windows. Because propping doors open creates 
security and confidentiality concerns, Human Resources proposed temporary telework under the existing 
personnel policy until the HVAC system is repaired, tentatively by October 16. The request applies to the 
Legal Counsel office staff, while counsel will continue attending meetings as needed. The Board raised no 
objection, and Human Resources indicated the temporary agreements would be processed through the existing 
telework procedure.
