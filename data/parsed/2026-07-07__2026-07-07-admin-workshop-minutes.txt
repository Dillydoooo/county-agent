2. Department Business 
a. District Attorney 
1. Axon Justice Contract 
Maria Huling, Chief Admin Supervisor- DA presented the District Attorney's request to enter into a long-term Axon Justice 
software agreement to improve management of digital evidence. Ms. Huling explained that the office currently utilizes 
Axon's free platform, which limits evidence storage and requires staff to maintain large volumes of digital evidence on local 
media. The proposed subscription would provide secure cloud-based storage, centralized management of digital evidence, 
streamlined discovery for defense counsel, automated notifications when additional evidence is received, and vendor-
supported training for staff. The software would also eliminate the need to distribute evidence using physical media, 
improving efficiency in large criminal cases.  
Commissioners discussed data ownership, long-term accessibility of evidence, contract termination provisions, and 
protection of County records. Commissioner Richardson emphasized the importance of ensuring the County retains access 
to all evidence should the County ever transition to another vendor. Ms. Huling explained that evidence remains available 
through originating law enforcement agencies and confirmed the agreement had been reviewed by Legal Counsel. She 
further advised that funding for the subscription had been included in the adopted Fiscal Year 2026-2027 budget. 
Commissioners expressed support for improving evidence management while encouraging continued oversight of long-
term data retention practices.  
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the Axon Justice Contract. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon 
roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
b. Parks 
1. Whitehorse Paving - Aegis Asphalt - $117,699.00 
Tamra Martin, Fairgrounds Director, presented a contract with Aegis Asphalt in the amount of $117,699.00 to 
complete paving improvements associated with the Whitehorse Park renovation project. Ms. Martin reported 
that the project remains on schedule, with the renovated campground anticipated to reopen to the public around 
September 1, 2026. She explained that the paving work represents one of the final construction phases and 
includes improvements to campground roads, expanded RV sites, and other infrastructure completed during the 
renovation. Funding is provided through the Parks Capital Fund, utilizing Land and Water Conservation Fund 
grant monies together with County reserves.  
Discussion included the history of Whitehorse Park, preservation of mature trees during construction, ADA 
accessibility improvements, installation of new restroom facilities, and collaboration with local recreational 
organizations to incorporate remote-control vehicle trails into the park's design. Commissioners expressed 
appreciation for staff's efforts to preserve the character of the park while modernizing facilities and improving 
accessibility for future visitors. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to approve the Whitehorse Paving - Aegis Asphalt - $117,699.00. Commissioner 
Martin seconded the motion. Upon roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, Commissioner 
Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0.

3. Board Business 
a. Board Order No. 2026-039; In the Matter of Separating the Information Technology and 
Emergency Management Functions of Josephine County and Establishing Separate Department 
Director Classifications. 
Commissioners reviewed Board Order No. 2026-039 formally separating the Information Technology and Emergency 
Management functions into independent departments. Discussion included previous Board actions that combined the 
departments and the Board's desire to clearly establish separate departmental leadership through formal Board action. 
Commissioners agreed that separating the departments would improve organizational clarity and provide a more effective 
administrative structure. Commissioner Martin moved to approve Board Order No. 2026-039; In the Matter of Separating 
the Information Technology and Emergency Management Functions of Josephine County and Establishing Separate 
Department Director Classifications. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call; Commissioner 
Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
b. Board Order No. 2026-040; In the Matter of Suspending the Acceptance of Applications for 
Mineral Exploration Permits and Mining Lease. 
Commissioners continued discussion regarding the County's Mining Ordinance and the temporary suspension 
of applications for mineral exploration permits and mining leases. Commissioner Martin expressed support for 
comprehensively rewriting the ordinance to eliminate outdated provisions, resolve conflicting amendments 
accumulated over several decades, and reduce potential legal liability. Commissioner Richardson discussed 
ongoing litigation involving the existing ordinance and emphasized the importance of developing a modern 
replacement that protects the County while allowing appropriate consideration of future mining proposals.  
Commissioners agreed that extending the suspension for three months would provide sufficient time to convene 
Legal Counsel, the Forestry Director, and representatives of the Mining Advisory Committee to evaluate 
possible revisions and determine whether a rewritten ordinance or alternative permitting process would better

c. 4H Extension - OSU Service District IGA 
Commissioners reviewed the proposed Intergovernmental Agreement between the Josephine County 4-H/Extension 
Service District and Oregon State University. Discussion centered on the duration of the agreement, legal review, and 
attachment provisions. Commissioners concluded that additional review by Legal Counsel would be beneficial before 
execution of the agreement. The matter was tabled to a future Legal Update session. 
d. 4H Extension District Fund. 
Commissioners discussed the expiration of the District's reserve fund and potential future uses of the remaining 
balance. Discussion included options for reauthorizing the reserve, permissible uses of reserve funds, possible 
furnishings for the District's new location, and the need to review statutory requirements before taking action. 
Commissioners agreed to revisit the matter during a future Legal Update with the Finance Director present. The 
matter was tabled pending additional legal and financial review.  
3. OTHER 
Commissioner Martin provided an update regarding research into replacement of the County's aging radio communications 
system. Discussion included information obtained from neighboring counties regarding recent communications system 
modernization projects, including funding methods, infrastructure improvements, and estimated project costs. 
Commissioners discussed deficiencies identified in Josephine County's current system, including limited coverage in 
portions of western Josephine County, aging infrastructure, and the need to establish technical specifications before 
determining funding options.  
 
Commissioners agreed that development of a comprehensive communications system should begin with identifying 
operational needs for all departments utilizing the County radio network before evaluating financing alternatives. 
Departments identified for participation included the Sheriff's Office, Corrections, Public Works, Parks, Forestry, Facilities, 
and Emergency Management. The Board also discussed potential grant opportunities, cooperative purchasing programs, 
and future financing options. 
 
By consensus, the Board directed staff to schedule a County Radio Communications Workshop for Monday, July 27, 
2026, at 10:00 a.m. in Room 157 to allow affected departments to jointly discuss system requirements, coverage needs, 
and long-term planning for modernization of the County's emergency communications infrastructure.
