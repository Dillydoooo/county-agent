2. Department Business 
a. Parks 
1. Whitehorse Park - Aegis Asphalt Construction Change Order $86,499.95 
Tamra Martin, Recreation Director, presented a construction change order with Aegis Asphalt in the amount of $86,499.95 
for the Whitehorse Park renovation project. Ms. Martin explained that the original paving contract primarily covered the 
individual camping sites, driveways, and parking areas. After accumulated material was removed and the connections 
between the newly improved areas and the existing roadway became visible, staff determined that an overlay of the entire 
roadway was also necessary. Completing the roadway at the same time would create a cleaner finished appearance, improve 
the connections between the sites and road, and extend the useful life of the improvements.  The overall renovation remained 
under budget because Parks staff had completed a significant portion of the work in-house. Funding for the change order 
would come from the Parks Capital Fund and the Land and Water Conservation Fund grant awarded for the project. Ms. 
Martin reported that the completed upper area would include ten additional RV sites and five campsites. She also noted that 
the project design allowed the County to preserve the mature trees and shaded character of the park. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve signing Authority to Tamra Martin, Recreation Director for the 
Whitehorse Park - Aegis Asphalt Construction Change Order $86,499.95.  Seconded by Commissioner Smith. 
Upon Roll Call: Commissioner Richardson – Absent, Commissioner Martin – Yes, Commissioner Smith – Yes. 
Motion carried 2-0.  
 
b. Community Development 
1. BCC Ordinance 2026-002 for Comp Plan Zone Change and Goal Exception 
approved on April 8, 2026 
Tami Smith, Community Development, presented the remaining procedural steps for Ordinance No. 2026-002, 
involving an amendment of the Comprehensive Plan designation from Farm to Residential and a zone change 
from Farm Resource to Rural Residential Five-Acre. 
The underlying land-use request had been approved on April 8, 2026. Community Development requested that 
the first reading of the ordinance be scheduled for August 19, 2026, followed by the second reading on 
September 2, 2026.  Commissioners discussed the amount of time that had passed since approval of the land-
use matter. Staff explained that the appeal period was 21 days and that additional time was needed to confirm 
whether an appeal had been filed, coordinate the Board’s schedule, and provide the required newspaper notice. 
Commissioners requested that future ordinance proceedings be scheduled as promptly as possible following 
expiration of the appeal period.  Without objection, the Board directed staff to schedule the first reading for 
August 19, 2026, and the second reading for September 2, 2026. No formal motion was required.

Ken Clever, IT Director, presented a request to purchase updated drives for the County’s Pure Storage array 
through Xiologix. Mr. Clever explained that the storage array contains County data, files, and application 
servers and is a central component of the County’s daily computer operations.

The existing drives were approximately ten years old and were reaching the end of their supported life. 
Although the storage unit and controllers remained serviceable, the manufacturer was discontinuing its drive 
buyback program at the end of July. Completing the purchase before that deadline would allow the County to

3. Board Business 
a. Association of Oregon Counties – Dues $41,329.93 
The Board considered annual Association of Oregon Counties membership dues in the amount of $41,329.93. 
Commissioners explained that County membership in the Association of Oregon Counties is a prerequisite for 
participation in CIS insurance programs. CIS provides the County with liability coverage, workers’ 
compensation services, employee benefits support, claims management, legal defense, training, loss prevention, 
and risk-management assistance. The Board noted that the dues therefore support more than general association 
activities and are necessary for maintaining essential County insurance coverage. Commissioner Martin moved to 
approve Association of Oregon Counties – Dues $41,329.93. Commissioner Smith seconded the motion. Upon Roll 
Call: Commissioner Richardson – absent; Commissioner Martin – Yes; Commissioner Smith – Yes. Motion carried 2-0. 
b. Communications 
Commissioner Martin provided an update regarding the County’s public safety radio communications system. 
She reported that letters had been sent to three state or congressional representatives seeking information 
regarding possible grants or loans to assist with the cost of repairing and replacing the system. 
The Board had previously approved approximately $85,000 for an interim repair. The necessary equipment had 
been ordered, but the projected delivery date was approximately September 11, 2026, despite efforts to expedite 
the order.  Commissioners explained that the radio system supports the Sheriff’s Office, Emergency 
Management, and other first responders. The current system had been repaired repeatedly and required 
complete modernization. Commissioner Martin stated that she would continue exploring FEMA, State 
Emergency Management, and other grant opportunities before the County considered alternative financing for 
the full replacement project. No formal action was taken.
