2. Human Resources 
a. Replace Existing Classification to Refill Vacant Position 
Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe, Interim Human Resources Director, and Matt Eide, Finance Director, presented a 
proposal to replace the vacant, budgeted Human Resources Analyst classification with a Human Resources 
Risk, Safety, and ADA Coordinator classification. The Human Resources Department currently had two filled 
positions: the Interim Human Resources Director and a Human Resources Generalist. The proposed 
coordinator would fill the department's third currently budgeted position and would not increase the 
department's present full-time-equivalent count or adopted expenditures. 
Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe explained that the proposed coordinator would be responsible for higher-risk and more 
complex program areas, including County risk management, workplace safety, workers' compensation, 
Americans with Disabilities Act coordination, employee education and training, and assistance with questions 
from employees and members of the public. Risk-management responsibilities had previously been handled 
through Legal Counsel, and the County had not maintained a dedicated risk-management position for an 
extended period. Establishing a dedicated resource was intended to improve consistency, reduce organizational 
risk, and relieve workload pressure on the existing Human Resources staff. 
The Board discussed the continuing need for a Human Resources Analyst. Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe stated that the 
analyst classification remains essential because it supports paid-leave administration, employee benefits and 
health insurance, compensation and promotion changes, and other personnel processes necessary for County 
employees to be hired, paid, and supported. Existing staff could provide temporary coverage for those 
functions, but Human Resources anticipated requesting an additional analyst position through a future 
supplemental budget. 
Mr. Eide reviewed recent Human Resources staffing levels. The department had five positions in Fiscal Year 
2024-2025, four positions in Fiscal Year 2025-2026, and three positions authorized in the current budget, with 
only two positions presently filled. The County employed approximately 430 people, representing 400.085 
full-time equivalents as of the end of June. Commissioners emphasized that filling the currently authorized 
vacancy would restore a budgeted position rather than expand the current County workforce. 
Discussion was held regarding the supplemental-budget process. Mr. Eide explained that a supplemental 
budget affecting less than ten percent of an individual fund is subject to a more streamlined process, although 
public notice, Budget Committee involvement, and formal adoption by resolution would still be required. The 
Board may adjust authorized full-time-equivalent positions when the change does not increase overall 
appropriations. The Human Resources fund was approximately $1 million, and the anticipated Human 
Resources request alone was not expected to exceed the ten-percent threshold; however, additional 
departmental requests could affect the scope and timeline of the supplemental budget. 
Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe proposed posting the new position for approximately 12 days, reviewing applications as 
they were received, and attempting to complete recruitment in time for an August 24, 2026 start date. Missing 
that date would likely delay the next practical orientation and start date until mid-September because the

following scheduled orientation period fell during the Labor Day closure. She recommended using the 
County's ordinary recruitment methods rather than an executive search firm. The proposed job description had 
been revised to include the risk, safety, workers' compensation, and ADA responsibilities that had not been 
consistently assigned elsewhere. 
Commissioners also discussed tracking employee qualifications and maintaining current job descriptions. 
Human Resources maintains certifications and training records submitted by departments, while individual 
departments directly monitor operational requirements such as commercial driver's licenses and specialized 
technical credentials. The Board did not direct an immediate audit of every employee file, but supported 
continued review of job descriptions and qualification requirements as vacancies arise. Human Resources was 
also developing supervisory training to improve understanding of department leadership responsibilities and 
employee classifications. 
Without objection, the Board authorized Human Resources to replace the vacant Human Resources Analyst 
classification with the Human Resources Risk, Safety, and ADA Coordinator classification and proceed with 
recruitment. 
The Board then discussed potential items for a future supplemental budget. Chair Smith identified the need for 
additional support for solid-waste enforcement and Community Development code or building-permit 
enforcement. The Board noted that the existing solid-waste enforcement workload could not reasonably be 
handled by one employee and that code-enforcement concerns had been a recurring issue raised by the public. 
Commissioners agreed that staff should identify the specific positions that may have been omitted or reduced 
during the recent budget process, determine the funds affected, prepare current job descriptions, and evaluate 
the operational need and available funding before convening the supplemental-budget process. The Board did 
not intend to open the process as an unrestricted Countywide request for additional staffing because doing so 
could increase the cost, exceed statutory thresholds, and delay adoption. No formal action was taken regarding 
the supplemental budget.
