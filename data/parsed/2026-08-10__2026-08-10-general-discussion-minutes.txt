1.  Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest 
Commissioners Richardson, Martin, and Smith each declared no potential conflicts of interest. 
2.  Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention Five-Year Local Option Tax 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, presented a revised draft of the Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention Five-Year 
Local Option Tax for continued Board discussion. He explained that the discussion from the prior week had 
resulted in preparation of an alternative proposal for an 89-cent-per-$1,000 assessed value levy dedicated to 
the adult jail, without including juvenile detention in the ballot measure. The draft was being reviewed in 
preparation for possible action at the Wednesday Weekly Business Session. 
Commissioner Richardson asked Legal Counsel to clarify the draft reference to an assessed value of 
approximately $239,908. Mr. Hicks explained that the figure was supplied by the Assessor and represented 
the average assessed value of improved residential property, rather than the average or median value of all 
property. The Board agreed that explaining the figure would help residents understand the estimated tax 
impact. 
Commissioner Richardson revisited the proposed commitment to maintain jail capacity for 185 adults in 
custody and asked whether the jail historically operated at that level. Sheriff Dave Daniel stated that the jail 
had historically housed approximately 185 adults in custody and, at times, more, although recent populations 
have generally been lower. He explained that current housing capacity is affected by the classification and 
behavior of adults in custody, mental-health concerns, and the need to separate some individuals. Sheriff 
Daniel emphasized that staffing requirements are driven by the need to safely operate the entire facility and 
its intake, booking, control, food, medical, and housing functions, and do not decrease proportionally when 
the daily population is below 185. 
The Board discussed historic staffing and budget performance. Commissioner Richardson reviewed prior 
levy-year personnel expenditures and noted that personnel budgets had regularly been underspent, largely 
because vacancies and turnover can take many months to refill. Sheriff Daniel explained that the jail was 
currently below desired staffing levels, was using overtime, and had patrol deputies volunteering for overtime 
in the jail. He stated that recruitment and training can take six to seven months before a new corrections 
deputy is fully useful and that the 89-cent figure had been developed to support the staffing level the Sheriff 
believes is necessary to operate the jail safely and reliably. 
The Commissioners considered several possible levy rates and funding structures, including a lower jail-only 
rate, the 89-cent jail-only proposal, retaining the existing 93-cent rate, and a higher rate that would dedicate 
more revenue to juvenile services. Commissioner Richardson questioned whether historical personnel 
savings could support a slightly lower jail rate while preserving service levels and allowing the County to 
address juvenile funding separately. Commissioner Martin expressed concern that repeatedly changing the 
proposed rate and structure could be confusing to voters and stated a preference for keeping the existing 93-
cent rate. Chair Smith similarly emphasized that voter understanding and confidence were central 
considerations and favored a straightforward renewal rather than presenting a substantially different tax 
structure.

Sheriff Daniel continued to support the 89-cent jail funding level, explaining that the figure reflected the 
staffing and operational resources his office believes are necessary to maintain the stated jail service 
commitment. The Board also discussed that unused levy revenue remains within the dedicated fund and can 
carry forward rather than being redirected to unrelated purposes. 
Recess: The Board recessed the General Discussion at 2:15 p.m. and reconvened at 2:45 p.m. to obtain 
additional financial information. Matt Eide and Aurora Frost joined the discussion regarding jail operating 
costs and levy projections. 
After reconvening, Finance and the Sheriff reviewed the jail budget in greater detail. The discussion 
distinguished salaries and overtime from benefits and other operating costs such as inmate medical care, 
food, supplies, uniforms, training, equipment, facilities costs, insurance, and internal service charges. 
Finance explained that historical personnel vacancies had created budget cushions, while the current fiscal-
year budget anticipates fuller staffing. The Board discussed that a lower levy rate could reduce revenue by 
approximately $110,000 for each one-cent reduction and that any shortfall in a levy promising a specific jail 
service level could ultimately require General Fund support. 
Commissioner Martin reiterated her preference for renewing the existing 93-cent levy, noting that the County 
would still need to address any funding gap for juvenile services and that retaining the familiar rate would 
be easier for the public to understand. Commissioner Richardson continued to favor a clearly defined jail-
only approach but acknowledged the need for a long-term plan for juvenile justice and the District Attorney. 
Chair Smith agreed that keeping the current rate would provide greater flexibility while the County develops 
a more comprehensive public-safety funding strategy. 
Mr. Hicks reviewed the State ballot-title guidance regarding use of the term renewal. He explained that a 
measure may be described as renewing a current local option tax when it is for substantially the same purpose 
and the requested rate or amount is equal to or less than the current rate. The Board discussed revised 
explanatory language under which the adult jail would remain the first funding priority, including 
maintaining capacity for 185 adults in custody and the ability to accept persons arrested in Josephine County, 
while any remaining revenue from the measure could partially support Juvenile Department detention 
operations. Continued juvenile service levels would remain subject to the availability of other funding. 
The Board discussed the need to preserve flexibility for future funding solutions for Juvenile Justice and the 
District Attorney and stated that the proposed levy language should not prevent the County from considering 
a future separate levy, service district, or other funding source. Commissioners also discussed beginning a 
public-information effort regarding the role Juvenile Justice plays in public safety, rehabilitation, and 
reducing future adult criminal activity. 
By the conclusion of the discussion, the Board reached consensus to proceed with a 93-cent renewal approach 
using revised language that would fully fund the adult jail first and allow remaining levy revenue, if any, to 
partially fund Juvenile Department detention operations, subject to the availability of other funds. Legal 
Counsel was directed to revise the draft and return it to the Board for consideration at the upcoming Weekly 
Business Session. No formal vote on the ballot measure was taken during this General Discussion.
