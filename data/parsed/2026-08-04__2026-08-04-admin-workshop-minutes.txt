2. Department Business 
a. Treasurer 
1. Yearly Readoption of Investment Policy 
Dustin Calvo, County Treasurer, presented the annual readoption of the Josephine County Investment Policy. Mr. Calvo 
explained that portions of the policy contain outdated statutory citations and language and require a comprehensive revision. 
He recommended temporary readoption so the County would retain the policy safeguards governing investment 
diversification, qualified financial institutions, credit quality, and investment authority while the revised policy is prepared. 
Mr. Calvo stated that, without readoption, the Board would need to issue a written order authorizing the Treasurer to 
continue investing County funds, and the County would otherwise rely primarily upon minimum statutory requirements. 
Major revisions will require review by the Oregon Short Term Fund Board before the updated policy can return to the Board 
for final adoption. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to readopt the Josephine County Investment Policy as presented solely to preserve the 
County's authority under ORS 294.135 to hold investments beyond 18 months and to direct the Treasurer to prepare and 
present a revised policy incorporating the corrections identified in the staff review, including updated statutory citations, 
FINRA-compliant broker qualifications, credit-rating floors, repurchase-agreement term limits, and an updated authorized-
institution list for Oregon Short Term Fund Board review and final adoption at a future meeting. Commissioner Martin 
seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - 
Absent. Motion carried 2-0. 
b. Community Justice 
1. Josephine County Provider Contract with Continuum Behavioral Health and Recovery 
Services (formerly OnTrack) 
Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, presented a provider contract with Continuum Behavioral Health and Recovery 
Services, formerly known as OnTrack, for outpatient substance-use-disorder treatment. The contract is funded through the 
Oregon Health Authority Intergovernmental Agreement previously approved by the Board, which provides approximately 
$8.31 million for mental-health and substance-use-disorder treatment services in Josephine County. 
The contract begins January 1, 2026, and continues through June 30, 2027, unless the underlying agreement is amended, 
extended, or terminated. The maximum amount payable under the contract is $125,000. Mr. Hyde stated that Continuum 
joins ADAPT and Choices Counseling Center as the three providers receiving awards from this portion of the State funding. 
Mr. Hyde confirmed that Community Justice will collect program data and can provide the Board with information regarding 
the services delivered and the use of the awarded funds. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to approve the Josephine County Provider Contract with Continuum Behavioral Health 
and Recovery Services in an amount not to exceed $125,000 and authorize the required Board signatures. Commissioner 
Martin seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner 
Smith - Absent. Motion carried 2-0.

d. Information Technology 
1. Strategic Communications Planning 
Thomas Gray, Public Relations Officer, presented a draft Josephine County Strategic Communications Plan and 
accompanying presentation. Mr. Gray explained that the plan is intended to establish a Countywide framework that 
individual departments can use when developing more detailed communications plans. The document is designed to remain 
adaptable and to be revised as the County receives feedback and evaluates the effectiveness of its communications. 
The proposed communications goal focuses on informing and protecting Josephine County through transparency, access to 
County services, community-building opportunities, and protection of the County government's reputation. The initial 
measurable objectives include increasing annual follower counts by 15 percent for County Facebook and Instagram accounts 
with fewer than 5,000 followers; bringing County websites and mobile applications into compliance with federal 
accessibility standards by April 26, 2027; and establishing baseline website-traffic and social-media-engagement 
measurements. 
Mr. Gray reviewed proposed core messages emphasizing public service, safety, functionality, integrity, accurate and timely 
information, efficient use of alternative funding, and the value of community participation in local government. He also 
reviewed the plan's audience analysis, strategies, communication tactics, measurement methods, and appendices for future 
tactics, department-specific communications goals, the social-media administrative policy, and the County branding guide. 
Mr. Gray requested constructive feedback from the Board and County leadership. He planned to present the draft at the 
managers meeting, incorporate two rounds of review, and return a near-final or final plan to the Board for formal adoption. 
Commissioners expressed appreciation for the work and encouraged all department directors and managers to participate in 
the review process. No formal action was taken.

b. Review of County Fuel Purchasing and P-Card Guidance 
Public Works Director Rob Brandes and Finance Director Matt Eide reviewed County fuel-purchasing practices. They 
explained that the most economical option is the bulk-fuel facility at Public Works, followed by use of the County-issued 
Pacific Pride fuel card. Purchases at other stations are permitted when operational needs, travel, location, or access make 
the preferred options impractical. 
The County budgets for approximately 150,000 gallons of fuel annually. The Sheriff's Office uses nearly half of that amount, 
and Transit accounts for approximately 30 percent. Staff noted that the price difference between the Public Works bulk rate 
and higher-priced retail stations can approach one dollar per gallon, making advance planning and use of the preferred fuel 
sources financially significant. 
County-issued purchasing cards are not the preferred method for routine fuel purchases. Finance reviews purchasing-card 
transactions and follows up when a card is used for fuel instead of the County fuel system or Pacific Pride card. The Board 
did not request a formal report but supported periodic reminders to employees regarding the available fuel options and the 
importance of cost-conscious planning. No formal action was taken.
