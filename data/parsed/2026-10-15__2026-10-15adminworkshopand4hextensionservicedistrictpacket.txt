Resolution No. 2026-039 
WHEREAS, ORS 181A.820 to 181A.829 and ORS 180.805 to 180.810 (together, the 
"State Immigration Laws," including the Sanctuary Promise Act of 2021) restrict the extent to 
which every Oregon public body, including counties and sheriffs, may inquire into immigration 
status, share information with, provide facility access to, or otherwise assist federal immigration 
authorities; no county may exempt itself from these restrictions; and Josephine County has 
adopted no ordinance, policy, or practice of its own limiting such cooperation, so that any limit 
on the County's cooperation arises solely from state law; and 
WHEREAS, since January 2025 federal executive orders and agency actions have 
sought to suspend or condition federal funds to "sanctuary jurisdictions," and on August 5, 2025 
the United States Department of Justice so designated the State of Oregon itself, on the basis 
of characteristics that the State Immigration Laws impose on every Oregon county; and 
WHEREAS, federal grants are awarded on the condition that the recipient certify 
compliance with applicable federal laws, regulations, executive orders, and program conditions, 
and federal agencies have begun to include in those certifications express terms concerning 
cooperation with federal immigration enforcement; and 
WHEREAS, under the federal False Claims Act, 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729-3733, a "person" 
that knowingly makes a false certification material to a federal payment is liable for three times 
the government's damages plus a civil penalty for each claim; any private person, whether or 
not harmed, may sue under seal in the name of the United States and keep 15 to 30 percent of 
the recovery; and the United States Supreme Court has held that a State is not a "person" 
subject to that Act (Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens, 529 
U.S. 765 (2000)) but a county is (Cook County v. United States ex rel. Chandler, 538 U.S. 119 
(2003)), so that the State bears no False Claims Act exposure for the policy it enacted while the 
counties required to carry it out bear all of it; and 
WHEREAS, the State Immigration Laws authorize enforcement against public bodies 
that fail to follow them, so a county that cooperates with federal immigration authorities beyond 
what state law permits risks liability under state law, while a county that cannot certify full 
compliance with federal grant conditions risks loss of federal funds and liability under the federal 
False Claims Act; and 
WHEREAS, the Board values its partnership with the State, does not challenge the 
validity of the State Immigration Laws, and seeks dialogue and practical relief rather than 
confrontation; 
NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED that Josephine County will comply 
with state and federal law and will cooperate with federal, state, and local law enforcement, 
including federal immigration authorities, to the fullest extent the law permits, and that the 
County has adopted, and intends, no policy of its own limiting that cooperation. 
Item 3a
Resolution No. 2026-xxx 
IT IS FURTHER RESOLVED that Josephine County respectfully asks: 
 
(a) The Legislative Assembly to consult with the Association of Oregon Counties, the 
Oregon State Sheriffs' Association, and affected counties, and weigh counties' federal 
funding and False Claims Act exposure, before enacting or expanding laws restricting 
cooperation with federal immigration enforcement; 
 
(b) The Governor or the members of the Legislative Assembly representing Josephine 
County to request, and the Attorney General to issue, formal guidance and model 
disclosure language for federal grant certifications that address immigration-related 
cooperation or 8 U.S.C. §§ 1373 and 1644, and to ensure that for funds passed through 
state agencies such certifications are made by the State as prime recipient rather than 
flowed down to counties; 
 
(c) The Legislative Assembly to enact legislation committing the State to defend and 
indemnify counties and their officers against False Claims Act claims arising from good-
faith compliance with state law, with such indemnification to include attorney fees, 
investigation costs, settlement judgments, penalties, and related litigation expenses; 
 
(d) The Legislative Assembly to make any county whole for federal funds withheld, reduced, 
recouped, suspended, terminated, or required to be repaid because of the county's 
compliance with the State Immigration Laws; 
 
(e)  The Governor to seek written acknowledgment from federal grantors that Oregon 
counties' cooperation is constrained by state law; and 
 
(f)  The Legislative Assembly to consider whether targeted amendments, such as permitting 
a public body to act where necessary to satisfy a lawful condition of federal funding, 
could reduce counties' exposure without abandoning the Legislature's policy objectives. 
 
IT IS FURTHER RESOLVED that the Board shall transmit this Resolution, by letter 
signed by its members, to the Governor, the Attorney General, the presiding officers of the 
Legislative Assembly and the members representing Josephine County, the Association of 
Oregon Counties, the Oregon State Sheriffs' Association, and the boards of commissioners of 
the other Oregon counties, with an invitation to join in these requests. 
 
IT IS FURTHER RESOLVED that nothing in this Resolution is an admission that any 
prior certification was inaccurate, a declaration that the County is or is not a "sanctuary 
jurisdiction" within the meaning of any federal order, list, or grant condition, or a challenge to the 
validity of any state or federal law. This Resolution takes effect upon adoption.
