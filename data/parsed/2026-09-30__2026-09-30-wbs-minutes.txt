3. REQUESTS/COMMENTS FROM CITIZENS: (Each person will be given three (3) minutes to speak) 
Judy Ahrens, Josephine County resident, spoke about public education and student proficiency in reading, mathematics, 
and science. She expressed concern about Oregon and Southern Oregon test scores, encouraged a greater focus on reading, 
writing, arithmetic, and cursive handwriting, and urged residents to attend school board meetings and ask how instructional 
resources are being used. 
Ken Cuyler, Chief Financial Officer of Zydevex, introduced the Southern Oregon company and discussed an Oregon 
Health Authority notice concerning an emerging fungal public-health threat. He stated that the company has patented 
technology intended to help prevent transmission, described ongoing clinical study of its products, and requested a meeting 
with the Board, Public Health, and jail health personnel to discuss the technology and possible County use. 
Herman Beartreger, Josephine County resident, spoke regarding the proposed jail levy. He objected to comments attributed 
to him during a recent radio program, stated that he supports funding both the adult jail and Juvenile Justice, questioned the 
amount of revenue the proposed levy would generate compared with prior levies, and urged the County to consider 
taxpayer affordability and operating efficiencies. 
John Flad, a former Josephine County resident, spoke on behalf of residents of a nearby manufactured-home community. 
He described substantial increases in space rent, difficulty selling homes, and declining resale values that he said have 
caused financial hardship for senior residents. He requested the County's assistance in identifying ways to protect 
vulnerable residents and address what he characterized as wealth loss from the community. 
Laurel Keith, a resident of Roegli Estates, described increases in her space rent and concerns about affordability, aging 
infrastructure, property maintenance, and the ability of residents to retain value in their manufactured homes. She asked for 
County awareness, resources, and collaboration regarding conditions affecting residents of manufactured-home 
communities. 
Mark Jones, Josephine County resident, commented on the proposed jail levy and referenced prior levy projections. He 
stated that the current levy proposal would direct most revenue to the adult jail and asked whether the County was pursuing 
other revenue sources to continue fully funding Juvenile Justice. 
4. BOARD MAY REVIEW PUBLIC COMMENTS/QUESTIONS: 
Commissioner Richardson responded to the education comments by discussing Grants Pass School District 7 reading goals 
and the distinction between state proficiency measures and workforce-readiness benchmarks. He said he was open to 
meeting with Zydevex and directed Mr. Cuyler to contact the Commissioners' Office. Regarding manufactured-home 
concerns, he said the Board has limited direct authority but could explore whether there are ways to assist. He also addressed 
the jail levy, stating that the proposed $0.93 rate remains unchanged from prior levy rates but that inflation has reduced its 
purchasing power. He said current projections allow continued funding of adult jail and Juvenile Justice services in the near 
term, while future budgets will require adjustments. 
Commissioner Martin thanked the speakers and supported arranging a meeting with Zydevex. She questioned the stated 
estimate that the proposed levy would raise $20 million more than prior levies and reviewed recent levy revenue figures,

6. OTHER MATTERS: (ORS 192.640(1) ... Commissioners may introduce other matters for consideration by the 
entire board) 
Commissioner Richardson reported that, at the September 29 Administrative Workshop, the Board reduced six budgeted 
Sheriff's Office FTE positions that could not be supported under the current budget and collective bargaining 
agreements: two Patrol Deputy positions, two Corrections Deputy positions, and two Major Crimes Detective positions. 
He also highlighted the Josephine County Clerk's property-recording notification service, available through the Clerk's 
webpage, which allows residents to receive email alerts when documents are recorded under a monitored name or 
property and may help identify potential fraud.
