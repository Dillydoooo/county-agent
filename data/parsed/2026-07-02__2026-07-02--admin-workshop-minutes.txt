Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe Interim Human Resources Director presented several personnel requisitions related to 
the Forestry Department and reviewed the results of a comprehensive compensation study completed for 
Forestry classifications. Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe explained that Forestry job descriptions had not undergone a 
formal compensation review since 2017 and that the department has experienced ongoing recruitment and 
retention challenges. She reported that Forestry positions have remained vacant for extended periods despite 
repeated recruitment efforts, with applicant pools consisting of relatively few qualified candidates. The 
compensation study compared Josephine County positions to similar forestry classifications in comparable 
Oregon jurisdictions and identified compensation disparities, particularly at the entry-level positions.  
Dave Streeter Forestry Director discussed the unique challenges associated with recruiting and retaining 
qualified forestry professionals. He explained that Josephine County competes directly with the Bureau of Land 
Management, the United States Forest Service, and private timber companies that often offer substantially 
higher wages. Mr. Streeter stated that because experienced forestry professionals are difficult to recruit, the 
department frequently hires employees with strong work ethics and trains them internally, only to lose those 
employees once they become qualified for higher-paying positions elsewhere. He further emphasized that 
Forestry operations generate sufficient revenue through the County's timber sale program to fully fund 
departmental personnel costs without relying upon General Fund resources and expressed his desire to retain the 
experienced employees currently serving the department.  
Commissioner Richardson reviewed the compensation analysis and discussed the overall structure of the 
County's salary schedules. He observed that the County's entry-level wages appeared less competitive than 
comparable employers while upper-end wages remained relatively consistent with surrounding jurisdictions. 
Commissioner Richardson questioned whether future collective bargaining discussions should include 
evaluation of the progression between salary steps to improve recruitment and retention while maintaining long-
term fiscal responsibility. Discussion followed regarding hiring employees above Step 1, possible future 
bargaining implications, and the differences between public and private sector compensation practices.  
Matt Eide, Finance Director, discussed employee retention trends and explained that turnover commonly occurs 
during the first several years of employment before employees become invested in long-term retirement benefits

4. OTHER 
Commissioner Martin proposed implementing semiannual department presentations during County Administration 
Workshops to provide the Board with regular updates regarding departmental accomplishments, ongoing projects, staffing, 
operational challenges, strategic goals, and emerging issues. Commissioners discussed the value of maintaining regular 
communication with departments throughout the year rather than limiting discussions primarily to the annual budget 
process. It was suggested that each department receive two opportunities annually to present updates using a standardized 
reporting format that would allow the Board to better understand departmental priorities and long-term planning efforts. 
Commissioners expressed support for developing a reporting schedule and standardized outline for future presentations. 
 
Commissioner Richardson discussed the importance of proactive strategic planning for County operations and infrastructure 
rather than reacting after significant problems arise. Discussion included long-term planning for technology, 
communications, facilities, equipment replacement, and other major capital investments. Commissioner Martin also 
discussed establishing an internal planning group to evaluate future facility improvements, maintenance priorities, and

County Radio Communications System 
Commissioners reviewed a recently completed Day Wireless assessment regarding the County's public safety radio 
communications system. The report identified numerous deficiencies throughout the communications network, including 
failed microwave paths, degraded signal strength, aging infrastructure, equipment failures, battery and repeater deficiencies, 
dispatch console issues, and multiple system components that have exceeded their expected service life. Commissioners 
expressed concern that the report concluded the existing communications system requires comprehensive modernization 
rather than incremental repairs. 
 
Discussion focused on identifying potential funding opportunities for replacement of the radio system, including possible 
Full Faith and Credit financing, grant opportunities through FEMA and Oregon Emergency Management, and researching 
similar replacement projects completed by neighboring counties. Commissioners agreed that replacement of the 
communications system represents a significant public safety priority affecting the Sheriff's Office, Emergency 
Management, Public Works, and other County operations. Staff was directed to continue gathering cost estimates, funding 
information, and grant opportunities. Commissioners further agreed to include a standing agenda item during future County 
Administration Workshops to receive ongoing updates regarding the communications system until a long-term replacement 
strategy is established.
