1. Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest  
All commissioners present have reviewed the agenda, and it was determined that no conflicts are present. 
 
2. Oregon Groundwater Rulemaking Process – Public Comment  
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel provided a follow-up regarding the Oregon Water Resources Department's 
proposed groundwater rulemaking and the pending public comment deadline. Hicks noted that additional 
information from the previous presenter had not yet been received but advised the Board that comments could 
still be submitted before the close of the comment period. 
 
Commissioners reported that a draft comment letter had been prepared utilizing the information previously 
provided to the Board. The letter would be submitted by certified mail and electronically before the deadline. 
Staff also reported that information regarding the public comment opportunity had been posted on the County 
website to encourage public participation. 
 
Commissioners discussed concerns regarding proposed groundwater regulations, including potential well 
metering requirements, impacts to private well owners, and the importance of protecting local water rights. 
The Board agreed that submitting comments opposing unnecessary regulation was appropriate and directed 
that the finalized letter be transmitted prior to the deadline. 
 
3. Jail & Juvenile Levy 
Legal Counsel reviewed the procedural requirements necessary to establish a county service district that 
could potentially provide a long-term funding mechanism for juvenile services. Discussion focused on 
statutory timelines, coordination required with the Cities of Grants Pass and Cave Junction, required public 
hearings, and the overall timeframe needed should the Board elect to pursue district formation.  
Commissioners discussed the timing of initiating the process. Commissioner Richardson expressed support 
for beginning the preliminary legal work immediately in order to preserve future options while recognizing 
that creation of a district would not obligate the County to seek funding. Chair Smith expressed concern that 
initiating formal district formation before the upcoming Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention Levy election 
could create unnecessary public confusion and potentially affect voter support for the levy. 
Following discussion, the Board reached consensus to authorize Legal Counsel to begin preparatory work 
associated with district formation, including discussions with affected jurisdictions and preparation of 
necessary documentation, while postponing formal action establishing the district until a later date.  No 
formal action was taken. 
4. Potential Formation of County Service District for Law Enforcement – Juvenile Delinquency 
Enforcement 
Commissioners continued discussions regarding renewal of the existing Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention 
Levy. Community Justice Director Scott Hyde reviewed current funding allocations and explained that 
operating costs have increased substantially during the past ten years while levy revenues have remained 
unchanged. Hyde stated that under the proposed renewal approximately $0.89 of the existing $0.93 levy

would be required to operate the Adult Jail, leaving approximately $0.04 available for juvenile detention 
services.  
Director Hyde explained that Juvenile Justice has historically utilized multiple funding sources, including 
grant revenue, forestry funding, residential treatment revenue, and levy proceeds. Staff recently separated 
individual program costs to more accurately identify the actual operating expense associated with juvenile 
detention. Discussions occurred regarding projected operating costs, reserve funding, forestry reserve 
allocations, residential treatment revenue, detention facility operations, and long-term sustainability. 
Commissioners discussed the County's responsibility to maintain juvenile detention services and 
emphasized the importance of preserving programs that provide early intervention, accountability, 
rehabilitation, and community safety. Hyde explained that the residential treatment program is expected to 
remain financially self-supporting while juvenile detention will require additional long-term funding beyond 
the current levy.  
Commissioners also discussed future funding options, including potential levy adjustments, additional 
revenue opportunities, and development of a dedicated juvenile services funding source. Chair Smith 
expressed support for maintaining the current levy rate rather than requesting an increase, noting concerns 
that increasing taxes could jeopardize voter approval. Commissioner Richardson emphasized the importance 
of identifying stable long-term funding in order to improve employee retention and maintain critical County 
services.  
Director Hyde encouraged Commissioners and members of the public to tour the Juvenile Justice facility 
and explained that Josephine County's program has become recognized statewide for its rehabilitative 
approach. Discussion highlighted the importance of educating the public regarding the facility's role in 
reducing juvenile crime, protecting the community, and providing services that reduce future criminal 
behavior. No formal action was taken. 
5. Snell & Wilmer Invoice - Request to Increase Spending 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, presented a request to increase the spending authorization associated with Snell 
& Wilmer's representation of multiple Oregon counties participating in litigation involving surplus proceeds 
from tax foreclosed property sales. Hicks explained that Josephine County is one of several participating 
counties sharing litigation expenses through a joint defense agreement.  
 
The Board expressed support for increasing the authorized expenditure limit to $20,000 to allow Finance to 
continue processing litigation-related invoices as necessary. No objection was raised, and staff was directed 
to proceed.
