3. Open Session Cont’d 
a. Consideration and decision regarding the dismissal or discipline of, or to hear complaints or charges brought 
against a public officer, employee, staff member or individual agent: Human Resources Director 
 
Vice Chair Martin reconvened Open Session pursuant to Michelle Shaw's request that the due process hearing 
be conducted publicly. Commissioner Martin explained that the purpose of the hearing was to provide notice 
of concerns identified by the Board regarding Ms. Shaw's performance as Human Resources Director and to 
provide her with a meaningful opportunity to respond before the Board determined how to proceed. 
Commissioner Martin emphasized that no conclusions regarding Ms. Shaw's continued employment had been 
reached and that the purpose of the hearing was to ensure procedural due process.  
 
Commissioner Martin outlined the Board's concerns regarding the administration of workplace investigations, 
including the absence of a consistently structured investigative process, extended timelines for completing 
investigations involving employees placed on paid administrative leave, incomplete investigative work 
product, and delays in providing information to outside employment counsel. Commissioners expressed 
concern that prolonged investigations affected departmental operations, delayed personnel decisions, and 
created uncertainty for employees and management. 
 
Ms. Shaw described her standard investigative process, explaining that investigations begin with interviews of 
complainants, supervisors, and witnesses and include organization of investigative materials within the Human 
Resources shared drive. She explained that one investigation involved multiple conflicts of interest, which

prompted her to retain outside employment counsel to assist with the investigation. Ms. Shaw stated that 
staffing limitations, changes in Board membership, ongoing information received throughout the investigation, 
and reliance upon outside counsel contributed to the overall timeline. 
 
Commissioners acknowledged the challenges presented by the circumstances but expressed concern that 
investigations remained unresolved for several months despite repeated requests that they be completed 
promptly. Commissioner Richardson noted that outside reviewers had advised that one investigation was 
incomplete and required additional work before it could be relied upon. Commissioner Martin further 
discussed the operational impact resulting from prolonged vacancies, interim management assignments, and 
reduced employee morale during the pendency of the investigations. Ms. Shaw responded that County morale 
had been affected by organizational changes occurring over several years and did not believe the investigations 
alone were responsible for those concerns. 
 
The Board next discussed concerns regarding the accuracy and completeness of personnel information 
presented to Commissioners. Topics included securing employee offices following placement on 
administrative leave, communication regarding a long-standing workers' compensation claim, investigative 
information not presented to the Board, personnel requisitions, compensation recommendations, and 
application of County compensation practices. 
 
Commissioner Richardson explained that offices associated with employees placed on administrative leave 
had not initially been secured, raising concerns regarding preservation of evidence and integrity of 
investigations. Ms. Shaw responded that she had not previously been trained regarding securing employee 
offices as part of an investigation and believed existing County policies addressed disabling building and 
computer access but did not specifically address office inventories or securing physical workspaces. 
Commissioners stated they expected the Human Resources Director to recognize those investigative 
responsibilities as part of the position. 
 
Commissioners also discussed information presented during a Budget Committee meeting concerning a 
historical workers' compensation claim. Commissioner Martin stated that questions raised during the public 
meeting required additional research by Commissioners before the matter could be fully explained. Ms. Shaw 
responded that she had contacted SAIF and CIS seeking information regarding the historical claim and believed 
she had taken reasonable steps to obtain available records. Commissioners acknowledged those efforts but 
expressed concern regarding the timeliness of follow-up and communication with the Board.  
 
Discussion also included investigative materials previously submitted by department management, personnel 
requisitions requiring correction before Board approval, and several compensation-related recommendations 
that required additional review before presentation to the Board. Commissioners emphasized their expectation 
that Human Resources recommendations presented for Board consideration be thoroughly researched and 
administratively complete. Ms. Shaw acknowledged that occasional errors had occurred but stated that Human 
Resources strives to provide accurate information and correct any mistakes when identified.   
 
Commissioners next discussed several assignments they believed had not been completed within expected 
timeframes. Discussion included a workers' compensation settlement agreement executed by an employee but

not presented to the Board before Ms. Shaw was placed on administrative leave, requiring additional legal 
review before completion. Ms. Shaw explained that she had followed guidance received from outside 
employment counsel regarding execution of the agreement and believed the timing of Board approval was 
consistent with those instructions. 
 
The Board also discussed administration of collective bargaining negotiations. Commissioner Martin stated 
that negotiations should generally begin earlier in the calendar year and expressed concern regarding delays in 
scheduling bargaining sessions and communicating with bargaining units. Ms. Shaw responded that she had 
communicated with bargaining representatives, coordinated with outside employment counsel, developed 
proposed contract language, and initiated negotiations with multiple bargaining units. She further explained 
that changes in outside legal counsel and ongoing negotiations contributed to scheduling delays.  
 
Commissioners expressed concern that Human Resources relied extensively upon outside attorneys to organize 
negotiations rather than independently advancing the bargaining process and believed greater preparation and 
internal leadership were expected from the Human Resources Director. Commissioner Richardson emphasized 
the importance of establishing strategic bargaining objectives before formal negotiations begin and ensuring 
the Board receives timely recommendations throughout the negotiation process. 
 
The Board discussed concerns regarding the handling and distribution of confidential personnel and medical 
information. Commissioners referenced an email distributed to multiple recipients that included sensitive 
medical information relating to a County employee and questioned whether the information had been shared 
more broadly than necessary. Ms. Shaw stated that she did not recall the specific communication but agreed 
that confidential medical information should generally be disclosed only when necessary for employment 
administration. Commissioners emphasized the importance of protecting confidential personnel information 
and maintaining appropriate limitations on distribution of sensitive records. Additional discussion occurred 
regarding disclosure of identifying information during previous public meetings and the Board's expectation 
that personnel matters remain confidential whenever possible. 
 
Following completion of the discussion, Commissioners asked Ms. Shaw whether she wished to provide any 
additional information or clarification regarding the concerns presented. Ms. Shaw declined to add further 
comments. 
 
Commissioner Martin thanked Ms. Shaw for participating in the due process hearing and advised that the 
Board would consider the information presented before determining the appropriate course of action. 
Commissioners explained that, consistent with County personnel policy, no employment decision would be 
made during the hearing and that any future action would occur at a subsequent meeting following the required 
waiting period. No formal action was taken. 
 
The meeting adjourned at 4:14 p.m.
