1) Open Meeting – Gary Richardson, Budget Committee Chair 
Commissioner Richardson opened the meeting at 9:01 a.m.  
2) Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest 
Prior to departmental presentations, Budget Officer Arthur O'Hare provided follow-up information requested 
by the Budget Committee during previous meetings. O'Hare reviewed information regarding reserve funds, 
including procedures for establishing and dissolving reserve funds, and provided an index identifying the 
relationship between County funds and programs. O'Hare also provided clarification regarding budgeted full-
time equivalent positions and distributed information received from Legal Counsel regarding public meeting 
law requirements, advisory committees, litigation reporting, and legal research subscription costs. 
3) Department Presentations: 
a. Sheriff-Dave Daniel- Exhibit 1 PowerPoint Presentation 
Dave Daniel, Sheriff presented the proposed Fiscal Year 2026-2027 Sheriff's Office budget, including Adult 
Jail, Animal Shelter and Animal Control, Patrol, Dispatch, Evidence, Court Security, Search and Rescue, 
Marine Patrol, Civil Division, Administration, and JMET operations. Daniel reviewed current staffing levels, 
recruitment and retention challenges, jail operations, animal control services, technology improvements, 
grant funding sources, and long-term financial planning. 
Discussion focused on staffing vacancies, law enforcement service district funding, general fund support, jail 
operations, levy funding, recruitment and retention efforts, compensation concerns, technology 
improvements, body-worn cameras, school resource deputies, and future public safety needs. Committee 
members asked questions regarding staffing levels, levy funding, funding allocations, service district 
revenues, and operational priorities. Daniel discussed the Sheriff's Office request for additional patrol 
staffing and the importance of maintaining competitive compensation to improve retention and reduce 
employee turnover. Exhibit 1 PowerPoint Presentation 
 
b. District Attorney- Joshua Eastman-Exhibit 2 PowerPoint Presentation  
Joshua Eastman, District Attorney presented the proposed Fiscal Year 2026-2027 District Attorney budget. 
Eastman reviewed departmental responsibilities, staffing levels, prosecution services, victim assistance 
programs, specialty court participation, and legal services provided throughout Josephine County. 
Discussion occurred regarding case volumes, staffing challenges, recruitment and retention of attorneys, 
compensation concerns, specialty court programs, grant funding opportunities, and long-term sustainability 
of prosecutorial services. Committee members discussed operational needs and future staffing 
considerations. Exhibit 2 PowerPoint Presentation 
 
c. Community Justice– Scott Hyde-Exhibit 3 PowerPoint Presentation  
Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, presented the proposed Fiscal Year 2026-2027 Community Justice 
budget. Hyde reviewed Adult Parole and Probation, Juvenile Justice, Prevention and Treatment Services, 
Behavioral Health Deflection, Family Treatment Court, and related community supervision programs. 
Discussion included grant funding, state revenue sources, juvenile detention operations, parole and probation 
services, treatment programs, youth prevention services, staffing needs, community partnerships, and future 
program expansion opportunities. Committee members discussed program effectiveness, service delivery 
requirements, and long-term funding considerations. 
 
 
4) Budget Officer answers questions/provides requested information 
Arthur O'Hare responded to Budget Committee questions and provided additional clarification regarding 
departmental funding structures, staffing allocations, levy funding, reserve balances, and budget presentation 
information discussed during departmental presentations. O'Hare answered questions regarding fund 
relationships, personnel calculations, and funding mechanisms utilized throughout County operations. 
5) Public Comment (limit of three minutes each) 
Bill Ertl, Josephine County resident, spoke regarding the Law Enforcement Service District Advisory 
Committee and expressed support for the Sheriff's Office budget request. 
Ron Lamb, Josephine County resident, spoke regarding Measure 17-115 and public safety funding. 
6) Confirm next scheduled meeting.  
The next Budget Committee Meeting was confirmed for Thursday, June 4, 2026, at 9:00 a.m. 
The meeting adjourned at 12:24 p.m.

ORS 419C.001
Purposes of Juvenile Justice System in Delinquency Cases 
(1) The Legislative Assembly declares that in delinquency cases, the purposes of the Oregon juvenile 
justice system from apprehension forward are to protect the public and reduce juvenile delinquency and to 
provide fair and impartial procedures for the initiation, adjudication and disposition of allegations of 
delinquent conduct. The system is founded on the principles of personal responsibility, accountability and 
reformation within the context of public safety and restitution to the victims and to the community. The 
system shall provide a continuum of services that emphasize prevention of further criminal activity by the 
use of early and certain sanctions, reformation and rehabilitation programs and swift and decisive 
intervention in delinquent behavior. The system shall be open and accountable to the people of Oregon 
and their elected representatives.

Juvenile Residential Treatment Program
Turning Point is a residential program operating under the Josephine County Juvenile Justice Department that 
serves youth ages 13-17 in need of residential treatment services. The program provides individualized service 
plans to youth requiring stabilization, assessment and treatment. The program provides an environment for youth 
that cannot presently live safely in the community as a Behavior Rehabilitation Services treatment program aimed 
at developing skills in multiple areas including personal responsibility, accountability, interpersonal effectiveness 
and communication.
Youth receive structured, 24-hour supervision with anticipated length of stay of up to 270 days. The aftercare goal 
is for the youth to transition to a permanent residence.
