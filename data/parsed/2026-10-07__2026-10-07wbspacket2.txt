Item 3a
Article F: Information & Communication 
F-01: County  Communication
A. General Policy
The County will utilize reasonable methods to make relevant and current factual information and
notices available to citizens and employees, such as through the website, bulletin boards, listservs,
signage, and social media.
B. Responsible Authority
1. The Department Head, or their designees, shall be the Responsible Authority for any physical
premises designated to their department, for any webpage on the County website dedicated to
their department, any listservs created for their department, and/or any authorized social media
account established for their department subject to the approval of the Board of County
Commissioners.
2. The Board of County Commissioners, or their designees, shall be the Responsible Authority for
all other physical premises, County webpages, County listservs, and/or County social media
accounts.
3. Union representatives shall be the Responsible Authority for any bulletin board allowed for
union use.
C. General Principles
1. Notices on exterior bulletin boards or webpages are to be dated and remain on the bulletin board
or webpage for a period of not more than two weeks unless the time period is extended by the
Responsible Authority or otherwise required by law. Outdated webpage content shall be
archived.
2. The Responsible Authority should review the materials on their bulletin boards and other signage
regularly and remove any materials that are no longer current and/or relevant. Outdated or
irrelevant social media and webpage content shall be archived.
3. Human Resources shall ensure that mandatory employment postings are posted and accessible.
It is the responsibility of Department Heads to ensure that each worksite maintains bulletins with
current employment postings as provided by Human Resources.
4. All items must be considered to be in good taste and free of any material that may be considered
slanderous or derogatory towards the county. County communication resources may not be
used to take a position on any political candidate, ballot measure, or other matter up for election.
5. Use of County communication resources is for County business only and shall not be used for
personal gain or purpose by any employee.
D. Bulletin Boards
1. Exterior Bulletin Boards are maintained for the purpose of posting legal notices and County
communication with the public. No one may post information on such bulletin boards without
ﬁrst obtaining approval from the Responsible Authority.
2. Internal Bulletin Boards are maintained for the purposes of communicating legally required
information and/or sharing County wide and Department speciﬁc information. They may also be
used to display departmental information, news, and items of interest, value, or help to employees

H. Social Media
1.
Purpose: County departments may use social media Internet sites and technology to
provide information and to interact with the public. “Social media” is broadly defined as
Internet-based communications platforms that allow people to communicate, create
communities, and/or share information and content. The County encourages the use of
social media, where appropriate, to further the goals of the County and the missions of
its departments; however, the County has an overriding interest in deciding what is
“spoken” on behalf of the County. This policy establishes guidelines for the use of social
media by County departments.
2.
General Policy: Josephine County’s website (www.josephinecounty.gov) will serve as
the County’s online home of information. County offices and departments may also
maintain other websites as appropriate. Social media will be used to provide further
promotion of Josephine County objectives on platforms that are widely used by

community members, making information and engagement more accessible and 
reaching a broader audience. County staff should provide links back to County websites 
on social media as practical and appropriate.  
3.
Authorization and Approval:  Departments will work with the Public Information Officer
to establish and maintain relevant social media accounts. The Public Information Officer
will maintain top-level access to these accounts (usernames, passwords, admin-level
privileges, etc.). Departments may seek ongoing authorization to manage content on
their social media accounts, with either full or partial independence, which the Public
Information Officer may provide via written agreement. Departmental management of
social media accounts is still subject to compliance with the Josephine County Social
Media Policy.
Elected officials may choose to establish and maintain social media accounts for their
respective offices. For such accounts, elected officials may choose to follow the
Josephine County Social Media Policy for records retention and comments
management, or they may choose to establish their own policies, in accordance with
applicable law.
4.
Content: County social media accounts will be used to promote messages and content
that furthers Josephine County’s objectives. Content will maintain a tone and level of
professionalism appropriate to the messaging (e.g., posts about emergencies should be
professional and serious, posts about community events may be lighthearted and fun,
etc.). Any content to be posted that has been generated by a non-governmental entity
(including citizens, businesses or a media outlet) shall be approved by the Public
Information Officer or the appropriate Department or Office leader prior to posting.
5.
Site Maintenance: Whenever possible and practical, content posted to County social
media sites should contain links directing users back to the County’s official website for
in-depth information, forms, documents, or online services. Designated department
staff approved by the Public Information Officer shall be responsible for the content and
upkeep of social media sites their department may create. All postings are subject to
approval by the Web & Public Information Coordinator or designee, unless otherwise
specified. Only County employees will have administrative/ownership access to County
social media accounts.
6.
Compliance and Public Records Law: Use of County social media sites shall comply with
all applicable policies, rules, and regulations of Josephine County. County social media
sites are subject to state public records laws. Any content maintained in a social media
format that is related to County business, including a list of subscribers and posted
communication, is a public record. Content related to County business shall be
maintained in an accessible format so that it can be produced in response to a public
records request. Whenever possible, such sites shall clearly indicate that any content
posted or submitted for posting is subject to public disclosure.
7.
Records Retention: State records retention laws (ORS Chapter 166) apply to social
media formats and content. The Public Information Officer shall set records policies for
County social media accounts and maintain any necessary contracts or work to retain
social media records, in compliance with state law.

8.
Restrictions: County social media sites shall include a notice to site users and visitors
that the following content will not be allowed on the site:
a.
Comments that are clearly or repeatedly not related to the particular social media
thread;
b. Profane and vulgar language or content;
c.
Content that personally attacks a person’s or group’s age, race, sex, national
origin, family status, physical or mental ability, or religion;
d. Sexually explicit content or links to sexually explicit content;
e.
Spam that can include solicitation, promoting particular services or products, or
posting links to other websites;
f.
Conduct or encouragement of illegal activity;
g.
Information that compromises the safety or security of the public or public systems;
h. Content that violates copyright, trademark, or intellectual property law;
i.

j.
Content that reveals another person’s private information.
Any content removed based on these restrictions shall be retained, including the time 
and date of the posting and the identity of the poster (if available). Josephine County 
reserves the right to restrict and remove any content posted on County social media 
sites that is determined to be in violation of this policy or other applicable law, rule, or 
regulation. 
9.
The IT Director shall be responsible for making determinations on blocking social media
accounts. Commenters and persons messaging Josephine County social media accounts
shall not be blocked from Josephine County social media accounts except in cases when:
a. The commenter displays a continued pattern of disregard for Josephine County’s
social media comment policy and/or terms of use.
b. The commenter’s behavior is so egregious that the IT Director concludes the
commenter shows a severe risk of continuing behavior that violates Josephine
County social media comment policy and/or terms of use.
10.
Confidentiality and Personal Use: Confidentiality and Personal Use. County employees
and elected officials are prohibited from disclosing any information on any social media
sites that may be confidential or may otherwise compromise the County. Only County
social media sites may be used by County employees and elected officials to conduct
County business. County employees and elected officials are discouraged from using
personal equipment or personal accounts to post information to County social media
sites. County employees and elected officials shall not use personal equipment or
personal accounts to post information regarding County business on other social media
sites.
Personal use of County equipment by County employees, elected officials, or others to
access social media sites is prohibited.

Item 3b
F-06: Public Records Requests
A. Purpose
To set forth the County procedures when members of the public request records
pursuant to Oregon’s Public Records Laws.
B. General Policy
Public records of the County shall be maintained and managed in a manner that
protects the integrity of the records and does not hinder their lawful disclosure when
requested. Public records shall be maintained from the time of creation to the time of
ﬁnal disposition in accordance with the public records archiving requirements of state
law.
C. Definitions
"Custodian" refers to a public body mandated, directly or indirectly, to create,
maintain, care for or control public records. The Board of County Commissioners is the
custodian of all public records for Josephine County, except those where another
elected official is the custodian.
“Public Record” has the meaning established in ORS 192.311.
D. Roles and Responsibilities
The Board of County Commissioners has designated the Josephine County Information
Technology Department's Web and Public Information Coordinator as the Records
Management Officer (RMO) to coordinate the County’s Public Records Management
Program in accordance with ORS 192.105 (2)(a).
1. All decisions by the RMO regarding public records for which the Board of County
Commissioners is the custodian are considered decisions of the Board of County
Commissioners. Decisions of the Board of County Commissioners are appealable
to the Josephine County District Attorney in accordance with ORS 192.324(6),
192.407 - 192.415.
2. Decisions regarding public records for which an Elected Official is the custodian or
are in the custody of any other person but for which an elected official has
claimed the right to withhold disclosure are considered decisions of the Elected
Official and are thereby appealable to Circuit Court in accordance with ORS
192.427. The RMO has no role in decisions made by an Elected Official.
E. Training
Public records training will be incorporated as part of regular employee training.
F. Procedures
The County shall abide by applicable state laws when acknowledging receipt of and
processing requests for public records.
1. Information and Records Requests

Persons requesting Public Records shall submit a public records request

iii. Submit a completed Public Records Request form in person to one of
the following County Offices or Departments: Sheriff’s Office, Clerk’s
Office, Assessor’s Office, Surveyor’s Office, Planning Department,
Building and Community Development Department, or Community
Justice Department. Public records requests submitted to an individual
department or office should be for its respective records only, and staff may
redirect a requestor if the department or office does not keep the requested
records. All other requests for records shall be submitted to the Board of
Commissioners Office.
b. Processing
After the County receives a completed Public Records request form, the
department or office in possession of the record shall either obtain all records
believed to complete the request or shall contact the RMO with an estimate of
the number of hours that will be required to satisfy the request. If the
estimated time necessary shall result in a fee exceeding $25.00, the RMO shall
contact the requestor with a fee estimate and confirm proceeding with the
request or allow the requestor to modify or withdraw the request. The
custodian of the record shall then forward the following information to the
County RMO:

e. Fees and Charges
There will be no cost for hard copies if the request is entirely processed and
provided electronically. There will be no cost for labor if the request can be met
within 15 minutes; all other requests will be charged at rates established
pursuant to the Uniform Fee Procedure and other applicable law. Charges
shall be based upon the rate set for the personnel who compile, review, copy or
in any way process records in response to a request. The County shall not
charge more than the rate of the lowest-compensated employee capable of
completing the work. If a requester wishes to review the materials with no
copies made, a charge for requisite staff time and document compilation and
production may be assessed. If the estimated charges exceed $25, the County
may require a 100% deposit. When a fee exceeds $25, the County shall obtain
written conﬁrmation of the requester’s assent to the fee prior to processing the
request.
f.
Fee Waivers or Reductions
When the County determines that a record’s disclosure is in the public
interest, the RMO may waive or substantially reduce fees to produce the
records in accordance with law.

Resolution No. 2026-xxx 
WHEREAS, state laws restrict the extent to which every Oregon public body, 
including counties and sheriffs, may inquire into immigration status, share information 
with, provide facility access to, or otherwise assist federal immigration authorities, and 
no county may exempt itself from these restrictions; and 
WHEREAS, the federal laws require compliance with the terms of federal grants, 
and the grants often expressly require adherence to federal laws, rules and Executive 
Orders; and 
WHEREAS, counties are exposed on the other side as well: the Sanctuary 
Promise Act authorizes enforcement against public bodies that fail to follow it, so a 
county that cooperates with relevant federal laws too much risks liability under state law, 
and one that cooperates too little risks loss of federal funds and federal liability; and 
WHEREAS, the Board values its partnership with the State, does not challenge 
the validity of these laws, and seeks dialogue and practical relief rather than 
confrontation; 
NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, that Josephine County 
respectfully asks the Governor, the Attorney General, and the Legislative Assembly to: 
(a) consult with the Association of Oregon Counties, the Oregon State Sheriffs'
Association, and affected counties, and weigh counties' federal funding and False
Claims Act exposure, before enacting or expanding laws restricting cooperation with
federal immigration enforcement; (b) Issue an Attorney General's Opinion and model
disclosure language for federal grant certifications that address immigration-related
cooperation or 8 U.S.C. §§ 1373 and 1644, and ensure that for funds passed through
state agencies such certifications are made by the State as prime recipient rather than
flowed down to counties; (c) enact legislation committing the State to defend and
indemnify counties and their officers against False Claims Act claims arising from good­
faith compliance with state law; (d) make any county whole for federal funds withheld or
reduced because of that compliance; (e) seek written acknowledgment from federal
granters that Oregon counties' cooperation is constrained by state law; and (f) consider
whether targeted amendments, such as permitting a public body to act where necessary
to satisfy federal law or a lawful condition of federal funding, could reduce counties'
exposure without abandoning the Legislature's policy objectives.
