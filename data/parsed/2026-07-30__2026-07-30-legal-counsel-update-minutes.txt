2. 4H Building Fund Reserves
Wally Hicks, County Legal Counsel, provided a follow-up regarding the Josephine County 4-H/Extension
Service District Building Reserve Fund. The reserve had been authorized for a ten-year period that expired
on June 30, 2026. Legal Counsel explained that the Governing Body had discretion to establish a new building
reserve, create another type of reserve, or allow the expired reserve to remain discontinued.
Upon expiration, the remaining money stayed within the 4-H/Extension Service District as unrestricted fund 
balance and could not be transferred to the County’s general funds. Approximately $44,000 had already been 
included in the adopted Fiscal Year 2026-2027 budget for furnishings and equipment at the District’s new 
leased space at Rogue Community College. That expenditure could proceed through the normal approval 
process without reestablishing the reserve. 
The Board discussed whether the District should continue accumulating funds for a future County-owned 
Extension facility. Commissioners noted that the prior facility had required substantial maintenance and that 
leased space could provide flexibility without leaving the County responsible for major repairs, replacement, 
or end-of-life building costs. Legal Counsel advised that the current fiscal-year budget and tax rate would 
remain valid; however, the District would need to reevaluate its future tax needs during the next budget cycle 
if it no longer intended to accumulate capital funds. 
By consensus, the Board allowed the Building Reserve Fund to remain expired and agreed to revisit the need 
for a new reserve during the next budget process after Extension had operated in its new location for a year. 
No formal action was taken.

5. (For 8/5 WBS) Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Housing Rehabilitation Grant 
Contract 2025 - #H25012 - Approval of Conflict of Interest and Contract 
Wally Hicks, County Legal Counsel, and Helene Lulich, Property Manager, presented an update regarding 
the new housing rehabilitation grant. The program would continue assisting qualifying low-income 
homeowners with essential repairs, with an emphasis on roofs, septic systems, wells, and related health or 
safety work. Ms. Lulich reported that more than 40 residents had already expressed interest and would receive 
application information once the agreements were approved. 
Home Bridging had assisted with the grant application and was identified as the proposed subrecipient in the 
application submitted to the State. Legal Counsel explained that the State must approve a subrecipient before 
the County can proceed with the grant. The conflict-of-interest exception currently approved by Business 
Oregon would apply to this grant; however, the State advised that the County would need to request a new 
exception if Chad Hanson were elected to the Board of County Commissioners because his status would 
materially change. 
The Board directed that the grant contract be placed on the August 5, 2026 Weekly Business Session agenda 
so the public would have an opportunity to hear the presentation and comment. Ms. Lulich and a representative 
of Home Bridging were asked to attend and answer detailed program questions. No formal action was taken. 
6. (For 8/5 WBS) Subrecipient Agreement between Josephine County and Home Bridging for the 
2025 Housing Repair and Rehabilitation Program 
The Board discussed the proposed 2025 Subrecipient Agreement together with the grant contract. Home 
Bridging would receive applications and administer the homeowner selection and rehabilitation process, while 
Josephine County would remain ultimately responsible for compliance with the grant requirements. Staff 
reported that Property Management reviewed the selected properties and applications and that Finance 
reviewed supporting financial records before reimbursement requests were submitted. 
Commissioners questioned whether the County had a standardized, ongoing audit process for all outside 
subrecipients receiving pass-through grant funds. Staff stated that the existing program included State review 
and County oversight, but the County did not currently have a single general audit procedure applicable to 
every outside subrecipient. The Board identified that issue for future policy consideration. Staff also 
confirmed that the County did not receive an administrative fee for the time spent overseeing this program. 
The Board directed that the Subrecipient Agreement be placed on the August 5, 2026 Weekly Business 
Session agenda with the related grant contract. No formal action was taken. 
7. Personnel Policy, Article 9, Section 5 - Artificial Intelligence - Chatbots 
Wally Hicks, County Legal Counsel, and Ken Clever, Information Technology Director, presented proposed 
revisions to the County’s artificial-intelligence policy. Legal Counsel recommended maintaining a written 
policy so the County could use emerging technology responsibly while remaining current with modern 
workplace practices. The policy would need to function as a living document and return for review as 
artificial-intelligence tools and legal requirements continued to develop.

Mr. Clever explained that the existing policy was overly restrictive. The revised draft distinguished between 
simple, single-user tools and applications that were shared, required authentication, accessed County systems, 
or handled protected information. Multi-user or secured tools would require Information Technology review. 
Employees using personal artificial-intelligence accounts for County business would remain subject to County 
security, confidentiality, records, and acceptable-use requirements. 
Discussion focused on protecting confidential and regulated data, including information subject to HIPAA, 
criminal-justice security requirements, and other State or federal restrictions. Mr. Clever stated that employees 
may not always understand where information submitted to a cloud-based service is stored or reused. The 
County could reduce that risk by using approved licenses or, where appropriate, developing an internally 
hosted artificial-intelligence environment that kept protected data within County-controlled systems. 
Information Technology had begun issuing Claude and Microsoft artificial-intelligence licenses to employees. 
Mr. Clever reported substantial efficiency gains from employees using the tools to create workflow 
applications, improve documents, develop policies and procedures, and assist with emergency-planning 
materials. Current token usage cost approximately $1,000 per month, which he believed was outweighed by 
the staff time being saved. 
Commissioners found a proposed requirement to identify every internal use of artificial intelligence 
unnecessarily restrictive, particularly for routine grammar, editing, and drafting assistance. The Board 
supported a more limited approach for public-facing content and discussed distinguishing legitimate image 
cleanup and graphic-design work from the creation of deceptive images, fabricated people or places, videos 
presented as real, or altered official records. 
Mr. Clever agreed to revise the draft and present it at the next managers’ meeting so department heads could 
ask questions and provide operational feedback. The Board also supported employee education and training 
concerning appropriate uses, data security, and available productivity tools. The revised policy will return to 
the Board for consideration. No formal action was taken.
