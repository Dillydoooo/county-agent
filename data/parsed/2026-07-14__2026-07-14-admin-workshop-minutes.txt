1. 4H Extension – OSU Service District IGA 
Wally Hicks, County Counsel, presented the proposed Intergovernmental Agreement between the Josephine County 4-
H/Extension Service District and Oregon State University Extension Service. Jamie Davis, OSU Southern Oregon Regional 
Director, joined the meeting remotely to answer questions regarding the agreement, facilities, expenditures, travel 
reimbursements, and longer-term operational plans. 
 
Ms. Davis explained that the Fiscal Year 2026-2027 budget includes approximately $1,650 per month to lease office space 
at Rogue Community College. The lease includes the office area and certain services, including janitorial service and refuse 
collection. The space will be vacant when occupied and must be furnished. Approximately $40,000 to $44,000 was included 
in the adopted budget from the District’s building reserve fund for cubicles, desks, chairs, and related durable furnishings. 
Ms. Davis clarified that OSU intends to purchase, rather than lease, the furnishings and that the equipment could be relocated 
and reused if Extension later moves into a larger or more permanent facility. 
 
The Board discussed whether the proposed RCC space would adequately satisfy OSU’s obligations under the agreement. 
Ms. Davis stated that it would meet the District’s immediate staffing and operational needs. She anticipated the location 
could remain suitable for one or two years but acknowledged that Extension may eventually require additional classroom 
and educational space. Future options could include leasing a larger area at RCC or identifying another long-term location. 
The Intergovernmental Agreement does not expressly govern the District’s separate building reserve fund, which was 
created by the Board for the purchase, improvement, or equipping of Extension facilities.  
 
Commissioners also requested clarification regarding travel expenses for OSU faculty based outside Josephine County. Ms. 
Davis explained that faculty located in Central Point are reimbursed by the Service District only for travel to and within 
Josephine County while providing services to County residents. Josephine County funds are not used for the employees’ 
office space, computers, professional development, or unrelated travel. Travel requests are submitted through OSU’s 
CONCUR system, include a documented business purpose, and are supported by backup documentation provided with 
OSU’s quarterly invoices to the County.  
 
Discussion also addressed language allowing the District to retain approved funds that are not remitted to OSU. Legal 
Counsel and Ms. Davis discussed the County’s continued control over District funds, reimbursement of OSU for actual 
approved expenses, and the use of retained funds for local Extension-related purposes. Commissioners emphasized the 
importance of ensuring that locally collected tax revenues remain under County oversight and directly benefit programs and 
services in Josephine County. 
 
Following discussion, the Board agreed the agreement should be placed before the Governing Body of the Josephine County 
4-H/Extension Service District for formal consideration at an upcoming Weekly Business Session. No formal action was 
taken.

4. SRS Election Allocation Form 
Legal Counsel reviewed the County’s annual election between funding under the Secure Rural Schools and Community 
Self-Determination Act and the traditional 1908 Act timber-receipt formula. Commissioners discussed anticipated federal 
timber activity, litigation that may delay future harvesting, and the relative financial stability provided by Secure Rural 
Schools funding. The Board concluded that the SRS election was the more prudent option under current conditions. 
  
The Board reviewed the allowable allocations among the three SRS titles. Title I provides funding for eligible County 
purposes, including roads and schools. Title II supports projects on federal forest lands through the applicable resource 
advisory process. Title III may be used for eligible wildfire prevention, emergency services, Firewise, and related County 
activities. Legal Counsel confirmed that Title III was capped at seven percent and Title I could receive between 80 and 85 
percent. 
 
Commissioners expressed a preference for directing as much funding as legally permitted toward Title III because of 
current wildfire risks and the needs of Emergency Management and Firewise. The proposed allocation placed the 
maximum seven percent in Title III, eight percent in Title II, and 85 percent in Title I. 
 
Commissioner Richardson moved to elect Secure Rural Schools funding with an allocation of 85 percent to Title I, eight 
percent to Title II, and seven percent to Title III. Upon roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – 
yes, Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0.

b. Public Health- Animal Shelter 
1. Animal Shetler Facility Upgrades 
Ryan Johnson, Facilities Director and Laura Jansen, Animal Shelter Manager presented an update regarding 
potential improvements to the Josephine County Animal Shelter. Staff proposed beginning with Phase I of the 
existing master plan, which would focus on medical, clinical, intake, food storage, laundry, dishwashing, and 
support spaces. Establishing those functions first would prepare the facility for future kennel construction and 
expansion.  
 
Ms. Jansen explained that Phase I could create an area capable of supporting in-house spay and neuter services. 
The ability to perform those services on site could reduce outside veterinary expenses, improve operational 
efficiency, and increase the number of animals served without immediately expanding staffing or the overall 
scope of shelter operations. 
 
The existing plans were described as conceptual rather than construction-ready. Staff intended to revise the 
layout to reflect current needs, identify which elements were essential, and obtain updated cost-per-square-foot 
estimates. A prior grant opportunity had been sufficient to support Phase I, but the County had not secured the

award. Staff agreed to investigate whether the grant program or a similar funding opportunity may become 
available again. 
 
Commissioner Richardson discussed Full Faith and Credit financing as a possible option. Such financing could 
spread the cost over the useful life of the improvements rather than requiring one year’s taxpayers to bear the 
full expense. He noted that the financing would use existing County revenues and would not require a separate

c. Public Works  
1. Purchase of 9 Vehicles- Josephine County Fleet 
Rob Brandes, Public Works Director presented the scheduled purchase of nine vehicles through the Josephine 
County Fleet Program. Staff explained that the purchases had been included in the adopted Fiscal Year 2026-
2027 budget and were generally part of the County’s established replacement schedule. 
 
The request included five replacement patrol vehicles for the Sheriff’s Office, two additional vehicles for the 
newly approved Major Crimes detective positions, one replacement vehicle for Community Justice prevention 
and school-outreach services, and one transport van. Sheriff’s Office representatives explained that patrol 
vehicles are driven extensively and include specialized equipment that affects both their useful life and resale 
value. Some replaced vehicles may be retained as backup units to support expansion of patrol operations, while 
others will be evaluated for auction. 
 
Community Justice described its replacement vehicle as one used for school outreach, youth transportation, 
home visits, medical appointments, meetings, and regular travel to the Illinois Valley. The existing vehicle had 
developed transmission problems and could not be operated reliably under normal conditions. 
 
Fleet staff explained that vehicles are obtained through State purchasing contracts at prices generally below 
retail. Departments pay mileage, operating, maintenance, and depreciation charges into the Fleet Program, 
allowing the County to build reserves for future replacements. Surplus vehicles are first evaluated for 
reassignment to other County departments before being sold at public auction. 
 
Commissioner Richardson moved to Approve the purchase of 9 Vehicles through the Josephine County Fleet Program. 
Commissioner Smith seconded the motion. Upon roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, 
Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0.

3. Board Business 
a. Communications 
Ryan Johnson, Facilities Director joined the Board’s continued discussion regarding the County’s aging public safety 
radio communications system. Commissioners reiterated that a complete replacement project requires development of 
detailed technical specifications before reliable costs can be obtained. A separate engineering consultant was reviewing 
the County’s existing sites and coverage needs, while Day Wireless had been asked to update a prior estimate for the 
current mountaintop network.  
 
Staff reported that a principal microwave link between the Justice Building and a mountaintop communications site had 
failed near the end of May. The failed link supports transmission to several other mountaintop sites, and its loss has 
created additional coverage gaps. The Sheriff’s Office had characterized the condition as an officer-safety issue because 
deputies may need to operate in pairs in areas where radio communication is unreliable. 
 
Day Wireless identified replacement equipment compatible with the County’s existing system and potentially reusable 
within a future upgraded network. The proposed equipment had an estimated lead time of eight to ten weeks. 
Commissioners discussed whether the repair should proceed while the County continues planning a complete system 
replacement. The Board agreed the immediate link must be restored as soon as practicable and should not be delayed 
while the larger project is developed. 
 
Commissioner Martin requested clarification regarding delivery and installation timelines, compatibility with the future 
system, warranties, dish alignment, and whether the quoted lead time included installation. Facilities staff agreed to 
conduct a joint call with Day Wireless and obtain firm scheduling information. Staff confirmed that the County controlled 
the necessary sites, permits and electrical service would not create significant delays, and County staff could perform the 
limited maintenance required after installation. 
 
The Board also discussed the upcoming July 27 communications workshop, which would include County departments and 
other agencies dependent upon radio coverage. The City of Cave Junction had expressed interest in participating, and the 
Board discussed inviting the City of Grants Pass. Commissioners continued to support pursuit of grants and other external 
funding but acknowledged that the County may need to finance the complete replacement independently if outside 
funding is not available within a reasonable timeframe.
