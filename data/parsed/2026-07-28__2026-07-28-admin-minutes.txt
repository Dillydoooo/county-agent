a. Assessor 
1. ORMAP Intergovernmental Agreement Contract #PO-15000-00062322 
Chris Parton, County Assessor, presented the ORMAP Intergovernmental Agreement Contract and requested authorization 
to execute the agreement on behalf of Josephine County. The agreement provides State funding for the Assessor's Office to 
continue digitizing and republishing County tax maps. 
Mr. Parton explained that the funding would support approximately 0.80 full-time equivalent personnel for one year. 
Josephine County maintains nearly 1,200 tax maps, and many maps covering the more remote portions of the County had 
not been comprehensively updated for more than 20 years. The older maps were originally prepared using manual drafting 
methods and are now being converted into a modern digital format for use by County staff and the public. 
The department had already begun the work and was maintaining a current list of completed and pending maps. Mr. Parton 
anticipated that the project would require at least one year and noted that the agreement allows the parties to consider an 
extension if additional time is needed to complete the eligible work. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve ORMAP Intergovernmental Agreement Contract #PO-15000-00062322 and 
authorize the Assessor to sign the agreement. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: 
Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
 
b. Public Works 
1. Advisory Board Appointments 
Rob Brandes, Public Works Director, presented proposed appointments to three transportation and solid waste committees 
on which Public Works has historically represented Josephine County. 
Mr. Brandes first discussed the Rogue Valley Area Commission on Transportation, commonly referred to as RVACT. The 
commission includes public- and private-sector representatives from Josephine and Jackson Counties and reviews and 
prioritizes transportation projects for consideration at the regional and state levels. The proposed arrangement would retain 
the current Public Works representative as Josephine County's primary voting member and designate Mr. Brandes as the 
alternate. 
The Board next discussed the Josephine County/Grants Pass Solid Waste Agency. The agency includes three County 
representatives, three City of Grants Pass representatives, and one City of Cave Junction representative. It oversees franchise 
agreements involving Republic Services and Waste Connections and addresses rates, audits, routes, customer concerns, and 
solid waste enforcement. Mr. Brandes offered to serve because of his long-term institutional knowledge of the agency and 
its programs. 
The final appointment involved the Middle Rogue Metropolitan Planning Organization Policy Committee. Mr. Brandes 
explained that the organization includes a Citizens Advisory Committee, Technical Advisory Committee, and Policy 
Committee. The proposed arrangement would allow the current Public Works representative to concentrate on the Technical 
Advisory Committee while Mr. Brandes served on the Policy Committee. 
Commissioners emphasized that the committees' recommendations and project-prioritization decisions can substantially 
affect Josephine County. Discussion also addressed the Solid Waste Agency's audit authority and the value of using 
enforcement and voluntary compliance to reduce illegal dumping rather than attempting to physically remove every instance 
of improperly discarded material. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the Public Works Advisory Board appointments as presented. Commissioner 
Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; 
Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0.

2. Purchase of four (4) pick-up trucks, Operation Division Replacements 
Mr. Brandes requested authorization to purchase four replacement pickup trucks for the Public Works Operations Division. 
Two of the vehicles scheduled for replacement had approximately 170,000 and 190,000 miles. Other vehicles had developed 
computer and electrical-control problems affecting air conditioning, exhaust systems, battery performance, and general 
reliability. 
The replacement trucks would support daily operations, including transporting crews and materials, pilot-car duties, 
emergency response, and snow-plowing activities. The vehicles would be purchased through the State cooperative 
purchasing contract at an estimated cost of approximately $47,000 each, compared with a manufacturer's suggested retail 
price of approximately $56,000. Public Works would also install bed liners, headache racks, and quick-response snowplow 
attachments where appropriate. 
Three of the vehicles being replaced were 2009 models, and one was a 1998 model. Commissioners discussed the County 
Fleet Program's effort to replace vehicles while they retain resale value. Mr. Brandes explained that Public Works generally 
retains heavy-duty vehicles longer than passenger vehicles because they are designed for extended use and the department 
can temporarily reassign vehicles when a unit is unavailable. 
Public Works vehicles also experience considerable idling, low-speed operation, and worksite use that may not be reflected 
by mileage alone. Before the replaced vehicles are offered at public auction, they will be evaluated for possible reassignment 
to other County departments. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to approve the purchase of four (4) pick-up trucks as Operation Division replacements. 
Commissioner Martin seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; 
Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
 
3. Amendment 2 ODOT Delivered Federal Project Wolf Creek Bridge 
Mr. Brandes presented Amendment 2 to the Oregon Department of Transportation agreement for replacement of a weight-
restricted bridge in the Wolf Creek area. The bridge provides access between the Lower Wolf Creek and Grave Creek areas 
and is limited to approximately 15 tons under its most restrictive vehicle configuration. 
The existing alignment also creates difficulty for longer commercial vehicles because the bridge connects to the roadway 
near a steep embankment and at an unfavorable angle. The replacement project would shift the bridge alignment to provide 
a safer approach and improve access for commercial, emergency, and logging vehicles. 
The estimated project cost had increased by approximately $400,000 as engineering and design work progressed. ODOT 
would provide approximately $111,000 of the additional amount, and Public Works would be responsible for approximately 
$284,000. Mr. Brandes noted that the County's ordinary match for eligible bridge projects is approximately 10.27 percent, 
although the County's share of this amendment was greater. 
Construction was anticipated to begin in approximately May 2027 and continue for three to four months. The bridge would 
be closed during construction because the traffic volume did not support the cost of a temporary bridge. Depending upon a 
traveler's location, the detour could add approximately nine to 35 minutes. 
Mr. Brandes explained that the County applies for every practical State and federal bridge opportunity because the available 
funding allows Josephine County to replace major infrastructure at a fraction of the total cost. The County obtained funding 
for three bridges during the applicable funding cycle. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve Amendment 2 for the ODOT Delivered Federal Project, Wolf Creek Bridge, as 
amended. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; 
Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
 
c. Transit 
1. Designation of Signature Authority 
Scott Chancey, Transit Manager, requested that the Finance Director be designated as an authorized official for Federal 
Transit Administration grant submissions and National Transit Database reporting. 
Mr. Chancey explained that the designation provides an appropriate separation of duties. Transit staff prepare grant 
applications and compile the required ridership, financial, cost-per-mile, and operational information. The Finance Director 
would formally submit or certify the completed information so that the same individual would not prepare, submit, and 
approve a grant or federal report. 
The designation would not eliminate the Board's authority or any required legal review. It would establish the Finance 
Director as the County's authorized official for the applicable federal reporting and submission systems, consistent with the 
arrangement historically used by the Transit program.

Yes. Motion carried 3-0.

2. DHS - IGA 188044 
Mr. Chancey presented Oregon Department of Human Services Intergovernmental Agreement No. 188044 involving 
transportation for eligible elderly and disabled individuals receiving qualifying home-support services. 
Transit currently provides door-to-door service within three-quarters of a mile of its fixed routes. Eligible passengers 
generally pay approximately $2 per trip. Under the proposed agreement, the County could request reimbursement for the 
marginal cost of qualifying rides, estimated at approximately $41.97 per trip. 
The program would not require Transit to extend service beyond its existing service area, operating hours, or holiday 
schedule. Mr. Chancey stated that technological improvements would make the program easier to administer than a similar 
program used approximately six or seven years earlier. 
The number of eligible local clients had not yet been confirmed, although Mr. Chancey anticipated fewer than 50. Transit 
had the capacity to provide approximately 100 additional rides per month without adding staff or vehicles. Approximately 
$45,000 in anticipated revenue had been included in the department's budget, although the actual amount would depend 
upon participation. 
Legal Counsel had reviewed the agreement. Reimbursement would be requested after qualifying trips were provided and 
documented. The Board agreed that receiving the federal reimbursement would more accurately recognize the cost of 
providing the service than collecting only the ordinary passenger fare. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to approve DHS Intergovernmental Agreement No. 188044 and authorize the Chair to 
sign. Commissioner Smith seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - 
Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
 
d. Airports 
1. Airports Department Update 
Mike Crisafulli, Airports Manager, provided an update regarding capital projects and operations at the Grants Pass and 
Illinois Valley Airports. 
Taxiway Alpha Extension 
The FAA grant agreement and construction contract for the Taxiway Alpha Extension had been received. Knife River 
Materials would require approximately one to one-and-one-half months to complete required submittals, testing, 
mobilization, and other preconstruction work. Construction was expected to begin near the end of August and the contract 
provided a 50-day construction period. 
The contract amount was approximately $1.694 million, compared with an engineer's estimate of approximately $2.29 
million and a next-lowest bid of approximately $2.8 million. Staff would monitor weather and smoke conditions because 
rain or wildfire smoke could affect construction and striping operations. 
Illinois Valley Airport Fuel Farm 
All Construction Elements, a Cave Junction contractor, was completing the Illinois Valley Airport fuel farm project. The 
approximately $566,000 project was funded through a State grant awarded under House Bill 5202. The principal delay 
involved fabrication and delivery of the fuel tank, which required approximately four months after approval of the 
contractor's submittals. Delivery was anticipated in October or November, with the remaining work coordinated so the 
facility could be operational before the following spring. 
Runway 13/31 Extension and Hangar Building 
The Runway 13/31 Extension was in the closeout phase. The FAA was scheduling a flight inspection, with an additional 
inspection anticipated later in the summer for the instrument approaches. Staff expected the first inspection to occur within 
approximately four weeks. Construction of the four-unit hangar building was complete, and the County was waiting for the 
contractor's final invoice before closing the project. 
Wildfire Operations 
The airports had experienced less wildfire-related aircraft activity than during the prior year because some response aircraft 
were staged closer to current incidents. During the prior September, approximately 13 helicopters had been staged at the 
airport. Airports was using asphalt grindings from a Knife River highway project to construct temporary helicopter parking 
pads in previously unusable areas, providing additional stabilized parking capacity during future wildfire operations. 
Hangar Leases and Insurance Requirements 
There were no current hangar vacancies. Staff continued working through expired or incomplete ground leases and had 
substantially completed a commercial ground-lease template. A private-hangar ground-lease template was expected to 
require additional time. 
Mr. Crisafulli was also working with the County's insurance representatives to review and standardize insurance 
requirements. Private hangar tenants had reported annual insurance quotes of approximately $1,500 for policies meeting a

Ms. Eckhardt-Doupe requested that the Board establish the County's medical-insurance contribution increase for non-union 
employees before open enrollment began on August 15, 2026.

employees held confidential or supervisory positions.
