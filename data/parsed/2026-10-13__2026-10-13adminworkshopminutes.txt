1. Contract for Personal Services – Amendment No. 1 with BC Electronics, LLC for 
maintenance and installation of IT equipment on transit vehicles – Extend the term 
to December 31, 2027 and increase the hourly rate from $100 to $125 – Not to exceed 
$25,000 – Approval and signature 
Scott Chancey, Transit Program Supervisor, presented Amendment No. 1 to the Contract for Personal Services 
with BC Electronics, LLC. He explained that the original contract had been approved by the full Board and 
that Legal Counsel advised the amendment should be approved by the Board as well. The contractor maintains, 
installs and updates the IT equipment on all transit vehicles, including camera, video and audio systems, tablets 
running Transit software, GPS units and Cradlepoint modems that transmit data and carry a free Wi-Fi channel 
for passengers. The original contract expired in December 2025; the lapse was identified by Finance, and 
Transit needs a contract in place for the coming year. Work is performed on an as-needed basis, with 
approximately $7,000 budgeted for the service this year and the amendment capped at $25,000. The service 
is paid from state funds allocated to transit operations within the Transit budget, not County General Fund. 
In response to Board questions, Mr. Chancey stated that the contractor is a local individual with an engineering 
background who has installed camera systems and IT equipment for Transit for approximately five years, 
responds within 24 hours when equipment fails, and performs fleet-wide updates in the evening or on 
Saturdays when the vehicles are in the yard. He explained that he had asked the County IT Department three 
or four times whether it wished to perform the work before engaging the contractor and had not received a 
commitment; the equipment resides on vendor networks rather than County systems, anything that interacts 
with the County network is cleared with IT, and he would continue to offer the work to IT as a way to leverage

2. Spare Labs Inc. Invoice No. SL-202609-1075 – Annual subscription for the Spare 
One passenger app (trip planning, real-time bus arrival, ride booking and pass 
purchases) for July 30, 2026 through July 29, 2027 – $31,500 – Approval of payment 
Mr. Chancey presented Spare Labs Inc. Invoice No. SL-202609-1075 in the amount of $31,500 for the annual 
subscription to the Spare One passenger app, a platform provided by the same company that supplies Transit’s 
demand-response dispatching software. Passengers use the app on a smart device to plan trips, view projected 
and real-time bus arrivals by stop, book rides and purchase passes. The ride-booking function supported the 
Transit On Demand evening service, which operated from 6:00 to 9:30 p.m. until Mr. Chancey discontinued 
it in July for financial reasons; the capability remains available if the service resumes and is included in the 
annual cost. The pass feature issues a dynamic bus pass or trip ticket displayed on the rider’s phone with 
motion that makes it difficult to counterfeit, similar to electronic concert tickets. The subscription was 
originally $30,000 annually, and the $31,500 invoice reflects a 5 percent increase, consistent with other Transit 
services. The cost is included in the Transit budget from funds earmarked for public transit service, with no 
County General Fund involved. 
In response to a Board question about whether the IT Department had reviewed the renewal, Mr. Chancey 
stated that IT was involved in the first year, that nothing resides on County equipment because riders use the 
vendor’s online platform and the information is transmitted to Transit, and that he would ask IT to identify 
any concerns. He also reported that the Treasurer’s Office now has access to the platform because pass 
purchases are deposited to County accounts. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve payment of Spare Labs Inc. Invoice No. SL-202609-1075 in the 
amount of $31,500. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner 
Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
3. Board Business 
a. Board Order No. 2026-059 – In the Matter of Continuing the Suspension of Acceptance of 
Applications for Mineral Exploration Permits and Mining Leases – Continues the 
suspension established by Board Order No. 2026-040 for an additional one (1) year, 
effective October 8, 2026 through October 8, 2027 
Chair Smith stated that he had placed Board Order No. 2026-059 on the agenda to continue the suspension of 
the acceptance of applications for mineral exploration permits and mining leases established by Board Order 
No. 2026-040. Dave Streeter, Forestry, advised that the prior suspension had expired on October 8, 2026, so 
an application could technically be submitted at any time, and recommended that the Board suspend 
acceptance of applications and then address the ordinance however it saw fit rather than be placed in the 
position of processing an application under the existing ordinance. 
Commissioners discussed the length of the suspension and the future of the Mining Ordinance (Josephine 
County Code Chapter 5.15). Chair Smith favored a longer suspension of up to one year because the County is 
in the midst of litigation predicated on the ordinance. Commissioner Martin preferred to begin the process of 
repealing the ordinance, stating that applications could be handled case by case through a standard contract 
without the liability exposure the ordinance has created and that the pending litigation would be decided on 
the facts in place at the time. Commissioners noted that County Legal Counsel had indicated the subject is 
outside his practice area and that the Board would consider engaging counsel with relevant expertise later in 
the week, and that the previous suspension had run to October 8 and lapsed before the Board completed its 
work on the ordinance, which favored a shorter term to keep the matter on task. It was noted that this would 
be the third 90-day suspension, and the Board was asked to make the matter a priority so that it is resolved 
within the period.

b. Letter to Senators Wyden, Merkley, Crapo and Risch and Congressman Bentz in Support 
of Continued Secure Rural Schools (SRS) Funding – Approval and signatures 
Commissioner Martin presented a letter to Senators Wyden, Merkley, Crapo and Risch and Congressman 
Bentz encouraging passage of the Secure Rural Schools reauthorization legislation introduced in Congress on 
September 30, 2026. She explained that the County relies on SRS payments and should be proactive rather 
than reactive, noting that the last lapse in the program took approximately two years to restore. The County 
will receive one more payment in spring 2027, which is already budgeted, and needs certainty to budget for 
the coming years; the legislation under consideration would extend the program to 2030. Commissioner 
Richardson suggested that the letter place greater emphasis on a permanent, long-term solution rather than 
another two- or three-year extension, citing the importance of stable funding for the County’s emergency 
services, schools and the Forest Service. Commissioner Martin agreed to revise the letter to include that 
request. 
Commissioners also discussed the relationship between SRS payments and O&C timber receipts, including 
the possibility that the pending revision of the O&C resource management plan could increase harvest and 
again present the County with a choice between the two sources. They noted that O&C receipts are deposited 
to the General Fund at the County’s discretion, while SRS funds are restricted to specified categories, 
including search and rescue, fire planning and emergency services; that Payment in Lieu of Taxes is permanent 
but applies to Forest Service lands and is not the same as O&C funding; and that the O&C Act of 1937 directs 
that those lands be managed for permanent forest production. Commissioners expressed support for both 
funding sources and noted that Congressman Bentz would be in the area the following week. The Board 
approved the letter, as revised, without objection.
