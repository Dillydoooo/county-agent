a. Juvenile Justice 
1. Provider Agreement with Restorative Solutions in the amount of $20,000.00 
Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, presented a renewal agreement with Restorative Solutions for restorative justice 
training services. Hyde explained that restorative justice focuses on repairing harm caused by criminal behavior through 
communication, accountability, and relationship restoration. The training supports Community Justice staff and reinforces 
the department's restorative justice model, particularly within juvenile programs. Hyde noted the agreement is an annual 
renewal, not to exceed $20,000, and was included within the approved budget. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve Contract with Restorative Solutions in the amount of $20,000.00. Commissioner 
Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, 
Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
 
2. Resolution No. 2026-024; In the Matter of the Josephine County Board of Commissioners Affirming   
Tobacco Retail Enforcement as Way to Protect Legal Consumer Access and Support a Regulated    
Market Place 
Eckhardt-Doupen Martinez, Prevention, presented a revised resolution regarding tobacco retail enforcement. Martinez 
explained that Community Justice receives state funding to conduct tobacco retailer inspections, compliance checks, and 
youth access enforcement activities. He clarified that the resolution did not originate from Community Justice but was 
revised at the Board's request to recognize both adult consumer choice and the protection of lawful businesses while 
maintaining support for youth tobacco prevention efforts. Commissioners discussed the prior resolution approved by the 
Board and acknowledged the need to formally correct the process through a properly noticed agenda item. Discussion 
occurred regarding balancing adult consumer choice with efforts to prevent youth access to tobacco and nicotine products. 
Commissioners also discussed correspondence received from public health advocacy organizations and the importance of 
distinguishing adult consumer rights from youth prevention strategies 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve Rescind Resolution No. 2026-009 and Approve Resolution No. 2026-027; In the 
Matter of the Josephine County Board of Commissioners Affirming Tobacco Retail Enforcement as Way to Protect Legal 
Consumer Access and Support a Regulated Market Place. Commissioner Smith seconded the motion. Upon roll call; 
Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
 
b. Public Works 
1. Access Josephine County Property to Devils Slide 
Richard Pike, Public Works Manager, and Helene Lulich, Property Manager, presented a request from Rural Metro Fire 
and the Oregon Department of Forestry for access across County-owned property near Devil's Slide. Meyer explained that 
emergency responders have experienced difficulties accessing the area during fire suppression and emergency response 
incidents and that the proposed project would create a gravel access route sufficient for fire apparatus and emergency 
vehicles. Meyer noted that the project would be completed and maintained by the requesting agencies with no anticipated 
financial impact to Josephine County. Commissioners discussed emergency access needs, wildfire response, maintenance 
responsibilities, property rights, and liability considerations. Commissioner Richardson expressed concern regarding long-
term access rights and recommended a more formal agreement rather than informal email authorization. Legal Counsel 
advised that an access agreement would provide appropriate protections without creating a permanent easement. 
Commissioners agreed that emergency access improvements were beneficial and directed staff to proceed with temporary 
authorization while preparing a more formal agreement for future consideration.

3. BOARD BUSINESS 
a. Oregon  Groundwater Association Presentation 
Kevin Gill, representing the Oregon Groundwater Association, provided an overview of pending Oregon Water Resources 
Department rulemaking related to groundwater regulation, exempt wells, enforcement procedures, and water distribution. 
Gill explained that many Josephine County residents rely upon private wells and emphasized the importance of local 
participation in the rulemaking process. He encouraged the Board to submit comments before the June 26, 2026 deadline 
and offered to provide supporting materials and suggested comment language.  
Commissioners discussed water rights, groundwater management, exempt well regulation, and proposed state-level policy 
changes. Concerns were expressed regarding potential future requirements affecting private well owners and agricultural 
water users. The Board directed staff to place the matter on a future agenda for further discussion and public awareness 
 
b. Resolution No. 2026-028; In the Matter of Consenting to the Change in Assignment of Southern Oregon  
    Sanitation, Inc. 
Wally Hicks, Counsel, presented Resolution No. 2026-028 regarding the proposed assignment and transfer of Southern 
Oregon Sanitation, Inc. franchise interests. Hicks explained that the resolution was substantially consistent with actions 
being considered by other affected jurisdictions and would also be presented during the upcoming Weekly Business Session 
to allow for public comment prior to any Board action.  
Representative Trent Carpenter of Southern Oregon Sanitation and Waste Connections discussed the pending ownership 
transfer and reviewed the regulatory process governing solid waste franchises. Discussion focused on franchise oversight, 
rate-setting authority, annual financial reviews, and the role of the Southern Oregon Solid Waste Agency. Commissioners 
expressed interest in ensuring that the public understood that any future rate increases would remain subject to existing 
review and approval processes and that the proposed transaction represented an ownership change rather than a modification 
of service levels or franchise obligations.  
No action was taken. The matter was scheduled for consideration during the Weekly Business Session
