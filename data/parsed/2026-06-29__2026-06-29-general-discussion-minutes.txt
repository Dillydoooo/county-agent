2. Murphy Irrigation District 
Chair Smith opened discussion regarding the proposed removal of the Murphy Diversion Dam and invited 
representatives of the Murphy Irrigation District and interested parties to participate in an open discussion 
regarding the project. Commissioner Richardson stated that the purpose of the meeting was to better understand 
the proposed removal project, evaluate possible alternatives, and identify opportunities to preserve reliable 
irrigation service while addressing environmental concerns. Representatives of the Murphy Irrigation District, 
including Thoren Halvorsen, Kevin Fay, Kristin Bryke, Andrea Applegate, Herman Berchiger, Sr., and 
Dave Combe, participated throughout the discussion. Public Works Director Rob Brandes also provided 
historical information regarding the diversion structure and surrounding infrastructure. 
 
Mr. Halvorsen reviewed the history of the Murphy Irrigation District's collaboration agreement with 
WaterWatch of Oregon, explaining that the District entered into negotiations after being advised that litigation 
was likely if an agreement could not be reached. Ms. Bryke discussed the legal framework surrounding the 
agreement, the permitting process, and the District's obligations under the negotiated settlement. 
Commissioners questioned whether additional alternatives remained available and discussed the financial 
challenges faced by the District in defending environmental litigation. 
 
Discussion then shifted to the engineering aspects of the proposed project. Mr. Fay explained the proposed 
replacement of the existing gravity-fed diversion with a pumping system, describing anticipated operational 
changes, infrastructure improvements, maintenance requirements, and long-term operating costs. 
Commissioner Smith questioned whether relocating portions of the project to nearby County-owned property 
could simplify maintenance access and reduce future operational challenges. Participants also discussed 
whether portable pumping equipment or alternative configurations might better serve District patrons over the 
long term. 
 
Commissioners and Murphy Irrigation District representatives discussed the economic impacts of converting 
to a pumping system. Mr. Halvorsen and Mr. Fay expressed concern that increasing electrical costs, equipment 
replacement, and ongoing maintenance expenses could substantially increase assessments to District patrons. 
Commissioner Richardson observed that rising operational costs could eventually reduce participation in the 
District, placing greater financial responsibility on remaining users and potentially affecting the long-term 
sustainability of irrigated agriculture within the Murphy area. 
 
Environmental considerations were also discussed. Mr. Combe shared observations regarding fish passage at 
the existing diversion structure and questioned whether available biological information demonstrated that 
removal of the diversion would significantly improve fish habitat. Commissioners discussed previous 
diversion removal projects within Southern Oregon and questioned whether additional scientific evaluation 
would be beneficial before permanently removing the existing diversion.

Ms. Applegate described her family's long history along the Applegate River and discussed the importance of 
dependable irrigation to local agricultural operations and rural property owners. Mr. Berchiger shared similar 
concerns, emphasizing the value of preserving affordable irrigation for family farms and expressing concern 
that long-term operating costs associated with the proposed pumping system could become burdensome for 
future generations. 
 
Public Works Director Rob Brandes provided historical perspective regarding the diversion structure and 
surrounding infrastructure based upon his experience with Josephine County Public Works. Discussion 
included historical maintenance activities, previous County involvement in the area, roadway access, and 
engineering considerations associated with the existing diversion. 
 
Throughout the discussion, Commissioners expressed appreciation for the information provided by Murphy 
Irrigation District representatives and agreed that additional evaluation of potential alternatives may be 
appropriate before the project proceeds. The Board discussed opportunities for continued coordination with 
state and federal agencies, Murphy Irrigation District representatives, and other interested stakeholders to 
identify solutions that preserve reliable irrigation service while addressing environmental and regulatory 
concerns.
