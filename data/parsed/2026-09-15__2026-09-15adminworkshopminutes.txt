2. Department Business 
a. Information Technology 
1. Strategic Communication Planning 
Thomas Gray, Public Information and Web Coordinator, reported that the second review period for the 
Strategic Communications Plan had concluded without additional edits and that the plan was ready for final 
review. He planned to consult Legal Counsel regarding whether formal adoption or Board consensus would 
be the appropriate approval process. Gray also reported that updates to the County social media and public 
records policies were nearing completion. CivicsPlus social-media archiving was being rolled out to 
improve public-record retention and moderation compliance, and Loomly social-media planning software 
was being expanded to additional departments following an initial rollout with Public Health. Gray said the 
County currently maintains approximately 24 to 25 social-media accounts across several departments and 
platforms, with content oversight shared between his office and the responsible department leadership. 
Gray also discussed communications work with Emergency Management, including Firewise materials, 
Know Your Zone and emergency-notification planning, and development of the jocoalerts.org web address 
as a centralized location for emergency information. He reported that Economic Development Grant 
promotions were underway and that the AI pilot project remained on schedule for an end-of-week report, 
including policy recommendations and a proposed auditing/logging tool for County AI software. Website 
accessibility work was expected to begin in October toward the federal compliance deadline in late April 
2027. Gray also reported positive feedback on the weekly County updates and said onboarding had begun 
for JustFOIA, which will provide a centralized public-record-request portal and tracking system; he 
currently receives approximately five to ten requests per week. No formal action was taken. 
b. Prevention 
1. Grant Agreement with Continuum Behavioral Health & Recovery Services for 
Opioid Settlement Funds Abatement Strategies - $140,206.58 
Shawn Martinez, Prevention and Treatment Services Manager, reviewed the County's opioid-settlement 
funding program before presenting the applications. She explained that Josephine County has 13 opioid 
settlements with varying payment schedules and reporting requirements, and that required reporting is 
current. The County has approximately $4.7 million in settlement funds on hand or projected over the life of 
the settlements. Prior community planning identified allowable priorities under Exhibit E, including 
overdose reversal, medication-assisted treatment, services for pregnant and postpartum women, warm 
handoffs and recovery services, treatment for incarcerated populations, prevention, syringe-service 
programs, and evidence-based data collection. The current application round opened May 15 and closed 
June 15; a subcommittee reviewed three applications and the Addictions Mental Health Public Health 
Advisory Committee recommended all three to the Board. Martinez then presented Continuum Behavioral 
Health & Recovery Services' request for $140,206.58 to support naloxone, medication-assisted treatment, 
services for pregnant and postpartum women, and expanded warm-handoff services. The proposal includes 
ambulatory withdrawal management in Cave Junction and Grants Pass and inpatient withdrawal

2. Grant Agreement of Opioid Settlement Funding with Grants Pass Sobering Center - 
$44,239.68 
Martinez presented the Grants Pass Sobering Center request for $44,239.68 to expand warm-handoff and 
recovery services. The funding would support peer services so that assistance remains available 24 hours a 
day, 365 days a year. Martinez explained that a warm handoff connects a person who is willing to enter 
treatment directly with peer support and treatment providers rather than simply providing a referral. The 
Board discussed the growth of the Sobering Center from its original function into a broader recovery-
support resource and the importance of maintaining sufficient staffing for around-the-clock services. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the Grant Agreement of Opioid Settlement Funding with Grants Pass 
Sobering Center in the amount of $44,239.68. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: 
Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
3. Grant Agreement for Opioid Settlement Funding with HIV Alliance - $28,874.00 
Martinez presented HIV Alliance's request for $28,874.00 to support naloxone access and overdose-reversal 
training, medication-assisted treatment connections, infectious-disease testing, and linkage to care for high-
risk individuals affected by substance-use disorder. The proposal integrates HIV, hepatitis C and syphilis 
rapid testing with care coordination and peer-support services. Martinez clarified that none of the requested 
settlement funds would be used to purchase syringes. The Board discussed harm-reduction services, prior 
funding history, and the fact that HIV Alliance is headquartered in Eugene but provides services in Southern 
Oregon, including Josephine County. Following approval of all three grants, Martinez and the Board 
discussed the finite nature of the settlement funds, whether long-term investment or endowment-type use is 
permissible, and the importance of supporting programs that can become sustainable. Martinez stated she 
would verify the applicable restrictions and noted a continuing community need for residential treatment 
capacity. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the Grant Agreement for Opioid Settlement Funding with HIV Alliance 
in the amount of $28,874.00. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner 
Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0.

c. Federal Roadless Rule comment 
Commissioner Richardson presented Josephine County's proposed comment regarding the U.S. Forest 
Service rulemaking on the 2001 Roadless Area Conservation Rule. He stated that approximately 146,000 
acres in Josephine County carry a roadless designation and that the County's principal request was to have a 
meaningful role in future rulemaking affecting local federal lands. The Board discussed the importance of 
considering Josephine County-specific conditions, including wildfire suppression and mitigation, 
emergency evacuation routes, road maintenance, and protection of water resources. Input had been 
incorporated from Emergency Management, Public Works, and the Sheriff's Office. Commissioners 
emphasized that wilderness areas are governed separately and that the County comment was intended to 
ensure local conditions and public-safety needs are considered rather than relying only on national-level 
assumptions. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to approve submission of Josephine County’s comment to the US Forest 
Service regarding the proposed rescission of the 2001 Roadless Area Conservation Rule. Commissioner Martin 
seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; 
Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0.
