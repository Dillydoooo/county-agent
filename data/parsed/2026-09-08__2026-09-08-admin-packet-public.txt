BY-LAWS 
 
ARTICLE I – NAME AND PURPOSE 
Section 1 – Name: The name of this organization shall be the Josephine County Economic 
Development Grant Advisory Committee (JCEDGAC). 
Section 2 – Purpose: The purpose of the JCEDGAC is to advise and make recommendations 
to the Josephine County Board of Commissioners (BCC) on matters pertaining to awarding 
Josephine County Economic Development Grants. Grants may support private-sector economic 
development projects as well as public infrastructure projects that enable new or expanded 
economic development, including projects proposed by Josephine County departments, that 
demonstrate a positive economic impact to Josephine County. Grant funds are not a substitute 
for a department’s ordinary maintenance, repair or capital-replacement obligations. The 
JCEDGAC shall comply with Josephine County Administrative Policies and Procedures, Policy 
A-7, Advisory Bodies (Board, Commissioners, Committees, Sub-Committees). 
ARTICLE II – COMMITTEE ROLE AND RESPONSIBILITIES 
Section 1 – JCEDGAC Role and Responsibilities: The JCEDGAC will perform the following: 
A. Review and make recommendations for funding submitted applications from all eligible 
applicants, 
including 
businesses, 
non-profit 
organizations, 
Josephine 
County 
departments and other entities.  
B. Evaluate each application on the basis of its overall economic impact to Josephine 
County, as described in Section 2 of this Article.  
Section 2 – Evaluation of Economic Impact: Applications shall be evaluated on demonstrated 
economic impact to Josephine County. Job creation and job retention are relevant factors, but 
neither shall be required for an application to receive a favorable recommendation. Economic 
impact may be demonstrated by anyone, or a combination of the following, without limitation: 
A. Jobs created or jobs retained; 
B. Construction or extension of development-enabling infrastructure – new infrastructure, or 
new service or capacity, that opens specific sites or areas for new or expanded 
economic activity, such as extending water, sewer, roads, broadband or other utilities to 
serve a business park, airport development area or industrial or commercial site: 
C. Increased commerce, tourism or other economic activity; 
D. Expanded services or capacity that support businesses, the workforce or residents of the 
County; 
E. Leveraging of additional federal, state, local or private investment; and 
F. Other measurable economic or public benefits to the county. 
 
The JCEDGAC may use a scoring rubric approved by the BCC to elevate and compare 
applications under this Article.

Section 3 – Infrastructure Eligibility: An infrastructure project, including on proposed by a 
County department, is eligible only if it creates, extends  or expands infrastructure service or 
capacity that directly enables identifiable new or expanded economic development – for 
example, extending sewer or water service to open a business park or airport development 
area. Maintenance, repair, replacement or rehabilitation of existing infrastructure and projects 
whose primary purpose is to restore or sustain existing services levels – for example, repairing 
an existing sewage treatment plant – are not eligible for grants funds and remain the 
responsibility of the applicable department’s ordinary operating or capital budget. 
Section 4 – Advisory Committee: The JCEDGAC shall be advisory in nature only and shall 
have no responsibility or authority over any public employee of the County. 
Section 5 – Additional Tasks: The JCEDGAC will review applications, budgets and project 
proposals as requested by the BCC or the Josephine County Finance Director. 
ARTICLE III – MEMBERSHIP 
Section 1 – Committee Composition: The JCEDGAC shall consist of five (5) members, who 
shall be appointed by the BCC and serve at the pleasure of the BCC. 
Section 2 – Membership Qualifications: All members of the JCEDGAC must be at least 
eighteen (18) years of age and must reside within the boundaries of Josephine County. 
Committee membership representation shall be as follows: 
A. Position one (1) will be a Business Owner. 
B. Position two (2) will be a Business Owner 
C. Position three (3) will be a member at large. 
D. Position four (4) will be a member at large. 
E. Position five (5) will be a member at large. 
 
Section 3 – Appointment of Members: All members of the committee shall be appointed or 
removed at the pleasure of the BCC. When a vacancy occurs, the BCC shall appoint a 
replacement member and may refer applications to the JCEDGAC for recommendation. 
Appointments to fill vacant positions shall be for the remainder of the term of said vacant 
positions.  
Section 4 – Term of Membership: All JCEDGAC members shall serve a one-year term and are 
eligible for re-appointment by the BCC. Appointments shall be limited to two (2) consecutive 
terms.  
Section 5 – Officers: Members of the advisory committee shall elect a chairperson and a vice-
chairperson, who will serve a term of one calendar year or until a successor is elected. The 
officers of the Committee shall be a Chair and a Vice Chair. The officers shall be elected at the 
first meeting of the JCEDGAC and shall take office immediately. The Chair shall preside at all 
meetings and shall perform those functions usually associated with the office and shall be able 
to make, second and vote on all motions. The Vice Chair shall preside in the absence of the 
Chair.  
EXHIBIT 1 
 
Section 6 – Resignation, Termination and Absences: Resignation from the JCEDGAC must 
be in writing and received by the BCC. Upon the recommendation of the Josephine County 
Finance Director, a Committee member may be terminated from the JCEDGAC by the BCC due 
to (1) excess absences, if that member has more than two (2) unexcused absences from 
Committee meetings in one year, and (2) conflict of interest. 
Section 7 – Conflict of Interest: Members shall comply with Oregon Government Ethics Law. 
As soon as a member becomes aware of an actual or potential conflict of interest concerning an 
application or other matter before the Committee, the member shall publicly disclose the nature 
of the conflict. 
A member with an actual or potential conflict of interest shall not participate in the review, 
discussion, scoring, recommendation or vote concerning that application or matter. The 
members’ disclosure and recusal shall be recorded in the meeting minutes. A recused member 
shall not be counted as participating in the matter for purposes of voting.

ARTICLE IV – MEETINGS 
Section 1 – Meeting Schedule: The JCEDGAC shall meet at least three (3) times per year at a 
time and place designated by the Josephine County Finance Director.  
Section 2 – Decision Making: The committee will make decisions by consensus of a majority 
of the members present. In the event there are four (4) members of the committee present and 
the decision ends in a tie, the tie breaker decision will go the Board of Commissioners.  
Section 3 – Quorum: Most of the filled positions then appointed to the committee will constitute 
a quorum for the purposes of conduction business and making decisions.  
Section 4 – Conduct of Meetings: The Josephine County Finance Director shall convene 
regularly scheduled meetings and shall control the conduct of the meetings. The Finance 
Director shall represent the JCEDGAC at meetings and communication with the BCC. 
Section 5 – Minutes and Records: The Josephine County Finance Director or his or her 
designee shall be responsible for keeping minutes of meetings and records of all JCEDGAC 
actions, and shall send out meeting notices and announcements, distribute copies of minutes 
and agendas to each JCEDGAC member and maintain records in accordance with Oregon 
Public Records Laws and state archives requirements.  
Section 6 – Open Meetings: All meetings of the JCEDGAC shall be open to the public. Notice 
of meetings shall be given by the Josephine County Finance Director in accordance with 
Oregon Public Meetings Laws.  
Section 7 – Special Meetings: Special meetings of the JCEDGAC may be called by the BCC 
of the Josephine County Finance Director. Notices of special meetings shall be sent out by the 
Finance Director to each Committee member at least one (1) week prior to the meeting. 
ARTICLE V – AMENDMENTS 
Section 1 – Amendments: These Bylaws may be amended by resolution of the Josephine 
County Board of Commissioners.

INSURANCE COVERAGE AND 
RESPONSIBILITIES OF VOLUNTEERS 
 
 
 
 
 
All volunteers will be provided a copy of their responsibilities. 
 
Josephine County provides volunteers with certain insurance coverage as described below.  Please read the following and sign below.  
If you have any questions, please ask your manager, supervisor, or Human Resources office. 
 
General Insurance Coverage:  Under certain circumstances, Josephine County will provide you with insurance coverage for bodily 
injury or property damage.  This coverage is only available when:   
 
1. Your actions are limited to only those duties assigned to you in your job description, or assigned to you by an authorized 
manager or supervisor; and 
2. You perform your assigned duties reasonably and in good faith, and you do not act in a manner that is reckless, or with intent 
to cause harm. 
 
The limits of this insurance coverage are provided in the Oregon Tort Claims Act, ORS 30.260 through 30.300. 
 
Worker’s Compensation Coverage:  County elects to provide Worker’s Compensation coverage for volunteers of the Sheriff’s Office 
Reserves and Search & Rescue. 
 
Accident Insurance Coverage:  The County also provides coverage for accidents sustained while performing services as a Registered 
Volunteer.  This coverage is only available when the injury occurs as the result of an accident which happens while performing assigned 
volunteer duties.  This coverage is secondary insurance; it will pay $5,000 for accidental death or disability and for eligible medical 
expenses up to $25,000 only after any other available insurance is applied toward the medical bill.   
 
Automobile Insurance Coverage:  County-Owned Vehicles:  Under certain circumstances, if you have an accident while you are 
driving a County-owned vehicle to perform assigned duties, the County will pay and defend claims against you for bodily injury or 
property damage.  The County will also pay for any damage to the County vehicle.  
 
Personal Vehicles:  Under certain circumstances, if you have an accident while you are driving your personal vehicle (or All Terrain 
Vehicle- ATV, specialty vehicle, aircraft, or other vehicle) to perform assigned duties, the County will pay and defend claims against 
you for bodily injury or property damage to others.  The County, however, shall not provide any insurance coverage for damages to 
your vehicle.  Payment of any claims by the County is secondary to your personal automobile insurance.    
 
Any coverage for accidents while driving County-Owned vehicles or Personal vehicles is only available when:   
 
1. You are driving as part of those duties assigned to you in your job description, or assigned to you by an authorized manager or 
supervisor; and 
2. You drive in a reasonable, cautious, and prudent manner, and follow all applicable laws, with due regard for safety; and 
3. You immediately report the accident to your manager or supervisor; and 
4. You cooperate fully with County Risk Management and Legal Counsel. 
 
The limits of this insurance coverage provided in the Oregon Tort Claims Act, ORS 30.260 through 30.300. 
 
Limitations:  Josephine County will NOT provide you with insurance coverage, and you may be personally responsible for any bodily 
injury, property damage, or damage to a vehicle if: 
 
1. Your actions are contrary to or not part of those duties assigned to you in your job description or by an authorized manager or 
supervisor; or  
2. You act recklessly, maliciously, with the intent to cause damage or injury, or  
 
3. You are accused of a crime; or  
4. You fail to cooperate with the Risk Management Office or County Legal Counsel, or you act in a manner that is against the 
County’s interests.

CONFIDENTIALITY AND  
ETHICS AGREEMENT 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Confidentiality is the preservation of information disclosed in a professional working relationship.  Certain 
information you obtain as a volunteer regarding clients and patrons is confidential.  Disclosure of such information 
could make you legally liable for violating confidentiality laws. 
 
There are many specific laws on what information is considered to be confidential, and what information is 
considered to be public.  In general, any information that relates to a specific person is confidential.   
 
Disclosing confidential information will lead to immediate dismissal as a volunteer with Josephine County.  In 
addition, disclosure of confidential information or improper use of confidential information to your benefit may 
result in criminal charges being brought against you.  Fines range from $2,500.00 to $25,000.00, as well as 
imprisonment in jail.
