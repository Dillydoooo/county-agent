d. Human Resources (taken first) 
1. Position Requisitions 
a. Community Corrections - Replace one Senior Department Specialist position with a Community 
Corrections Case Specialist position 
Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe, Interim Human Resources Director, and Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, presented the 
request to replace one existing Senior Department Specialist position in Community Corrections with a Community 
Corrections Case Specialist position. 
Mr. Hyde reported that the number of individuals on community supervision had increased from approximately 750 at the 
beginning of the year to approximately 820, with additional growth anticipated as lower-level drug cases are prosecuted. 
The reclassified position would assist with increased intake volume, lower-risk client check-ins, compliance monitoring, 
and required data tracking. He stated that sufficient funding is available within the Adult Community Corrections Fund to 
support the staffing change. 
The Board approved the position reclassification without objection. 
a. Public Health 
1. WIC Synergy Contract Amendment 
Janet Fredrickson, Public Health Director, and Kaitlen Carbajal presented an amendment extending the County's contract 
with Synergy for qualified WIC nutrition services through the end of 2026. 
Public Health explained that WIC requires access to qualified nutrition services and that Synergy has been used as a 
temporary solution since the County lost its in-house dietitian. The amendment adds approximately $30,000, paid from 
the WIC budget. Synergy currently provides approximately 40 to 50 hours of service per month at about $70 per hour. 
Staff hopes to have an in-house dietitian in place by January 2027; that position would be partially assigned to WIC and 
could also support broader clinic nutrition services under a recently received grant. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the WIC Synergy Contract Amendment as presented. Commissioner Richardson 
seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - 
Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
b. Transit 
1. EV Battery Work Order 
Scott Chancey, Transit Director, presented a work order for battery repairs to the County's remaining 2019 battery-electric 
transit bus. 
Mr. Chancey explained that, following a 2024 thermal event involving another electric bus, additional safety systems were 
installed before the 2019 bus could be returned to service. A manufacturer battery evaluation found that six of the twelve 
battery packs should be refurbished or replaced. The proposed work is approximately $30,000 to $31,000, with about 
89.75 percent paid from an existing federal grant and an estimated local match of approximately $3,100 already available 
in the Transit budget. Staff expects the repair to restore a usable, though limited, driving range for partial shifts and 
backup service. Mr. Chancey recommended the partial battery repair rather than an approximately $70,000 full 
replacement because of uncertainty regarding battery longevity and range, and because remaining grant funds may be 
rolled into a future bus-replacement grant. 
The Board approved the EV battery work order without objection.

c. Finance 
1. Economic Development Committee 
a. Board Order approving Membership 
Matthew Eide, Finance Director, updated the Board on recruitment for the Economic Development Committee and the 
membership requirements contained in the Board Order. 
Finance has received approximately a dozen applications following additional public outreach, but has had difficulty 
finding applicants who meet the designated large-business and medium-business categories. The Board and staff also 
discussed the intended meaning of the employment-readiness position, with Workforce Oregon, schools, and Rogue 
Community College identified as possible sources. Mr. Eide stated that Finance will continue targeted outreach and may 
return with recommended changes to the membership requirements if the required categories cannot be filled. The Board 
discussed whether a seven-member committee may be unnecessarily difficult to recruit and convene, and agreed the 
structure can be reevaluated after the first year. 
b. Governing Rules 
Mr. Eide also discussed the proposed governing rules, timeline, and intended use of economic-development lottery funds. 
The Board emphasized that the funds should produce tangible economic benefit for Josephine County, particularly job 
creation and infrastructure that supports economic activity, rather than primarily funding studies. State requirements for 
the lottery funds were described as broad, allowing the County to develop more specific local priorities. Because of 
recruitment delays, the committee process may extend into November, with a goal of completing the current cycle before 
year-end and bringing recommendations to the Board early next year for the following fiscal year. Mr. Eide will return 
with proposed membership and rule adjustments as needed. 
3. Board Business 
a. Association of O&C Counties (AOCC) Dues 
The Board discussed whether Josephine County should rejoin the Association of O&C Counties (AOCC) and the value of 
the Association's federal advocacy, legal and policy work, and access to information regarding O&C lands and federal 
timber revenue. 
Commissioners discussed the County's dependence on federally controlled lands and the importance of coordinated 
advocacy in Washington, D.C. Commissioner Richardson requested additional financial information before committing to 
membership, including the Association's budget and expenditures. Because AOCC dues were not included in the adopted 
County budget, the Board also discussed whether dues could be phased in, prorated, or paid in installments rather than as 
one annual payment. 
The Board directed Chair Smith to follow up with Doug Roberts of the Association of O&C Counties and with Legal 
Counsel Wally Hicks regarding AOCC budget information and options for a phased or installment payment of 
membership dues. No formal action was taken on membership. 
b. COCONUT - Automation Opportunity Sweep 
Tracy Thompson presented a proposal titled COCONUT - Automation Opportunity Sweep, describing it as a government-
efficiency assessment in which automation and artificial intelligence could be used as tools to identify process 
improvements. 
Mr. Thompson proposed assessing one to three County departments by interviewing staff, reviewing workflows and 
standard operating procedures, identifying performance measures and bottlenecks, and developing recommendations for 
measurable efficiencies. He stated that the assessment was not intended to reduce County staffing, but to reduce repetitive 
work and allow existing employees to focus on higher-value tasks. He described two possible pricing approaches: an 
approximately $10,000 up-front assessment for up to three departments, or an assessment performed at his own expense 
with an executive summary provided to the County and a $15,000 charge only if the Board chose to purchase the detailed 
findings and implementation roadmap. 
The Board questioned how County data, systems, intellectual property, confidentiality, and long-term ownership would be 
handled; how the proposed assessment would interact with existing departmental software; what staff time would be 
required; and what comparable work Mr. Thompson had previously completed. Mr. Thompson stated that County data 
and systems would remain County property, while his underlying methodologies would remain proprietary. He 
acknowledged that he had not previously implemented this exact system in an organization comparable in size and 
structure to Josephine County, although he cited his prior scientific, business, and process-optimization experience.

Information Technology staff advised that the County is already evaluating departmental processes and testing automation
