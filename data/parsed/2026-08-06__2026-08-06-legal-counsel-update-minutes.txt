2. Solid Waste Nuisance Abatement Litigation Authorization
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel; Janet Fredrickson, Public Health; and Thomas Peterson, Solid Waste, 
presented a long-standing solid waste nuisance involving an absentee property owner. Staff reported that the 
property had been monitored since 2019 and that repeated efforts to obtain voluntary compliance had not 
produced lasting corrections. Staff stated that the number of occupants and vehicles had increased 
substantially. The narrow roadway was frequently obstructed, neighboring properties had experienced theft 
and continuous traffic, and approximately a dozen individuals appeared to be occupying the property. Staff 
also documented damage to the creek and surrounding riparian area, including the use of household cleaners 
and detergents, removal of water for bathing, and discharge of heated wastewater into low-flow salmon 
habitat. Legal Counsel explained that the County could initiate an enforcement action seeking a court order 
appointing the County as receiver of the property. A receivership would authorize the County to remove the 
occupants, secure and clean the property, and bill the property owner for the abatement costs. Staff advised 
that the owner had repeatedly failed to secure the property or resolve the violations. The Board approved 
proceeding with the solid waste nuisance abatement litigation without objection. Staff will initiate the 
enforcement process, keep the Board and affected neighbors informed, and provide public information 
concerning the abatement effort as appropriate.  
3. Dissolution of the Road District
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, explained that the Road District had been created after the Oregon Legislature 
authorized counties to establish separate road districts in an effort to qualify for additional Secure Rural 
Schools funding. The federal Department of the Interior subsequently declined to recognize the district as a 
separate recipient and did not increase the County's payment. Because the Road District was not producing 
the intended financial benefit and continued to create administrative obligations, Legal Counsel 
recommended initiating the formal dissolution process. The Board approved proceeding with dissolution 
without objection. The formal dissolution documents will return to the Board for consideration. 
4. Opportunity to Intervene - Jewell School District v. Oregon Department of Forestry et al.;
Clatsop County Case No. 24CV13514
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, reviewed the history of county trust lands managed by the State of Oregon. He 
explained that counties had transferred tax-foreclosed forestlands to the State with the expectation that the 
lands would be managed and harvested for the benefit of the counties and their local taxing districts. A prior 
trust-lands lawsuit resulted in a substantial verdict that was later overturned on appeal. Counsel reported that 
attorney John DiLorenzo was representing the Jewell School District in new litigation and had invited 
affected counties to participate in confidential discussions regarding the case and possible intervention or 
support. Josephine County has a comparatively small interest in the trust lands, but local school districts and 
other taxing districts could receive some benefit from a successful claim. The Board directed Legal Counsel 
to participate in the upcoming informational discussions, evaluate the potential benefits and obligations of 
involvement, and report back to the Board before any commitment was made.

5. Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention Five-Year Local Option Tax 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel; Matt Eide, Finance Director; Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director; and 
Sheriff Dave Daniel discussed proposed ballot language for the local option tax supporting the adult jail and 
juvenile detention. Legal Counsel noted that the final measure documents were due to the County Clerk the 
following week and reviewed language used in the 2017 and 2021 measures. The Board initially considered 
retaining the existing rate of up to $0.93 per $1,000 of assessed value while removing fixed percentage 
allocations between the adult jail and juvenile detention. Commissioners emphasized that the public had 
expressed support for maintaining the current rate rather than increasing taxes and that the County needed 
flexibility to respond to changing revenues and public-safety needs. Finance reported that levy receipts had 
been sufficient to support the affected programs without general-fund support during the current levy 
period. Recent expenditures had remained below collections, in part because of unfilled positions, but 
inflation, wage growth, and successful recruitment could reduce that cushion during the later years of a new 
levy. Mr. Hyde explained that Juvenile Justice requires approximately $2.9 million annually and has limited 
ability to reduce costs because state law requires minimum staffing ratios regardless of the number of youth 
housed. He described the seriousness of the offenses associated with youth currently in custody and stated 
that other counties had very limited detention capacity available for contract use. The Board affirmed the 
importance of juvenile detention and rehabilitation to the County's overall public-safety system. The Board 
discussed whether a combined $0.93 measure could accurately promise continued operation of both the 
adult jail and juvenile detention at existing service levels for the full five-year term. Participants expressed 
concern that allocating only a small portion of the levy to Juvenile Justice while promising both programs 
could create an obligation the County might not be able to sustain from the General Fund. After extensive 
discussion, the Board directed Legal Counsel to prepare a stand-alone adult jail measure at approximately 
$0.89 per $1,000 of assessed value. The proposed measure would replace the adult-jail portion of the 
existing levy and support capacity for up to 185 inmates and the intake of persons arrested in Josephine 
County. Juvenile Justice would be addressed through existing funds in the near term while the County 
develops a separate, longer-term funding strategy and public-information effort. Legal Counsel will return 
with revised language. The Board also requested a separate General Discussion meeting and public input at 
the upcoming evening Weekly Business Session before making a final decision. 
6. Personnel Policy Revision: Article 8, Section 1 - Travel 
Wally Hicks and Leah Harper, Legal Counsel, presented options for revising the County travel policy. The 
existing language required Finance approval for department-head travel, including routine in-state business, 
and had created unnecessary administrative delays. The Board discussed distinguishing routine same-day 
travel from overnight travel and allowing recurring professional-association meetings to receive advance 
authorization. Scott Hyde described regularly scheduled meetings of the Oregon Association of Community 
Corrections Directors and requested a process allowing the Board to approve recurring overnight meetings 
in advance. The Board directed Legal Counsel to revise Option 2 by adding an overnight-travel requirement. 
Department heads will retain authority to approve travel for employees within their departments. 
Department heads will obtain Finance approval for overnight travel unless the Board has provided advance 
authorization for recurring meetings. Legal Counsel will prepare the revision for adoption by Board Order.
