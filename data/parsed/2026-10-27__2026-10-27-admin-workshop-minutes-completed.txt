c. Forestry 
1. Tree Planting Contract 2026PL-3 with Rogue River Forestry LLC – Approximately 
173,500 seedlings on 422 acres at $460–$520 per thousand (estimated $79,488) – Approve 
contract and authorize Dave Streeter to sign 
Dave Streeter, Forestry Director, presented Tree Planting Contract 2026PL-3 with Rogue River Forestry 
LLC for planting approximately 173,500 conifer seedlings on 422 acres in nine units of County forest 
lands during the fall 2026 and winter 2027 planting seasons, at bid prices of $460 to $520 per thousand 
seedlings, an estimated $79,488. Mr. Streeter explained that reforestation is a routine part of County 
forest management and that trees are planted at a higher density than the eventual stand because not all 
seedlings survive. Forestry staff conduct quality-control checks while planting is underway and later 
perform stocking surveys to evaluate survival and contractor performance. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve Tree Planting Contract 2026PL-3 with Rogue River Forestry 
LLC and authorize Dave Streeter to sign. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: 
Commissioner Richardson – Yes; Commissioner Martin – Yes; Commissioner Smith – Yes. Motion 
carried 3-0. 
d. Sheriff 
1. Purchase of five (5) 2026 Chevrolet Tahoe patrol vehicles for new deputy positions – 
OregonBuys Contract PO-107-00012917 – $287,649.40 plus $130,000 upfitting, from the 
Sheriff’s Equipment Reserve Fund – Approval 
Sheriff Dave Daniel presented the request to purchase five 2026 Chevrolet Tahoe patrol vehicles for the 
patrol deputy positions added in the 2026-27 budget, through OregonBuys Contract PO-107-00012917, at 
$57,529.88 each for a total of $287,649.40, plus approximately $130,000 for upfitting, funded from the 
Sheriff's Equipment Reserve Fund. Sheriff Daniel stated that the Tahoes were selected for their four-
wheel-drive capability, size, durability, and officer-safety advantages, and that he intends to transition the 
patrol fleet back toward Tahoes as vehicles become available. Because the Sheriff's Office has added 
positions, the five vehicles are additions to the fleet rather than immediate replacements; future vehicle 
purchases are expected to begin replacing older units. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the purchase of five 2026 Chevrolet Tahoe patrol vehicles under 
OregonBuys Contract PO-107-00012917 for $287,649.40, plus $130,000 for upfitting, from the Sheriff’s 
Equipment Reserve Fund. Commissioner Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner 
Richardson – Yes; Commissioner Martin – Yes; Commissioner Smith – Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
2. Fund 26 Animal Control Budget – Reduce contingency by $27,400 to increase personnel 
($10,100, 10% COLA) and vehicle expense ($17,300) – Approval 
Sheriff Dave Daniel presented the request to reduce Fund 26 contingency by $27,400 to increase the 
Animal Control Division personnel budget by $10,100, to $398,900, and the vehicle expense budget by 
$17,300, to $72,400. The personnel adjustment reflects the Sheriff's Association collective bargaining 
agreement, which was finalized after the budget was prepared. The vehicle adjustment reflects both 
higher fuel prices than budgeted and increased mileage, with the three Animal Control vehicles averaging 
approximately 6,100 miles per month compared with the approximately 4,500 miles per month originally 
budgeted. Sheriff Daniel also reported that Animal Control calls for service have continued to increase. 
The requested adjustments are funded from the existing Fund 26 contingency. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to approve reducing Fund 26 contingency by $27,400 to increase the 
Animal Control Division personnel budget by $10,100 and vehicle expense budget by $17,300. 
Commissioner Martin seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson – Yes; 
Commissioner Martin – Yes; Commissioner Smith – Yes. Motion carried 3-0.

3. Portable Radio Purchase – Portable radios for the new patrol deputy positions added in the 
2026-27 budget; the budgeted APX6000 model versus a newer model compatible with an 
upgraded radio system at a higher cost – Discussion 
Sheriff Dave Daniel discussed portable radios for the new patrol deputy positions. The budget assumed 
purchase of APX6000 radios; however, information received from Day Wireless indicated that the

broader communications-system plan. The item will return to a future Administrative Workshop.

b. Critical Oregon Airport Relief (COAR) Grant Program – Oregon Department of Aviation 
(ODAV) – Discussion 
Commissioner Martin and Mike Crisafulli, Airports Manager, discussed the Critical Oregon Airport 
Relief (COAR) grant program administered by the Oregon Department of Aviation. The program 
includes funding priorities for FAA grant match, Cascadia/emergency preparedness, and economic 
development. The proposed Illinois Valley Airport application would seek approximately $150,000 for 
economic-development infrastructure and would initially focus on developing four of the airport's 
sixteen industrial lots, including water and wastewater needs. Existing wells are low-producing and 
require evaluation, and above-ground water storage was discussed as a possible alternative for fire-
suppression needs. The grant application was due Friday. A grant of $150,000 would require an 
approximately ten-percent local match, bringing the potential project commitment to approximately 
$166,667; any final local-match commitment or grant acceptance would return to the Board as 
necessary. 
Without objection, the Board authorized staff to proceed with the COAR grant application.

Commissioner Richardson presented a proposed 90-Day Airport Lease Modernization Program intended 
to accelerate the review and updating of airport leases. Commissioner Richardson and Mike Crisafulli, 
Airports Manager, discussed the different lease types, revised templates, historical lease terms that 
require individual review, and the number of leases still needing work. Mr. Crisafulli advised that 
approximately 82 leases require some level of review or correction and that the work must account for 
prior agreements, leasehold rights, rate consistency, insurance requirements, and other property-specific 
history rather than simply replacing every lease with the same form. The Board also discussed use of the 
Re-Leased property-management software, which is currently being used primarily as a document 
repository but has additional capabilities for lease tracking, notices, payments, and reporting. The Board 
emphasized the need to move the lease work forward while maintaining consistent and defensible terms 
for airport tenants. 
No formal action was taken. 
Commissioner Richardson and Mike Crisafulli will return to the Administrative Workshop in two weeks 
with an adjusted implementation plan, including proposed goals and timelines for lease cleanup and use 
of the Re-Leased system.
