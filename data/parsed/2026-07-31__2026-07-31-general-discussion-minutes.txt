2. SRS Election Allocation 
Legal Counsel Wally Hicks explained that the Oregon Department of Administrative Services 
notified counties that July 31 was the deadline to reconsider their annual election between 
Secure Rural Schools funding and federal timber receipts. The Board had previously elected 
to receive Secure Rural Schools funding for the upcoming year. The purpose of the meeting 
was to review newly available information and confirm whether the Board wished to retain or 
change that election. 
Leah Harper provided background on the Secure Rural Schools program. Congress established 
the program in 2000 after reduced timber harvesting on federal lands substantially decreased 
the timber receipts historically paid to counties in lieu of property taxes. The program provides 
counties with a formula-based payment while allowing an annual election between Secure 
Rural Schools funding and the applicable federal timber-receipt calculation. 
Ms. Harper explained that Secure Rural Schools has been reauthorized multiple times and that 
counties have frequently faced uncertainty about whether Congress would continue the 
program. Participants understood the current authorization to extend through federal Fiscal 
Year 2026 and that additional funding beyond that period would require further congressional 
action. The election under consideration applied for one year and could be reconsidered during 
the following annual cycle. 
The Board discussed changes in how Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management O&C 
acreage is treated. The acreage is now combined for purposes of the election, and a county may 
not receive both Secure Rural Schools payments and timber receipts based upon the same 
federal acreage. The timing of timber-receipt payments may also lag one or more years after a 
harvest because sales must be advertised, awarded, completed, and paid before revenues are 
distributed to counties. 
Finance Director Matt Eide reviewed the timing of prior federal payments and noted that 
delayed receipts can create a decrease in one fiscal year followed by an increase in a later year. 
The figures presented to the Board indicated that even if projected timber receipts increased 
substantially, the estimated amount for Josephine County remained below the known Secure 
Rural Schools payment.

Mr. Hicks reported that the Bureau of Land Management had adopted a resource-management 
plan targeting approximately 500 million board feet of annual harvest, which represented a 
significant increase over recent levels. However, the County could not assume that the full 
projected harvest would immediately occur or generate receipts during the applicable election 
year. Timber-sale preparation, market conditions, available milling capacity, transportation 
expenses, and potential litigation could delay or reduce the anticipated revenue. 
The Board also discussed a recent assumption that removal of a federal 25-percent surcharge 
would increase the counties' share of O&C receipts from 50 percent to 75 percent. Mr. Hicks 
reported that Senator Wyden's office had confirmed with the Department of the Interior that 
the payment rate would remain at 50 percent because the governing Secure Rural Schools 
legislation had not been amended. 
Commissioners discussed reports that Douglas and Jackson Counties intended to elect timber 
receipts. Those counties hold larger amounts of O&C land and may have different financial, 
legal, or economic considerations. The Board noted that Josephine County's circumstances 
differed, including the comparative size of its anticipated payment, uncertainty surrounding 
federal harvest levels, the loss of local milling infrastructure, and the present timber and 
housing markets. 
Commissioners further discussed that litigation has historically prevented federal agencies 
from reaching announced timber-harvest targets. Although increased timber production could 
benefit the regional economy and provide future county revenue, the amount and timing of 
those receipts remained uncertain. Secure Rural Schools offered a known amount that could 
be used for County planning and budgeting. 
Following discussion, Commissioners agreed that the newly available information did not 
justify changing the County's prior election. The Board reached consensus to retain its election 
of Secure Rural Schools funding for the applicable one-year period and to review the available 
options again during the next annual election cycle. No additional formal action was taken.
