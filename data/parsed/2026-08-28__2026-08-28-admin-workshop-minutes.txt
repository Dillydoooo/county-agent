APPROVED ON SEPTEMBER 2, 2026 
BY THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS 
AT THE WEEKLY BUSINESS SESSION 
County Administration Workshop: August 28, 2026 
1:00 p.m. - Board of Commissioners Conference Room 
Attending: Commissioners Ron A. Smith; Colene M. Martin; Gary L. Richardson; and Nikole Guzman, Recorder 
Chair Ron Smith called the meeting to order at 1:00 p.m. 
1. Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest 
The Board declared there were no potential conflicts of interest. 
2. Department Business 
a. Finance 
1. Proposed Fee increases for District Attorney and Recreation 
Matthew Eide, Finance Director, reviewed proposed fee changes. The Recreation/Fair fees were tabled for additional 
consideration and will return through the broader annual fee-review process. Finance proceeded with the District Attorney 
proposal because the office is preparing to implement Evidence.com. District Attorney fees were last adjusted in 2016; the 
proposed changes are generally about 20 percent and are intended to bring charges in line with the current cost of service. 
Obsolete video-cassette and audio-tape fees would be discontinued as records and evidence move to digital delivery. 
The Board supported the District Attorney’s modernization efforts and discussed reviewing departmental fees more 
regularly so future changes can be smaller and more predictable. Finance confirmed the proposal is cost-recovery, not 
revenue-generating. The Board requested a consistent Countywide format showing current fees, proposed fees and 
percentage changes. Finance outlined public notices for September 2 and 9, a public hearing at the September 16, 2026 
Weekly Business Session, and an anticipated September 21 effective date if adopted. Without objection, the Board 
directed Finance to proceed with notice and bring the proposal forward for public hearing and formal action. 
3. Board Business 
a. Troutbeck Townhomes Middle Housing Land Division 
The Board considered the Troutbeck Townhomes Middle Housing Land Division. The map had been available for 
public review in the Board’s Office for several days. A minor wording or labeling revision was made to the drawings 
during the review period, which the Board stated did not materially alter the development. Commissioners noted the 
project would add needed housing. Commissioner Richardson made a motion to approve the Troutbeck Townhomes 
Middle Housing Land Division. Commissioner Martin seconded. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; 
Commissioner Martin - Yes; Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
b. COCONUT - Automation Opportunity Sweep 
The Board revisited the COCONUT automation opportunity proposal. Commissioners supported modernization and 
efficiency efforts generally but expressed concern about imposing an outside assessment on short-staffed departments 
that are already modernizing at their own pace. The Board emphasized the need for departmental buy-in, noted ongoing 
County AI-policy and modernization work, and raised concerns about protecting confidential or sensitive information. 
The Board expressed appreciation for the proposal but reached consensus that the County is not ready to proceed with 
COCONUT at this time and may revisit the concept later. No formal action was taken. 
 
4. Other: (ORS 192.640(1)...Commissioners may introduce other matters for consideration by the entire Board) 
No additional matters were introduced for consideration. 
 
5. Additional Subject Matters from Commissioners/Announcements 
Commissioner Richardson reported that seven departments had reached 100 percent employee-benefits enrollment: 
Emergency Management, Finance, Veterans Service Office, Legal Counsel, Surveyor, Treasurer and the Board of 
County Commissioners. Public Works was recognized for reaching 77 percent. Commissioner Martin reminded the 
public of the Whitehorse Park ribbon cutting Wednesday at 11:00 a.m. and recognized the Parks Department for the 
improvements. Chair Smith commended firefighters and the Sheriff’s Office for their response to local fires and 
reminded the public that fire danger remains until fall rain arrives.  
The meeting adjourned at 1:13 p.m.
