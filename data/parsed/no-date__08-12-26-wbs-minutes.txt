3. ADMINISTRATIVE ACTION(S) IN CONSIDERATION OF: 
a. Resolution No. 2026-031; In the Matter of Referring to Josephine County Voters at the November 3, 2026, 
Election a Measure for Renewal of Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention Five-Year Local Option Tax 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, presented the proposed five-year renewal of the existing Adult Jail and Juvenile 
Detention local option tax. He explained that voters first approved the levy in 20 l 7 and renewed it in 2021, and 
that the current levy will expire June 30, 2027. The proposed renewal would retain the existing rate of $0.93 per 
$1,000 of assessed value for another five years. 
Mr. Hicks explained that the proposal would maintain the Adult Jail's current inmate capacity of 185 and allow 
intake of adults arrested in Josephine County. Remaining levy revenue would be available to partially fund Juvenile 
Detention operations and related administrative expenses. He noted that Josephine County's permanent property 
tax rate is $0.585 per $1,000 of assessed value and that the local option levy is in addition to that permanent rate. 
At the proposed $0.93 rate, the tax would equal approximately $7.75 per month, or $93 per year, for each $100,000 
ofassessed value. For a home with the County's cited average assessed value of $239,908, the estimated cost would 
be approximately $18.59 per month, or $223 .11 per year. Mr. Hicks stated that the deadline to submit the measure 
to the County Clerk for the November election was Friday, August 14, 2026. 
4. REQUESTS/COMMENTS FROM CITIZENS: 
(Each person was given three (3) minutes to speak.) 
Herman Baertreger, Josephine County, stated that he supports funding both the Adult Jail and Juvenile Justice but 
opposed the proposed ballot language. He compared the proposed measure with the 2017 and 2021 levies and 
expressed concern that the current draft no longer assured funding for 14 juvenile detention beds. He also questioned 
the phrase "remaining revenue, if any" and asked for clearer language identifying how taxpayer funds would be 
allocated. 
Victor, Grants Pass, questioned the cost and utilization of the Adult Jail and Juvenile Justice facilities and urged the 
County to consider the District Attorney as part of the broader public-safety funding structure. He also supported 
continued exploration of artificial intelligence and the COCONUT government-efficiency proposal discussed at the 
prior Administrative Workshop. 
Adriana Rhinehart, Josephine County, identified herself as a former Josephine County prosecutor and current defense 
attorney who primarily represents juveniles. She stated that juveniles cannot be housed with adults, emphasized the 
importance of early intervention and local juvenile detention capacity, and urged the Board to support the levy while 
strengthening the language to provide more definite funding for Juvenile Justice. 
T.J. Tice, Josephine County, described a July vehicle crash that caused substantial damage to his business property 
and expressed frustration with the criminal case that followed. He stated that the experience reinforced his support 
for adequate law-enforcement, prosecution, and jail funding.

BOARD MAY REVIEW PUBLIC COMMENTS/QUESTIONS: 
Commissioner Richardson made a motion to approve Resolution No. 2026-031; In the Matter of Referring to 
Josephine County Voters at the November 3, 2026, Election a Measure for Renewal of Adult Jail and Juvenile 
Detention Five-Year Local Option Tax. Commissioner Martin seconded the motion. 
During discussion on the pending motion, Commissioner Martin proposed a friendly amendment to remove the 
words "if any" from the Measure Funding paragraph so the sentence would begin, "Remaining revenue from this 
measure would partially fund juvenile detention operations ... " Commissioner Smith seconded the amendment, 
and the amendment was incorporated into the pending motion. 
Mr. Hicks advised that removing the words "if any" would not materially change the practical application of the 
measure because the remaining language provides that Juvenile Detention operations could continue at the current 
service level subject to the availability of other funds, and the annual budget process would determine the amount 
of remaining levy revenue available in each year. The Board also discussed language stating that the Juvenile 
Detention Center can currently lodge up to 14 youth and reiterated the importance of maintaining the service. 
Upon roll call on the motion as amended: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; 
Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0.
