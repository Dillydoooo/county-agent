2. (For 10/7 WBS) Administrative Policy Revision: F-01; Social Media Policy 
Wally Hicks, Legal Counsel, introduced the proposed revisions to Administrative Policy F-
01, Social Media Policy. Thomas Gray, Public Information Officer, explained that the 
existing policy no longer fully reflected current County practices or developments in 
communications and First Amendment law. The proposed revisions clarify that County 
social media accounts are for official County business, that personal accounts should not be 
used for official County business, and that social-media records must be maintained for 
public-records purposes. The revisions also establish clearer standards for public comments, 
including treatment of scams, explicit material, spam and automated accounts while 
protecting lawful criticism and other protected speech. 
Gray explained that decisions to remove comments or block users would be centralized with 
the Information Technology Director, with input from the Public Information Officer and 
Legal Counsel, to promote consistent application of the policy. The Board discussed staff 
workload, whether comments may be disabled on some accounts, repeated rule violations, 
bot activity, and employee compliance with County social-media rules. Gray noted that 
County accounts are not monitored 24 hours a day and that disciplinary issues involving 
employees remain governed by the personnel policy. The Board reached consensus to place 
Administrative Policy F-01 on the October 7, 2026 Weekly Business Session agenda, with 
Gray to provide an overview. 
3. (For 10/7 WBS) Administrative Policy Revision: F-06; Public Records Policy 
Hicks and Gray presented the proposed revision to Administrative Policy F-06, Public 
Records Policy. Gray stated that the revisions were intended to bring the policy into 
alignment with current law and County practice and to create a consistent process for public-
records requests. The draft was reviewed with Oregon Public Records Advocate Todd 
Albert, and his recommendations were incorporated. The policy clarifies the distinction 
between records administered through the Board's designated Records Management Officer 
and records for which an independently elected County official acts as custodian, including 
the different appeal paths that may apply. 
The revised policy also standardizes intake procedures by requiring use of a County public-
records request form and identifying allowable methods and locations for submitting

4. Josephine County Strategic Communications Plan 
Gray presented the Josephine County Strategic Communications Plan and recommended 
that it be maintained as an Information Technology departmental policy rather than adopted 
as a formal Board policy. He explained that the plan is intended to be a living document that 
can be updated as communication needs change, while still returning to the Board for 
periodic review and at least annual reporting. The plan includes future development of 
crisis-communications procedures and allows staff to adapt tactics when immediate 
communication needs arise. 
The Board discussed program costs, public benefit, accessibility, and how the County would 
reach residents who do not use social media or have reliable internet access. Gray said 
current maintenance costs are within the IT budget and estimated a future communications 
budget in the general range of $30,000 to $50,000 annually as the program develops. He 
identified public-records compliance, emergency communication, promotion of County 
programs and services, print materials, in-person outreach, translations, and measurable 
communication metrics as components of the plan. The Board approved the Strategic 
Communications Plan without objection as a living departmental policy under Information 
Technology. 
5. Departmental Policy - Board of County Commissioners Direct Reports 
Hicks and Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe, Interim Human Resources Director, presented a 
proposed process for personnel matters involving employees who report directly to the 
Board. Eckhardt-Doupe explained that the purpose is to create a predictable procedure that 
can be followed regardless of who is serving as Human Resources Director, particularly 
because Public Meetings Law affects how personnel matters involving direct Board reports 
must be handled. 
The Board discussed whether the document should be characterized as a policy or procedure 
and where it should reside. Consensus favored Human Resources maintaining the procedure 
as the single authoritative source, with the Personnel Policy Manual amended to reference 
the separate procedure. The Board also discussed making the procedure publicly accessible 
and maintaining Human Resources' ability to consult labor counsel regarding 
implementation. The Board reached consensus to proceed with the process. Hicks indicated 
he would prepare a formal amendment to the Personnel Policy Manual referencing the 
procedure; no separate formal motion was taken.

Hicks presented a draft resolution concerning County compliance with state and federal law 
and potential federal-funding and False Claims Act exposure when state and federal 
requirements conflict. The Board discussed the effect of Oregon laws concerning 
immigration and sanctuary policies, federal grant certifications, and the concern that 
counties could be placed between inconsistent state and federal requirements. Hicks 
explained that the proposed resolution would be non-binding and would serve to formally 
notify state and federal officials of the County's concerns. 
Commissioner Martin discussed a similar resolution considered in Douglas County and 
raised concern about protecting opportunities for federal grant funding, including funding 
sought for the County's communications system. The Board agreed that the draft needed 
additional review and revision before adoption. Legal Counsel was directed to continue 
refining the language and bring the revised matter to a Weekly Business Session for public 
discussion before any final action.
