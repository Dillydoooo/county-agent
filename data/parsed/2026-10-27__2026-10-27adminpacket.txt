3. Compliance with DOJ regulations pertaining to civil rights and nondiscrimination - 28 C.F.R. Part 38 
 
The subrecipient, and any subrecipient ("subgrantee") at any tier, must comply with all applicable requirements 
of 28 C.F.R. Part 38. 
 
Among other things, 28 C.F.R. Part 38 states that recipients may not use direct Federal financial assistance from 
the Department of Justice to support or engage in any explicitly religious activities except when consistent with 
the Establishment Clause of the First Amendment to the U.S. Constitution and any other applicable 
requirements.  An organization receiving Federal financial assistance also may not, in providing services funded 
by the Department of Justice or in outreach activities related to such services, discriminate against a program 
beneficiary or prospective program beneficiary on the basis of religion, a religious belief, a refusal to hold a 
religious belief, or refusal to attend or participate in a religious practice.  Part 38 also sets out rules and 
requirements that pertain to subrecipient organizations that engage in or conduct explicitly religious activities, as 
well as rules and requirements that pertain to subrecipients that are faith-based or religious organizations.  In 
addition, Part 38 states that a faith-based organization that participates in a Department of Justice funded 
program retains its independence from the Government and may continue to carry out its mission consistent 
with religious freedom and conscience protections in Federal law.  
 
Subrecipients that provide social services under this award must give written notice to beneficiaries and 
prospective beneficiaries prior to the provision of services (if practicable) which shall include language 
substantially similar to the language in 28 CFR Part 38, Appendix C, sections (1) through (4). A sample written 
notice may be found at https://www.ojp.gov/program/civil-rights-office/partnerships-faith-based-and-other-
neighborhood-organizations.

17. Reclassification of various statutory provisions to a new Title 34 of the United States Code 
 
On September 1, 2017, various statutory provisions previously codified elsewhere in the U.S. Code were 
editorially reclassified (that is, moved and renumbered) to a new Title 34, entitled "Crime Control and Law 
Enforcement." The reclassification encompassed a number of statutory provisions pertinent to OJP awards (that 
is, OJP grants and cooperative agreements), including many provisions previously codified in Title 42 of the 
U.S. Code. 
 
Effective as of September 1, 2017, any reference in this award document to a statutory provision that has been 
reclassified to the new Title 34 of the U.S. Code is to be read as a reference to that statutory provision as 
reclassified to Title 34. This rule of construction specifically includes references set out in award conditions, 
references set out in material incorporated by reference through award conditions, and references set out in other 
award requirements.

EXHIBIT B
Planting Specifications
(1)
Acceptable Planting Spot:  A spot where the mineral soil is at least 12 inches in depth. 
(2)
Unacceptable Planting Spot: A spot where there is little or no mineral soil. (i.e.,  > 12” 
duff, ground in slash, > 12” burn pile ash, rocky ravel with very little soil) Seedlings will 
not be planted in organic matter, in areas of persistent standing water, in glades, on 
top of mounded soil, or above a cavity such as a burned out root system.
(3)
Seedlings will be firmly planted into mineral soil to provide a sturdy base for the tree 
and promote good root contact with the soil. Loose or high trees are unacceptable. 
Planted seedlings will show no displacement when given a firm pull. 
(4)
Seedling stem and roots system are to be planted in a vertical upright position.  No 
slip planting.  Roots will be planted straight and firmly packed in soil. Roots will not be 
“Balled up” or “J-rooted”.
(5)
Seedling root systems are to be planted in a near natural configuration. The planting 
hole must be wide and deep enough to ensure full distribution of the root system 
within the soil.
(6)
Container seedlings will be planted 1-1½ inches deeper than the container soil level. 
Bareroot seedlings will be planted 1 inch deeper than nursery root collar.
(7)
Spacing 10’ x 10’, or as *otherwise specified, will be carefully adhered to. 
Planters will space off natural and previously planted conifer between 1 and 6 feet in 
height, the assigned spacing distance (i.e., 10’ if assigned 10’ x 10’ spacing). Planters 
will space off natural conifer over 6 feet in height so that the seedling is planted 
beyond the drip line and at least 12 feet from the main stem of that natural conifer. 
When planting in small openings bordered 
by taller natural conifer, planters will 
need “open space” between the drip lines of those bordering trees in order to plant a 
seedling.
(8)
Roads: Road fill spacing will be 6’ horizontal distance below the edge of the road 
running surface. There will be no planting of any road cuts.
(9)
Draws: Any draw that shows scour from running water will need a spacing of 6 feet 
from the high water mark before planting a seedling.
(10)
Microsite planting behind logs, stumps, etc. is encouraged.
(11)
Every planting spot shall be prepared with a scalp removing all slash, duff, red rot, 
ash, rocky ravel, grass and weeds will be scalped cleanly in a 12”x12” square area 
before planting a seedling in the center of the scalp. Ravel, red rot and ash may need 
to be scalped to a depth of 12 inches before mineral soil is reached and a seedling 
can be planted.

Seedling Handling
(1)
The Contractor will be responsible for transporting seedlings to the planting site and 
distributing seedlings within the planting site.  All terrain vehicles may be required in 
some areas with unimproved roads.  All seedlings shall be kept cool during transport 
and during storage at the planting site. Planters will shade their bagged up seedlings 
while on break.
(2)
A single seedling will be removed from the planting bag after the planting hole has been 
prepared.  Planters will not hand carry seedlings from one planting spot to another or 
to another planter.
(3)
All bareroot seedlings will be dipped in a B vitamin and water solution before bagging up 
to help maintain root moisture and reduce planting shock.  Contractor will provide the 
water for dipping.  The County will furnish the B vitamin additive.
(4)
No more seedlings than can reasonably be planted for the day are to be removed from 
the storage cooler. Unplanted seedlings will be returned to cooler storage by the 
Contractor at the end of the day.
(5)
The following conditions will be considered unacceptable:  a) Any rough handling of a 
tree bag or box. (Example throwing bags out of truck) b) Thrashing of a seedlings root 
system for any reason. c) Tearing or pruning of seedling root system.
Additional Requirements
(1)
There must be a concerted effort by planters to push planting from bare soil areas into 
areas of brush, slash and duff within the planting unit.
(2)
Contractor should keep an accurate daily planting record.  Records should include 
date, planting unit location and the number of seedlings planted from each seedlot. 
Contractor’s daily records and planting inspector daily records should be checked for 
agreement at the end of each day.

Commissioner Proposal for Public Meeting
Proposal: The 90-Day Airport Lease Modernization Program
1.  Requested Board action
Commissioner Richardson requests that the Board direct the Department of Airports to implement the 90-Day 
Airport Lease Modernization Program on the schedule in Section 4; adopt the Section 4 milestones as Board 
deadlines; require a written progress report at each regular Board meeting until completion; designate 
Commissioner Richardson to oversee implementation on the Board's behalf; and affirm that the Commissioners' 
office will provide additional resources as appropriate. Suggested motion language is in Section 9.
2.  Background
Josephine County operates the Grants Pass Airport (3S8) and the Illinois Valley Airport (3S4) as enterprise 
activities funded by airport revenue, not general tax dollars. The airports hold more than 150 ground and hangar 
leases. A record-by-record review completed in October 2026, cross-checking the County's lease software 
against County rate files and executed lease documents, found 42 leases whose written terms expired as far 
back as 2014; 133 leases without a current insurance certificate; 133 overdue cost-of-living rent reviews dating 
to October 2025; hangars bought and sold over the years without assignments or updated leases; 33 
placeholder commencement dates; and several direct conflicts between County systems. Current collections are 
approximately $562,000 per year.
These conditions accumulated over roughly a decade and have been documented for the department. Routine 
departmental processes have not cleared the backlog. The purpose of this proposal is to convert the work from 
an open-ended task into a defined program with Board-adopted deadlines, public reporting, and direct Board 
oversight. Two recent leases, a January 2026 commercial ground lease that recovered three years of back rent 
and an October 2026 commercial ground lease signed at rates above the validated market average, demonstrate 
that the County's standard documents and rate methodology work when applied.
3.  Why 90 days is realistic
The Board may hear that a backlog a decade in the making requires years to resolve, or that airport operations 
leave no time for it. Neither holds on examination:
• The preparation is finished.  The lease-by-lease inventory, priority tiers, target dates, verification packets, 
and mail-merge files are already built from County records. What remains is execution.
• The work is batchable.  Nearly all 154 leases take one of four counsel-approved forms, processed in 
batches. Only a handful of commercial renewals require individual negotiation, and each has its own track 
and deadline.
• Tenants do the research.  The verification form asks each leaseholder to confirm or correct the County's 
record and supply insurance; 153 of 154 are reachable by email today.
• The timing fits operations.  November through January is the airports' slowest season. The program is 
deliberately scheduled for winter so it does not compete with summer flying activity.
• Delay has a price.  Lost revenue for every month the work remains undone.
The decade explains how the backlog accumulated. It does not govern how quickly the backlog can be cleared.

5.  Oversight and reporting
• A written progress report from the Airports Manager at each regular Board meeting, consisting of five 
numbers from the program dashboard: packets sent, responses received, documents signed, leases 
recorded current in the system, and insurance certificates on file.
• Weekly implementation review between Commissioner [COMMISSIONER NAME] and the Airports Manager.
• Any missed milestone requires a written statement of cause and a recovery plan at the next regular meeting.
• The lease remediation tracker (Attachment C) is the single source of record for program status.
6.  Fiscal impact
• Costs.  No new ongoing costs. The program runs primarily on existing staff, standardized documents, and 
lease-management software the County already owns. The Commissioners' office will provide additional 
resources as appropriate to complete the one-time catch-up.
• Revenue.  Executing the overdue CPI reviews restores approximately $19,700 per year under existing leases 
and adopted policy. A 2026 study of twelve comparable Oregon airports identifies a further path to 
approximately $96,000 per year as expired commercial ground leases renew at current adopted rates. All 
revenue remains in the airport enterprise fund.
• Grant protection.  Accurate records and consistently applied rates support compliance with FAA grant 
assurances, including self-sustainability and nondiscriminatory rates, protecting eligibility for federal runway 
and safety grants.
7.  Legal and policy considerations
County Counsel approves the four form documents before any mailing. Private hangar ground rates at the 
Grants Pass Airport already average above the validated peer market, so the program applies existing policy 
rather than broad increases. Follow-on items returning to the Board within 120 days: a transfer and assignment 
policy requiring County consent and a current lease before any hangar sale closes; an updated Rates and

Charges Policy reflecting the 2026 market study; and the 2026-2027 fee schedule, including rates for hangar 
rows not on the current published schedule.
8.  Alternatives
• A.  Adopt the directive as presented (recommended).
• B.  Adopt with modified milestones or reporting frequency.
• C.  Leave scheduling to departmental discretion. The October review found items outstanding since 2014 
under that approach; the identified revenue and the uninsured exposure would continue to accrue at a 
measurable monthly cost.
9.  Recommendation and suggested motion
Commissioner [COMMISSIONER NAME] recommends Alternative A. Suggested motion:
“I move that the Board of County Commissioners direct the Department of Airports to implement the 90-Day 
Airport Lease Modernization Program as presented; adopt the milestones in Section 4 of the proposal as Board 
deadlines; require a written progress report from the Airports Manager at each regular Board meeting until the 
program is complete; designate Commissioner Richardson to oversee implementation on the Board's behalf; affirm 
that the Board of County Commissioners' office will provide additional resources as appropriate to support this 
one-time effort; and direct staff to return to the Board within 120 days with a proposed transfer and assignment 
policy, an updated Rates and Charges Policy, and the 2026-2027 airport fee schedule.”
