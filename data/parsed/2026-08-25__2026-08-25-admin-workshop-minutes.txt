1. Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest 
Commissioners Richardson, Martin, and Smith each declared no potential conflicts of interest. 
2. Department Business 
a. Legal 
1. Resolution No. 2026-032 - Water/Sewer Infrastructure Extension Funding 
Leah Harper presented Resolution No. 2026-032 supporting Josephine County and the City of Cave Junction in pursuing 
state funding for planning and engineering associated with extending water and sewer infrastructure toward Kirby and the 
Illinois Valley Airport. The Board discussed that the County and City would pursue separate state programs serving the 
same general infrastructure-planning purpose, with Josephine County eligible to lead one application and the City eligible 
to lead the program limited to communities under 25,000 population. The Board expressed support and directed that the 
resolution move forward to a Weekly Business Session for public consideration. 
2. Letter of Support - City of Cave Junction Water/Sewer Infrastructure Application 
Ms. Harper reviewed the companion letter supporting the City of Cave Junction application. The Board confirmed that the 
request is for planning and feasibility work rather than construction of infrastructure and directed that the letter be placed 
on the next Weekly Business Session agenda with the related resolution. 
b. Finance 
1. Amendment to Board Order No. 2026-023 - Economic Development Grant Advisory Committee 
Matthew Eide, Finance Director, reviewed proposed revisions to the Economic Development Grant Advisory Committee. 
Finance recommended reducing the committee from seven members to five because applications had not been received 
for all of the originally designated categories. The proposal contemplated two business-owner positions and three at-large 
positions, with one-year terms for the initial committee. 
The Board discussed removing employee-count restrictions from the business-owner positions so that applicants from 
businesses of any size could qualify. Commissioners also discussed geographic representation, industry diversity, 
conflicts for persons who may wish to apply for County economic-development grants, and the advisory nature of the 
committee. Finance reported receiving eleven committee applications, including one from Cave Junction and ten from the 
Grants Pass area. The Board directed Finance to revise the order so both designated positions are simply business-owner 
positions and to bring the revised order to the following Weekly Business Session, rather than the August 26 meeting. 
2. Board Order No. 2026-045 - Economic Development Grant Advisory Committee Bylaws 
Finance reviewed draft bylaws, including revisions concerning terms of service and how a tie recommendation would be 
handled. Commissioner Richardson proposed additional language broadening the definition of economic development 
beyond direct job creation to include infrastructure or other projects that may generate economic activity, and discussed 
allowing County departments to compete for grants under the same criteria as other applicants. Other Commissioners 
requested time to review the proposed revisions and emphasized that the rules should remain general rather than be 
tailored to specific projects. 
The Board tabled the bylaws and the updated economic-development grant application for additional review and directed 
that both return for discussion at the next Administrative Workshop. 
c. Human Resources 
1. Collective Bargaining Agreement - Federation of Law Enforcement Officers 
Natalie Eckhardt-Doupe, Interim Human Resources Director, and Community Justice staff presented the completed three-
year collective bargaining agreement with the Federation of Law Enforcement Officers (FOPO). Human Resources 
described the negotiations as successful and stated that the agreement brings the bargaining unit closer to market 
comparables while remaining within a reasonable range for the County. 
Commissioner Martin made a motion to approve the Collective Bargaining Agreement – Federation of Law Enforcement 
Officers. Commissioner Smith seconded. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson - Yes; Commissioner Martin - Yes; 
Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0.

a. Clerk - Chief Deputy Clerk 
Human Resources reported that a Clerk's Office employee had retired and the department requested authority to refill the 
position. Review of the classification showed that the position had been titled Chief Administrative Supervisor even 
though the statutory title is Chief Deputy Clerk. Human Resources updated the title and job duties and stated that the 
position is budgeted and will be posted externally. No objection was stated to proceeding with the recruitment. 
b. Sheriff's Office Supervisory Compensation b-f 
Human Resources reviewed compensation for five non-union Sheriff's Office supervisory positions - two lieutenants, the 
undersheriff, the Animal Control Supervisor, and the Lead Dispatcher - after the recently completed Sheriff's Association 
and Sergeants bargaining agreements created wage compression or overlap with employees they supervise. Several of the 
affected classifications had not been comprehensively reviewed since 2017-2019. Human Resources completed 
comparative compensation work and proposed revised scales designed to maintain an approximately $10,000 separation 
between the sergeant and lieutenant ranges while remaining generally below nearby comparator agencies. 
Finance estimated that the lieutenants and Lead Dispatcher could be accommodated within existing departmental 
appropriations, while the Animal Control Supervisor and undersheriff adjustments could require approximately $40,000 
from the General Fund. The Board discussed the need to account for secondary compensation effects when collective 
bargaining agreements are negotiated, the importance of employee retention, maintaining reasonable step differentials, 
and starting future bargaining early enough to inform the annual budget process. Human Resources stated that the change 
remained below the threshold requiring a separate supplemental-budget action and that a tracking system would be 
established so classifications do not go extended periods without review. The Board authorized Human Resources to 
proceed with the pay-scale adjustments and signatures; no separate formal motion was taken. 
3. Emergency Management Director - Removal of Interim Designation 
Human Resources presented a personnel action to remove the word "Interim" from the Emergency Management Director 
title. The position had originally been filled on an interim basis while the County determined the structure of Information 
Technology and Emergency Management. Following separation of the departments and completion of an external 
recruitment process, Human Resources requested the title change only. The Board approved the change without objection.
