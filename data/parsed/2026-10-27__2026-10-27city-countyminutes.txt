City/County Monthly Update: October 27, 2026 
11:00 a.m. - Board of Commissioners Conference Room 
Attending: Commissioners Ron A. Smith; Colene M. Martin; Gary L. Richardson; and Nikole Guzman, 
Recorder 
Additional Attendees: Councilor Rick Riker, City of Grants Pass; Thomas Peterson, Solid Waste Specialist;

Chair Ron Smith called the joint meeting to order at 11:00 a.m. 
Commissioners Richardson and Martin stated they had no potential conflicts of interest. 
1. Discussion with City Representatives 
The City and County held an open discussion focused primarily on vehicle camping, illegal dumping, solid 
waste enforcement, and conditions at Devil's Slide. No formal agenda beyond the joint discussion was 
presented. 
Vehicle Camping / Parker's Place. The City and County revisited the prior discussion of whether County 
property might be available for a vehicle-camping area. It was reported that a review of County-owned 
property did not identify a suitable site. Thomas Peterson explained that Josephine County adopted 
hazardous-conditions provisions for public property in 2023 after recurring camping, solid-waste, drug-use, 
and cleanup problems, including costly cleanups in Wolf Creek and other locations. He advised that Public 
Health does not support establishing a County car-camping area at this time because of the likely 
environmental, public-health, law-enforcement, staffing, and financial impacts. The City representative also 
indicated that a low-barrier or vehicle-camping proposal was unlikely to receive support under current 
conditions. The concept was left for possible future discussion rather than advanced. 
Illegal Dumping, Solid Waste, and Tire Disposal. The group discussed whether higher disposal rates 
contribute to illegal dumping, including conditions around the former Kerby landfill. Peterson said his 
experience suggests dumping is often a behavioral or cultural issue rather than strictly a cost issue, noting 
recent cases involving newer vehicles bypassing lawful disposal sites. He described recent enforcement 
successes using photographs, video, discarded mail, social media, and coordination with law enforcement. 
Participants praised Clean Sweep and Friends of Country Living for cleanup efforts and discussed 
expanding tire-disposal opportunities and similar programs countywide. Peterson noted that Public Health 
now has an online complaint form and can also receive anonymous reports when residents are 
uncomfortable contacting law enforcement directly. 
Devil's Slide / City-Owned Property. Peterson reported that he and a Sheriff's deputy recently spent 
several hours at Devil's Slide meeting with residents and people occupying camps. Based on the field visit 
and mapping provided by nearby residents, most occupied camps are on City of Grants Pass property 
located outside the city limits and therefore within County code-enforcement jurisdiction. Peterson 
estimated approximately 10 to 20 people may be present at a given time. He explained that the County 
would address solid-waste and hazardous-condition violations in the same manner as violations on other 
property: the property owner would be notified, a voluntary-compliance plan established, and the site 
reinspected, with further enforcement available if needed. The County will continue patrol and education 
efforts on County property to discourage new camps. Fire danger, open burning, nearby residences, and 
railroad property were identified as significant concerns. The discussion distinguished the broader 
homelessness issue from the County's responsibility to enforce public-health and solid-waste codes. 
Public Education, Staffing, and Interagency Coordination. Peterson reported that Program Specialist 
Caitlin Brown would begin work the following week and assist with reviewing older compliance cases and 
organizing field work. He said additional field capacity may eventually be needed as the program rebuilds.

The group discussed continued coordination with the Sheriff's Office and resident-deputy program, fire 
districts, the Solid Waste Agency, DEQ, BLM, ODOT, neighborhood groups, and volunteer cleanup 
organizations. The Board encouraged Peterson to work with Public Information Officer Thomas Gray, 
Public Health leadership, the City of Grants Pass, and the City of Cave Junction on a public-education 
campaign, potentially including a community slogan or contest to discourage dumping and promote 
stewardship. Councilor Riker agreed to relay the Devil's Slide discussion to the City. The County also 
discussed exploring another tire-disposal event and periodically reevaluating program needs and progress. 
No decisions were made. 
2. Other/Additional Subject Matters from Commissioners - ORS 192.640(1) 
No additional matters were presented. 
The meeting adjourned at 11:59 a.m.
