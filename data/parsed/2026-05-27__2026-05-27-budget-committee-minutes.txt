3) Department Presentations: 
a. Clerk – Rhiannon Henkels Exhibit 1 PowerPoint Presentation 
Rhiannon Henkels, County Clerk, presented the proposed Clerk's Office budget and reviewed the 
department's statutory responsibilities, including elections administration, recording services, passport 
acceptance, marriage licenses, and administration of the Property Value Appeals Board. Henkels provided 
operational statistics related to recorded documents, voter registrations, marriage licenses, passport 
applications, and ongoing efforts to digitize historical records and improve public accessibility. She 
highlighted implementation of the Property Recording Alert Service (PRAS), expansion of online services, 
and future plans to improve public education regarding Oregon elections and election processes. Discussion 
occurred regarding department revenues, fee structures established by statute, election security 
enhancements, and the potential acquisition of ballot sorting equipment utilizing Secretary of State grant 
funding. Exhibit 1 – Clerk PowerPoint Presentation. 
b. Elected Officials Compensation Committee – Michelle Shaw Exhibit 2 Meeting Minutes 
Handout  
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources Director, presented recommendations from the Elected Officials 
Compensation Committee. Shaw explained that the committee reviewed compensation information from 
comparable Oregon counties and developed recommendations intended to improve competitiveness while 
recognizing current fiscal conditions. Recommended adjustments included a ten percent increase for the 
Sheriff and District Attorney, a four percent increase for Legal Counsel, a three percent increase for the 
Treasurer and Surveyor, and no increase for the Assessor, Clerk, or Commissioners. Committee members 
discussed recruitment and retention challenges, compensation comparisons among similarly situated 
counties, and distinctions associated with state-funded portions of District Attorney compensation. Exhibit 2 
– Elected Officials Compensation Committee Compensation Comparison. 
c. Assessor – Chris Parton-Exhibit 3 PowerPoint Presentation  
Chris Parton, County Assessor, presented the proposed Assessor's Office budget and reviewed the 
department's responsibility for maintaining assessment records, appraising taxable property, administering 
property tax functions, and maintaining mapping and ownership records. Parton reported that the office 
manages approximately $17 billion in taxable market value and approximately $10 billion in assessed value. 
He reviewed staffing levels, operational efficiencies, online services, and state performance benchmarks 
indicating the department continues to operate below recommended staffing levels. Discussion occurred 
regarding technology investments, artificial intelligence applications, customer service levels, staffing 
limitations, and the balance between maintaining current operations and pursuing future technological 
improvements. Exhibit 3 – Assessor PowerPoint Presentation. 
d. Treasurer – Dustin Calvo Exhibit 4 PowerPoint Presentation  
Dustin Calvo, Treasurer, presented the proposed Treasurer and Tax Department budget. Calvo reviewed the 
department's responsibilities for managing County funds, investments, tax collection, distribution of 
revenues to taxing districts, and maintenance of financial controls. He reported that investment earnings had 
exceeded $2.5 million during the fiscal year and that approximately ninety-one percent of current-year 
property taxes had been collected. Discussion occurred regarding investment strategies, staffing levels, office 
modernization efforts, internal controls, electronic payment processing, tax collection rates, and proposed 
improvements to public access and departmental efficiency. Committee members also discussed investment 
returns, staffing challenges, and opportunities to further streamline departmental operations through 
technology improvements. Exhibit 4 – Treasurer PowerPoint Presentation. 
e. Surveyor – Peter Allen Exhibit 5 PowerPoint Presentation 
Peter Allen, County Surveyor, presented the proposed Surveyor budget and reviewed statutory 
responsibilities associated with maintaining survey records, reviewing plats and surveys, preserving public 
land survey monuments, and providing public access to historical records. Allen explained that the 
department operates through both General Fund support and the Public Land Corner Preservation Fund. 
Discussion occurred regarding staffing levels, continued vacancies within the department, public access to 
records, digitization efforts, long-term preservation of historical documents, and the need to refill a Senior 
Department Specialist position to improve operational efficiency and customer service. Allen also reviewed 
trends affecting survey activity and recording volumes and discussed the relationship between survey 
services and infrastructure development throughout the County. Exhibit 5 – Surveyor PowerPoint 
Presentation. 
f. Building – Frank Wharregard Exhibit 6 PowerPoint Presentation 
Frank Wharregard, Building Official, presented the proposed Building Department budget and reviewed 
permitting activities, inspection services, code enforcement responsibilities, and departmental funding 
sources. Discussion occurred regarding construction activity, permit volumes, customer service 
improvements, staffing considerations, and efforts to maintain efficient turnaround times while complying 
with regulatory requirements. Committee members discussed the impact of development trends on 
departmental workload and future service demands. Exhibit 6 – Building Department Presentation. 
g. Planning – James Black Exhibit 7 PowerPoint Presentation 
James Black, Planning Director, presented the proposed Planning Department budget and reviewed land use 
planning responsibilities, development review activities, and statutory obligations associated with statewide 
land use requirements. Black discussed current development trends, planning workloads, long-range 
planning efforts, and departmental funding considerations. Discussion occurred regarding housing 
development, land use applications, staffing levels, customer service expectations, and future planning 
priorities within Josephine County. Exhibit  7– Planning Department Presentation. 
h. Veterans – Lisa Pickart  
Lisa Pickart, Veterans Services Officer, presented the proposed Veterans Services budget and reviewed 
programs available to veterans and their families. Pickart discussed ongoing efforts to assist veterans in 
obtaining federal and state benefits, outreach activities, claims processing, and coordination with community 
partners. Discussion occurred regarding the increasing complexity of veterans' benefit programs, demand for 
services, and the importance of ensuring veterans have access to available resources and assistance.

Elections
The County Clerk is the Chief Election Officer of the County, charged by statute with the conduct of Elections. This involves 
managing voter registration (received over the counter, by mail, from Dept. of Motor Vehicles, from on-line and registration 
drives) per the mandates of the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) and the Help America Vote Act (HAVA), establishing 
precinct boundaries and working with the cities on property annexation changes and adjusting district boundaries according to 
redistricting requirements.
The County Clerk also advises and administers the filing of initiative, referendum and recall petitions for proper forms and 
procedure per Oregon law and County Charter requirements. The County Clerk produces voters’ pamphlets for the electorate in 
conjunction with HAVA requirements of voter education.
Recording
The County Clerk is charged by statute with the recording, custodial retention and public availability of land records and other 
records allowed for recording by law. These include, but are not limited to deeds, mortgages, reconveyances, plats, subdivisions, 
powers of attorney, contracts, liens, Josephine County Tax Warrants and satisfactions, mining affidavits and other documents 
affecting the title to real property. The County Clerk’s Office issues and records marriage licenses. The fees are apportioned to 
Clerk, State Domestic Violence fund and County Juvenile Mediation Service. The office processes passport applications by Federal 
laws.
Board of Property Tax Appeals (BOPTA)
Oregon Statutes including but not limited to Chapters 309.
The County Clerk is the clerk to the Board of Property Tax Appeals, charged with appointing board members, accepting petitions, 
recording hearings and mailing orders, according to ORS and Department of Revenue directives.

PLANNING DIVISION
2026-2027 BUDGET
The State of Oregon mandates counties of the state to have planning 
and zoning programs.  This mandate, found in ORS 215, not only 
requires a planning and zoning program, but mandates other 
regulatory and land use laws that counties must enforce on behalf of 
the state.
In order to provide for orderly growth and development in the 
community, Josephine County facilitates this mandated function 
through coordinated programs of Planning and Building Safety.  The 
program’s emphasis is on educating and serving the public.
Planning Division – Purpose of 
Program 
Planning Division – Purpose of 
Program 
The Oregon Department of Land Conservation and Development (DLCD) serves as the 
oversight agency for the planning program and provides guidance for counties.  The 
Planning Division conducts reviews of land use proposals and issues land use permits 
under the authority of the Josephine County Code (JCC) Title 19 – Rural Land 
Development Code (RLDC).
The Community Development Department is also responsible for responding to reports of 
non-compliance with land use and building codes and County Ordinances.  Policies and 
procedures are established and implemented for investigating complaints to verify, and if 
founded, work cooperatively with property owners to achieve compliance.  The overriding 
goal is to educate property owners of the purpose for the regulations with hopes of 
achieving voluntary compliance. 
Budget Goal 1 - Improve Efficiency, Transparency, 
and Public Engagement
Enhance customer service by utilizing SMARTGOV application portal to help customers submit 
applications at their convenience.  Research SMARTGOV capabilities to move towards a more 
digital application review platform.  Update our website to communicate with our citizens on public 
notices and land use decisions.  Update website to allow access to documents and mapping 
systems to help customers research properties.   Proactively communicate with our community 
through the launch of social media page for our department.  This will enable us to more directly 
communicate with the citizens of Josephine County.        
Budget Goal 2 -Ensure Long-Term Financial 
Sustainability of Core Services
Work with BCC on finding balance between general fund needs and actual cost of service with 
recovery of fees.  Planning has statutory authority to collect fees to cover cost of service.  Annual 
review of Planning fees is a way to help make decisions on budget needs from general fund.             
Budget Goal 3 -Strategic Investment in Public Safety and 
Community Infrastructure
Although we are not directly responsible for public safety as the Sheriff’s office, DA, and Jail, our land use 
code does have requirements for public health and safety.  Code requirements for adequate water, septic, 
and access are key requirements for development in the County.  Planning takes this responsibility seriously.  
Planning’s role is more tertiary as more people develop; we make sure carrying capacity of the development 
allows for future public safety.

2026-2027 Budget summary conclusion
Efficiency and Sustainability remain at the core of the Planning Division for each 
budget season.  Allowances have been made in the process of projecting fees for 
our 2026-2027 fiscal year to reduce expenditures in order to close the gap 
between Revenue and Expenses.  
2026-2027 Budget summary conclusion
Historically, the Community Development Department has been fiscally 
dependent on economic highs and lows. The Planning Division is responsible for 
continuity of services but relies heavily on annual support from the budget 
committee for General Fund contribution.  There is no way to completely predict 
fluctuations in the development industry.  However, we must be able to provide 
mandated services at a level to comply with State statutes and the public’s needs.  
Moving forward, the Board of County Commissioners may want to explore fee 
increases in order to more precisely align actual cost of services provided with 
fees collected.
