Scope of 
Continued Hearing I Planning Clarification: 
James Black, Josephine County Planning Director, clarified that the question before the Board was whether to 
approve or deny the farm crop processing facility under the review criteria contained in the staff report. He stated 
that the continued hearing concerned the processing facility and the three 40-foot containers used for that operation, 
rather than the broader cultivation operation or general water use on the farm. 
Chair Smith stated that the prior hearing had proceeded through the evidentiary presentations and rebuttal. Planning 
reported that no new written information had been received as of the preceding Friday. The Chair offered each side 
five minutes to present any new information; neither side presented new information at that point, and the Board 
proceeded to deliberation. 
Board Deliberation I Proposed Additional Conditions: 
Commissioner Richardson asked Planning to address possible additional conditions that had been discussed at the 
August 24 hearing. Planning explained that Josephine County Code requires at least 25 percent of the material 
processed at a farm crop processing facility to originate from the subject farm. The Board clarified that the 
requirement concerns the source of the material being processed, not the percentage of the subject property crop 
that must be processed. Planning stated that existing OLCC tracking information and the County annual compliance 
process could be used to verify compliance without creating a separate reporting system. 
The Board also discussed a condition confirming that no water would be used in the manufacturing or processing 
operation. Planning stated that the processing proposal is a dry process and that such a condition could be imposed. 
The discussion clarified that the condition would address water used for manufacturing/processing and would not 
prohibit water needed for an emergency or other lawful safety purpose. 
Commissioner Richardson further asked whether commercial activity associated with the processing facility could 
be required to use Dick George Road rather than Greenview Road. Planning stated that such an access condition 
was within the Board's authority. Neil Burgess, Josephine County Engineer, advised that two access permits from 
Dick George Road had originally been issued in 2017, had expired, and were extended for purposes of this 
application. Public Works deemed the Dick George Road access permits acceptable for the proposed processing 
facility. 
Commissioner Martin acknowledged the concerns expressed by neighboring property owners during the appeal 
regarding odor, property values, quality of life, neighborhood relations, screening, and other impacts. She stated 
that the Board was obligated to apply the applicable law and County ordinances to the application. Commissioner 
Martin requested an additional condition requiring carbon filtration for the processing operation. 
Planning summarized the four additional conditions discussed by the Board: ( 1) at least 25 percent of the material 
processed at the facility must originate from Tax Lot 314; (2) no water shall be used for manufacturing/processing 
at the facility; (3) access for commercial activity associated with the processing facility shall be limited to Dick 
George Road and not Greenview Road; and ( 4) carbon filters shall be required for the processing facility.

Additional Neighbor Testimony I Owner-Applicant Response: 
A neighboring Greenview Road resident who had testified at the prior hearing asked whether the Commissioners 
had visited the area and reiterated concerns about expansion of cultivation, water demand, and traffic. Chair Smith 
stated that he had not been able to make a site visit during the intervening week and had instead reviewed the 
property and surrounding homes using Google Earth. 
The owner/applicant representative responded that the use of hoop houses and potted plants was intended to improve 
management efficiency and was not expected to increase water use; he stated the licensed canopy area remained 
within OLCC limits. He further stated that the operation did not use Greenview Road for routine access and that the 
Greenview access had only been used for emergency purposes. The Board again distinguished the existing 
cultivation operation from the processing-facility application before it. 
Decision: 
Planning Director Black restated that the Board could approve or deny the farm processing facility and, if approved, 
could add the four conditions discussed during the hearing to the conditions already contained in Exhibit N. He 
stated that Planning staff would prepare the written findings and final decision for subsequent Board review and 
approval. 
Commissioner Richardson moved to affirm the Planning Department's approval of 
the farm crop processing facility 
subject to the conditions of 
approval in Exhibit N and the four additional conditions stated by Planning Director 
James Black during the August 31, 2026 hearing. The motion was seconded. Upon roll call: Commissioner 
Richardson - Yes,· Commissioner Martin - Yes,· Commissioner Smith - Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
The Board directed Planning staff to prepare the written findings and final decision consistent with the Board's 
action for return to the Board. The Board's action affirmed the Planning Director's approval as modified by the 
additional conditions.
