2. Department Business 
a. Legal 
1. Special Service District for DA-Juvenile Justice - Jail 
Leah Harper, Assistant Legal Counsel, appeared on behalf of County Counsel Wally Hicks and asked 
whether the Board wished Legal Counsel to continue preparatory work on a possible special service district 
for the District Attorney, Juvenile Justice, and Jail. Harper explained that the work would create a potential 
future funding tool and would not itself impose a tax. The Board discussed that any future tax base or levy 
would require voter approval and that additional coordination, including with the incorporated cities, would 
be necessary before a district could be implemented. 
The Commissioners discussed whether beginning the preparatory work now would preserve future ballot 
options or whether the discussion should wait until after the November election. Commissioner Richardson 
favored completing the preliminary work in advance so a future Board would have the option available. 
Commissioners Smith and Martin expressed concern with the timing and preferred additional financial 
information before moving forward. 
Finance Director Matt Eide advised that current projections remain relatively stable in the near term but 
become less certain around the third year. He stated that by spring 2027 Finance should have additional 
operating data and a better ability to project the funding needs of the Jail and Juvenile Justice programs. The 
Board discussed revisiting the service-district concept after the election and again after the first of the year 
when updated financial information is available. 
No formal action was taken. The Board tabled the matter until after the November election. 
2. Agenda Modifications 
Leah Harper, Assistant Legal Counsel, reviewed the rules and best practices governing agenda 
modifications after public notice has been posted. Harper explained that matters not specifically listed may 
be raised and discussed when appropriate, but formal action on an unlisted matter should generally be 
delayed until it can be properly noticed for the public. She noted that some actions have separate statutory 
notice requirements and should be reviewed with Legal Counsel on a case-by-case basis. The Board also 
discussed emergency and time-sensitive matters, including grant deadlines, and clarified that once an 
agenda has been posted it should not be revised after the notice deadline. 
No formal action was taken.

e. Arguments Supporting the Levy 
Commissioner Martin asked whether the Commissioners wished to submit a voter pamphlet argument 
supporting renewal of the Adult Jail and Juvenile Detention levy. She reviewed prior commissioner-
supported arguments, including a 2017 submission, and explained that the $400 filing cost would be paid 
personally by the Commissioners rather than with County funds. The filing deadline was later that day. The 
Board reviewed three draft versions and discussed the role of Commissioners in educating voters, the 
consequences if the levy failed, the effect of inflation on the existing 93-cent rate, and the importance of 
maintaining stable Jail and Juvenile Justice services. Commissioner Richardson stated that elected officials 
should provide the public with the recommendation reached after reviewing the available information, while 
the Board also emphasized explaining the financial consequences and allowing voters to make the final 
decision. The Commissioners favored Version 1, centered on keeping existing services working, subject to 
minor edits. 
By consensus without objection, the Board agreed to proceed with a personally funded voter pamphlet 
argument in support of the levy using Version 1 as the basis for the final submission.
