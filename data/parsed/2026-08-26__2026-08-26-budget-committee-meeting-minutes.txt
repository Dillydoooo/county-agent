Supplemental Budget - Additional Staffing Needs for FY 2026-2027 
Budget Officer and Finance Director Matthew Eide presented a supplemental staffing package totaling 6.5 additional 
full-time-equivalent positions across several County funds. He explained that departments had been asked to identify 
either a significant omission from the adopted budget or materially new circumstances or funding that arose after 
adoption. 
The requests consisted of: a WIC dietitian in Public Health supported by new grant funding; one Solid Waste support 
position funded through Solid Waste franchise revenue; one Building and Safety code-enforcement position supported 
through departmental fees; one Community Corrections parole/probation position funded through reallocation within 
that department; two Internal Services positions, one focused on Finance audit work and one Human Resources 
Analyst; and a half-time Veterans Affairs support position. The new authorized expenditure associated with the 
package totaled $596,000, exclusive of the Community Corrections FTE that would be funded through an existing 
departmental reallocation. 
The Committee discussed whether some requests had previously been considered during adoption of the original 
budget, the distinction between Building and Safety code enforcement and Solid Waste enforcement, the source of 
funding for each position, and the effect of the new WIC grant. Mr. Eide explained that the full Public Health grant is 
approximately $398,000, with $107,000 allocated to the proposed dietitian position. 
Substantial discussion occurred regarding County reserves, contingencies, current vacancies, and recently negotiated 
collective bargaining agreements. Committee members questioned whether compensation increases could reduce the 
number of FTEs the County is able to fill within previously approved appropriations and discussed the importance of 
ensuring that budgeted FTE counts accurately reflect positions the County can actually staff. Mr. Eide stated that 
approximately forty or more positions were vacant at the time and that roughly twenty were then posted, although 
some postings represented multiple vacancies. 
The Committee also discussed law-enforcement staffing funded through voter-approved taxes, the distinction between 
authorized FTE and cash appropriations, and whether vacant positions should be reduced to offset compensation 
changes. Members expressed differing views on whether reductions should occur immediately or whether departments 
should be given time to recruit and manage vacancies and turnover. Finance agreed to continue analyzing the financial 
effect of completed collective bargaining agreements and to provide updated information as the actual costs are 
finalized. 
Mr. Eide reviewed fund-level impacts. He stated that the General Fund request presented that day was approximately 
$40,000 for the half-time Veterans Affairs position, while the other requests were supported through Public Health 
grant funds, Solid Waste funds, Building and Safety funds, Internal Services, or Community Corrections. Committee 
members also discussed the approximately $1.2 million operating contingency in Fund 10, restricted or dedicated 
reserves in other funds, and the need to distinguish available General Fund resources from funds legally limited to 
specific purposes.
