County Administration Workshop: June 9, 2026 
1:30 p.m. – Board of Commissioners Conference Room 
Attending: Commissioners Ron A. Smith; Colene M. Martin; Gary L. Richardson; and Nikole Guzman, Recorder 
 
Chair Ron Smith called the meeting to order at 1:30 p.m. 
1. Declaration of Potential Conflicts of Interest 
The board declared there are no potential conflicts of interest.  
2. Department Business 
a. Property Management 
1. Monthly Update re: Relocation of County Departments 
Helene Lulich, County Property Manager, provided an update regarding the pending sale of the DEMIC properties and 
ongoing efforts to identify suitable locations for County departments currently occupying affected facilities. Ms. Lulich 
reported that the due diligence period is expected to conclude on July 22, 2026, with closing anticipated on or before August 
21, 2026. Leaseback provisions remain in place for Public Health and Planning facilities following the transaction.  
Ms. Lulich and Facilities Director Ryan Johnson reported that several County-owned properties continue to be evaluated as 
potential relocation sites. Discussion focused on the N Street property, facility improvement needs, warehouse space 
availability, and potential use of modular office structures if alternate facilities cannot be secured. Commissioners discussed 
ongoing efforts to locate suitable office space for Public Health and other affected departments and acknowledged that 
additional evaluation would continue prior to any final relocation decisions. No formal action was taken. 
b. Sheriff 
1. Intergovernmental County - City Lease Agreement for Sheriff's Office Sub-Station Amendment 
No. 12 
Dave Daniel, County Sheriff, presented a renewal agreement with the City of Cave Junction for continued use of the Sheriff's 
Office substation facility located within the Cave Junction City Hall building. Sheriff Daniel explained that the facility has 
been utilized by the Sheriff's Office for many years as a substation, report writing location, and temporary holding facility 
serving the Illinois Valley. Discussion occurred regarding the history of the facility, operational needs, and continued use 
of the location to support law enforcement services in the area. 
Commissioner Martin made a motion to approve Intergovernmental County - City Lease Agreement for Sheriff's Office Sub-
Station Amendment No. 12. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Richardson. Upon roll call; Commissioner 
Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
2. City of Cave Junction Contract Deputy Agreement 
Dave Daniel, County Sheriff, presented the annual agreement with the City of Cave Junction for dedicated deputy patrol 
services. The agreement provides funding for one full-time deputy assigned to the City of Cave Junction in the amount of 
$211,471. Discussion occurred regarding the history of the agreement, law enforcement coverage provided within the 
Illinois Valley, impacts of the Law Enforcement Service District, and the Sheriff's Office practice of maintaining multiple 
deputies in the area to ensure adequate officer safety and service levels. Commissioners discussed the importance of 
maintaining law enforcement presence within Cave Junction and surrounding communities. 
Commissioner Martin made a motion to approve City of Cave Junction Contract Agreement. The motion was seconded by 
Commissioner Richardson. Upon roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, Commissioner 
Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
3. USFS Cooperative Law Enforcement Agreement 
Dave Daniel, County Sheriff, Sheriff Daniel presented the annual U.S. Forest Service Cooperative Law Enforcement 
Agreement. The agreement provides up to $50,000 in reimbursement funding for Sheriff's Office patrol activities conducted 
on federal forest lands within Josephine County. Sheriff Daniel explained that the agreement supports dedicated patrols and 
law enforcement activities occurring on National Forest lands and noted that Josephine County's extensive federal land base 
contributes to the need for these services. Discussions occurred regarding funding levels, patrol activity, and comparisons 
with neighboring counties.  
Commissioner Martin made a motion to approve USFS Cooperative Law Enforcement Agreement. The motion was 
seconded by Commissioner Richardson. Upon roll call; Commissioner Richardson – yes, Commissioner Martin – yes, 
Commissioner Smith – yes. Motion carried 3-0.

c. Human Resources 
1. Position Requisitions 
a. Prevention and Treatment Services – School Outreach Officer – Refill Vacated position. Vacated 
5/22/2026  
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources, presented a request to fill a vacant Prevention and Treatment Services School Outreach 
Officer position within Community Justice. Community Justice Director Scott Hyde explained that School Outreach 
Officers are embedded within local schools and provide prevention education, youth programming, and community outreach 
services. Hyde discussed the success of the program, statewide interest in the County's model, and the position's role 
supporting both school-based services and youth programs during summer months. The Board expressed support for filling 
the position. No objection was raised to proceeding with recruitment. 
2. Personnel Actions 
a. Corrections – Parole/Probation Officer Advanced – New Hire - PR 26-016 approved 2/26/2026 
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources, presented a request to hire a certified Parole and Probation Officer. Hyde 
reported that the candidate currently serves in Coos County and possesses extensive law enforcement and 
community corrections experience, including advanced certifications and more than a decade of parole and 
probation service. Hyde described the candidate as highly qualified and noted the rarity of recruiting an 
experienced certified officer directly from another jurisdiction. No objection was raised to proceeding with the 
hire. 
b. Corrections – Parole/Probation Officer Basic – Transfer - PR 26-016 approved 2/26/2026 
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources, presented a request involving an interdepartmental transfer from the Sheriff's Office 
into a Parole and Probation Officer position. Hyde explained that the employee brings significant law enforcement 
experience and would provide substantial value to Community Justice operations despite not previously serving in a parole 
and probation role. Discussion occurred regarding county policy requirements governing transfer compensation and the 
benefits of recruiting experienced employees from within County government. No objection was raised to proceeding with 
the transfer. 
c. District Attorney – Department Specialist – New Hire - PR 26-051 approved 4/28/2026 
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources, presented a request to fill a vacant Department Specialist position within the District 
Attorney's Office. Staff explained that the position serves as the primary front desk contact for the office and is responsible 
for reception duties, phone coverage, public assistance, discovery processing, scheduling support, and related administrative 
functions. Discussion occurred regarding the importance of maintaining front desk coverage and reducing interruptions to 
other staff members. No objection was raised to proceeding with recruitment. 
 
d. Transit – Transit Operator – Change FTE due to Layoff 
Michelle Shaw, Human Resources Director, informed the Board that a Transit Department position would be reduced from 
full-time to part-time status, resulting in a reduction from 1.0 FTE to 0.5 FTE. The adjustment was presented for 
informational purposes only. 
e. Treasury – Tax and Accounting Specialist I – New Hire - PR 26-047 approved 4/14/202 
Matter was tabled.

4. OTHER 
5. ADDITIONAL SUBJECT MATTERS FROM COMMISSIONERS/ANNOUNCEMENTS:  
Commissioner Martin revisited discussion regarding Resolution No. 2026-009, a resolution affirming tobacco retail 
enforcement as the primary strategy to prevent youth access to tobacco and nicotine products while protecting adult 
consumer choice. Martin reviewed previous discussions regarding the resolution and reported that Legal Counsel had 
determined adoption of the resolution would not affect County grant funding. Martin also referenced support received from 
the Oregon Neighborhood Store Association and discussed her interest in supporting adult consumer choice and local retail 
businesses while maintaining protections preventing youth access to tobacco products.  
Commissioners discussed state regulation of tobacco products, age restrictions, local business impacts, and broader public 
policy considerations. Commissioner Richardson commented on perceived inconsistencies in state policies relating to age 
restrictions and personal decision-making. Chair Smith expressed support for allowing adults access to legal products while 
recognizing the distinction between adult and youth access.  
Legal Counsel advised that the resolution had previously been assigned Resolution No. 2026-009. Commissioner Martin 
disclosed a potential conflict based upon work performed with United Strategies on unrelated matters and indicated she
