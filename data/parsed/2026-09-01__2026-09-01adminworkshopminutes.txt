b. Information Technology 
1. Strategic Communication Planning 
Thomas Gray, Public Information Officer, provided an update on the County strategic communications work. 
He reported that communications materials for the Firewise defensible-space project were completed and 
promotional scheduling had begun. The leadership feedback period for the Strategic Communications Plan was 
complete, with comments received from more than half of County leadership; a revised draft would be 
circulated the following week, with a near-final version anticipated by September 15. Gray also reported that 
updates to the social media and public records policies were nearly complete and would be returned to Legal 
Counsel for review. At Gray's request, the Board agreed to extend the AI pilot-project wrap-up by one week, 
from September 11 to September 18, due to staff vacations that delayed implementation work. 
2. Packet Posting for the Public 
Commissioner Richardson proposed resuming the practice of posting the Board's meeting packet with the 
public agenda on the County website, except for materials properly identified as confidential. The Board 
discussed transparency, public-records requests, media access, website placement, and the need for an internal 
review process to ensure sensitive or confidential material is not inadvertently posted. Gray advised that posting 
agenda packets is a common local-government transparency practice and could be accomplished without 
significant website changes. The Board agreed that Legal Counsel and departments should continue identifying 
confidential materials before submission. Without objection, the Board authorized the Board Secretary to begin 
posting public agenda packets with meeting agendas, using a careful rollout and appropriate review for 
confidential material. 
c. Community Corrections 
1. Agreement to Rent Real Property at 1112 NE D Street and Application for Real Property Tax 
Exemption 
Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, presented renewal of the County's lease at 1112 NE D Street and the 
related application for real property tax exemption. The County has leased the residence since 2017 for its 
transitional housing program. The renewed term would run from October 2026 through September 2027 at 
$1,500 per month, approximately a seven-percent increase from the prior year but still below the estimated 
market rent of approximately $1,995 per month. Hyde explained that the residence generally houses four to six 
individuals on parole or probation and is monitored through Welcome Home Oregon. The program provides a

2. In-State Travel Authorization 
Scott Hyde, Community Justice Director, requested blanket authorization for recurring in-state overnight travel 
to Oregon Association of Community Corrections Directors meetings. Hyde serves both with the full 
association and its executive team; the meetings alternate by month and generally require one overnight stay, 
occasionally two depending on location. He advised that the authorization would cover approximately 95 
percent of his anticipated travel requests. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve the in-state travel authorization as presented. Commissioner 
Richardson seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson – Yes; Commissioner Martin – Yes; 
Commissioner Smith – Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
d. Finance 
1. Board Order 2026-044 – Josephine County Economic Development Grant Advisory Committee 
Matt Eide, Finance Director, presented Board Order No. 2026-044, carrying forward the Board's prior direction 
to reduce the Josephine County Economic Development Grant Advisory Committee from seven members to 
five. The revised membership consists of two business-owner positions and three members at large, with the 
prior small-business employee-count restriction removed. The Board had no additional questions regarding the 
revised membership structure. 
Commissioner Martin moved to approve Board Order No. 2026-044 as presented. Commissioner Richardson 
seconded the motion. Upon roll call: Commissioner Richardson – Yes; Commissioner Martin – Yes; 
Commissioner Smith – Yes. Motion carried 3-0. 
2. Board Order 2026-045 - Accepting By-Laws 
Matt Eide, Finance Director, presented the revised proposed By-Laws for the Josephine County Economic 
Development Grant Advisory Committee. The Board discussed strengthening conflict-of-interest provisions, 
including requiring members to disclose conflicts and, if a conflict arises involving an applicant for grant funds, 
to resign from the committee rather than participate in decisions affecting competing applications. The Board 
also reviewed quorum and tie-vote provisions; a majority of filled positions constitutes a quorum, and any tie 
recommendation would be referred to the Board of Commissioners. The Board requested revised conflict-of-
interest language and tabled Board Order No. 2026-045 until the next County Administration Workshop. Eide 
advised that the process of interviewing applicants for the five committee positions could proceed while the By-
Laws were being finalized. 
3. New Economic Development Grant Application 
Matt Eide, Finance Director, presented the updated Economic Development Grant application previously 
circulated to the Board. The revised application included additional qualifying questions and was intended to 
provide a clearer and more consistent annual grant-review process. The Board expressed support for the revised 
application. 
The Board approved the new Economic Development Grant application without objection.

3. Board Business 
a. Recommendation of Re-Appointments to the Josephine County Mining Advisory Committee - 
Don Bellville - At Large and Joel Perkins - At Large 
The Board discussed Resolution No. 2026-033, which would reappoint Don Bellville and Joel Perkins as At-
Large members of the Josephine County Mining Advisory Committee for four-year terms expiring September 
1, 2030. Chair Smith supported the reappointments based on the members' mining experience. Commissioner 
Martin raised potential conflict-of-interest concerns and requested review by Legal Counsel before the Board 
acted. The matter was tabled to the next County Administration Workshop pending that review. 
b. Agricultural Education Grants - 4-H Extension Service District 
Commissioner Richardson introduced discussion of approximately $30,000 in Agricultural Education Grant 
funding associated with the 4-H Extension Service District and the need to establish a consistent application and 
selection process. The Board discussed whether a separate advisory committee was necessary, potential 
conflicts of interest, a once-a-year application period, eligibility criteria, and possible applicants such as FFA or 
4-H clubs. The Board directed Commissioner Richardson to develop a proposed process and timeline and return 
with additional information regarding the funding source, frequency, eligibility criteria, and application 
requirements. 
c. DEQ - Outstanding Resource Water Designation; Illinois River and Rough & Ready Creek 
Commissioner Richardson discussed the Oregon Department of Environmental Quality's rulemaking 
concerning a proposed Outstanding Resource Water designation for portions of the Illinois River and Rough & 
Ready Creek. He expressed concern that the designation could affect groundwater-connected activities, future 
municipal wastewater permitting, industrial lands in the Illinois Valley, and potential biomass or wildfire-
reduction economic-development projects. Richardson presented a draft County comment letter requesting that 
Josephine County have representation during the rulemaking process and indicated his willingness to serve as 
the County representative. The Board supported the letter and County participation without objection. 
4. OTHER 
Commissioner Martin presented a request from the Applegate Daughters of the American Revolution to host the 
American Battlefield Trust traveling exhibit in the Anne G. Basker Auditorium from December 8 through 
December 18, 2026. The exhibit would require approximately 500 square feet, would be staffed while open, and 
is intended to be promoted to local schools and the public. The Board supported the concept and directed staff 
to verify Auditorium availability, including any Circuit Court conflicts, and confirm that the sponsoring 
organization meets the County's requirements for fee-free use before proceeding. 
5. ADDITIONAL SUBJECT MATTERS FROM COMMISSIONERS/ANNOUNCEMENTS: 
The Board announced that the Whitehorse Park ribbon-cutting scheduled for September 2 had been canceled 
because of rain and would be rescheduled. Commissioners welcomed the rainfall while noting that fire-season 
conditions could continue depending on weather. Commissioner Richardson also reported that County benefit 
enrollment had improved substantially, with approximately 20 employees remaining incomplete at the close of 
the enrollment campaign, and thanked departments and employees for their participation. 
The meeting adjourned at 2:32 p.m.
