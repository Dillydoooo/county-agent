Board of County Commissioners, 
The discussion at the August 10, 2026 appeal hearing placed substantial weight on ORS 215.211 
and whether the Applicant's soils report satisfied the requirements of that statute. After carefully 
reviewing this statute, ORS 215.211 is not applicable to a Non-Farm Dwelling Permit Application 
and should not be considered for the decision. 
Instead, the BCC has the clear authority to review the applicable criteria for a non-farm dwelling 
application based on the materials submitted to determine if the application qualifies for the 
proposed use. 
Applicability of ORS 215.211 
ORS 215.211 addresses circumstances in which more detailed soil information is sought to assist 
a county in determining whether land qualifies as agricultural land. This would be used if the 
property were being rezoned to a permanent rural residential zone or similar. 
The Applicant is not seeking to remove the subject property from the Exclusive Farm Use (EFU) 
zone, formally reclassify the property's NRCS soil classifications in general, or establish that the 
property is no longer agricultural land. The property may remain agricultural land with EFU 
zoning regardless of whether the requested non-farm dwelling is approved. 
Instead, the Applicant is requesting authorization to place a non-farm dwelling on a portion of 
an agricultural parcel that is generally unsuitable for farming. ORS 215.284 and County 
Development Code standards expressly provide a process for approval of non-farm dwellings 
within an EFU zone where the applicable criteria are satisfied. These criteria have clearly been 
addressed in the application documents with supplemental responses and new soils information 
addressed below. 
Accordingly, the questions before the Board are not: 
"Does this property qualify as agricultural land?" 
Or; 
"Does the Applicant's soils study satisfy ORS 215.211?" 
Rather, the relevant question is:

A lot or parcel or portion of a lot or parcel is not "generally 
unsuitable" simply because it is too small to be farmed 
profitably by itself. If a lot or parcel or portion of a lot or parcel 
can be sold, leased, rented or otherwise managed as a part of a 
commercial farm or ranch, then the lot or parcel or portion of 
the lot or parcel is not "generally unsuitable." A lot or parcel or 
portion of a lot or parcel is presumed to be suitable if it is 
composed predominantly of Class I-IV soils. Just because a lot 
or parcel or portion of a lot or parcel is unsuitable for one farm 
use does not mean it is not suitable for another farm use; or 
Although the parcel is physically separated from adjacent resource lands by 
existing development features, including a shared asphalt driveway to the 
west serving multiple residences and an irrigation canal to the north, 
Planning could not determine that the parcel or portion of the parcel cannot 
reasonably be sold, /eased, rented, or otherwise managed as part of a 
commercial farm or ranch operation due to the predominance of Class I-IV 
soils and the agricultural capability classifications of the property.

Re: Letter of Support - 
Dirling Family Non-Farm Dwelling Application 
To the Josephine County Planning Department: 
My name is Emily Bertman, and I am a neighboring property owner at 4751 Lower River 
Rd in Grants Pass. I am writing in strong support of the Dirling family's application to 
construct a non-farm dwelling on their property, and to respectfully encourage the 
Department to reconsider its denial of that request. 
I have come to know the Dirling family through our shared road and have spoken at 
length with their son, Ray Jr., about their situation. His parents are aging and are no 
longer able to comfortably navigate the stairs in their current home. The proposed 
dwelling would allow them to remain close to their family, on property their family 
already owns, in a home designed to meet their needs as they get older. Meanwhile, 
Ray Jr. would move from the existing accessory dwelling unit into the parents' current 
home, making full use of the existing structures. This is a thoughtful, family-centered 
plan that causes no waste of resources and no disruption to the surrounding area. 
I understand that the denial was based on a determination that the Dirlings' land is 
farmable. As a neighboring landowner and an active organic farmer myself, I want to 
offer my perspective on that characterization. The field in question lies directly across 
the driveway from my property, and it is my observation, shared, I understand, by at 
least three other farmers who have assessed the land, that it cannot support even basic 
forage crops such as hay. The proposed home site is located near the existing 
residence, and would have minimal footprint relative to the parcel as a whole. In my 
view, designating this ground as productive farmland when experienced local farmers 
have found it unproductive would be a hardship on the Dirting family without a 
corresponding agricultural benefit to the community. 
I hope the Department will weigh this appeal with the full picture in mind- a family 
working to care for their elderly parents with dignity, on land that holds litUe practical 
agricultural value, with a plan that is sensible, modest in scope, and deeply rooted in the 
kind of rural community values we all share here in the Rogue Valley. I wholeheartedly 
support the Dirling family's request and ask that it be approved. 
With appreciation for your consideration, 
Emily Bertman

To whom it may concern, 
On the morning of July 3, 2026 at 10AM we, Raymond Dirting and Amanda Dirting had a conversation with 
Luis S Hernandez, The homeowner of 4803 Lower River Road Grants Pass, OR 97526. Louis Hernandez 
has been farming his 20 acres for the past couple of decades. He is an alfalfa hay and hazelnut farmer. 
He currently has 281 hazelnut trees on his property which is adjacent to the subject properties shared 
driveway. 
He received a letter from the county stating that our approval to build our one-story cottage had been 
denied. He told us because he is 87, He feels he would not like to write a letter however, if his late wife 
was still here, "she would have written one already." He gave us permission to "please quote him" by 
stating that in his opinion, the EFU land in subject that we have applied to build a house on is not 
farmable land and he would "never farm it". He said "it is too small". He said that "The county should let 
us build our cottage for our elderly parents to age in place in, and he would be happy to see us build." He 
also said that "as a neighbor to the property in question he has no reason to not build but wish's that the 
county would vote to let us build." 
Thank you so much for your time and your consideration, 
Amanda and Raymond Dirling 
To the County Commissioners Office and Whom it May Concern, 
My name is Caleb Hunter. I am a 4th generation farmer here in 
Southern Oregon. My family owns and operates APPLEGATE 
INVESTMENTS Co. Located at 6955 HWY 238 Jacksonville, OR 97530. We 
mainly farm alfalfa fields and cattle. We have 240 acres under 
cultivation. My family has been farming our land since the 1950's. I was 
approached by the Dirling family to give my professional opinion on the 
subject property located at ( 36-06-15-CO TL 2800) which is currently 
undeveloped land on Lower River Rd. When researching the property 
location, size and soil I have come to the conclusion that I would NOT 
farm this land. Even if I lived next door. The property size is too small.

plant any kind of crop. The driveway that the property has an easement

driveway that too many other property

satisfy our farming requirements and standards for a favorable and 
successful farm. It is in my professional opinion that the properties best 
use is for generational living. Allowing a small home to be built in order 
for the Dirling's to age gracefully in place and in doing so only using 2% 
of the observed "good soil" is using the property to it's fullest potential 
in both enhancing and preserving value to the property and also 
surrounding properties. 
Thank you for your consideration and your time, 
Caleb Hunter

This letter is provided to the Dirling Revocable Living Trust to document the expert opinion 
of Richard Vogl, Hydrogeologist, who holds a Bachelors and Master's degree in Geology 
from California State University, Los Angeles, and is a registered geologist in California, 
Certified Hydrogeologist, and Certified Engineering Geologist, with over 40 years of 
professional experience in the field. This expert opinion is related to the above referenced 
property in Joesphine County, Oregon (Subject Property). 
1. Executive Summary 
The subject property is an undeveloped parcel in southern Oregon with a thin veneer of 
topsoil, ranging from approximately 12 to 36 inches in depth, with an average of 22 inches, 
overlying a dense stream-gravel deposit. These soil conditions were recently assessed and 
documented by numerous potholes conducted across the property by an excavation 
contractor (See attached test pit photos). These physical soil conditions materially restrict 
the effective rooting zone, reduce available water storage, limit nutrient retention, increase 
drought stress, and create severe limitations for sustained crop production. Based on the 
described soil profile, the land should not be characterized as viable cropland without 
extraordinary and economically disproportionate intervention. 
2. Physical Soil Limitations

such a barrier. A restrictive layer may include gravel, rock, dense clay, or other materials

FINDINGS OF CONSISTENCY IN REGARD TO THE BAKER 
DWELLING NOT IN CONJUNCTION WITH FARM USE 
Application was made May 20, 1993, for consideration of a request 
by Buck N. and Lindi Lu Baker, for establishment of a home not in 
conjunction with farm use on property located in the Exclusive Farm 
District, described in the Assessor's Records as T 36, R 6, Section 
15-3, Tax Lot 3100. 
Section 6.030(4) 
includes such a use by administrative permit, 
requiring the owners of surrounding properties be notified of the 
request and be given the opportunity to comment. 
Notification complying with ordinance requirements was mailed June 
1, 
1993. 
The 10 day period for objections expired on June 11, 
1993, and no objections were received. 
I. CRITERIA: 
Section 6. 030 ( 4) establishes the review criteria for non-farm 
dwellings in the Exclusive Farm (EF) zone, requiring findings that 
the proposed dwelling satisfy all the following criteria: 
A. 
It is compatible with commercial farm uses as identified in ORS 
215.203 (2) (a). 
B. 
Is consistent with the agricultural land use policy of the 
State Legislature as expressed in ORS 215.243. 
C. 
Does not interfere with accepted farming practices as defined 
in ORS 215.203(2) (cl and does not materially alter the overall 
land use patterns in the area. 
D. 
Demonstration 
that 
the 
residence will 
be 
situated upon 
generally unsuitable land for the production of farm crops and 
livestock, 
considering the terrain, 
adverse 
soil or land 
conditions, 
drainage and flooding, vegetation, 
location and 
size of tract. 
E. 
The lot or parcel has been disqualified for valuation for farm 
use under 308.370 pursuant to the requirement of ORS 215.236. 
II. 
EVIDENCE: 
A. 
The property is zoned Exclusive Farm totaling 4.75 acres. 
B. 
The property is located in an area with a mixture of private 
ownership on lots from . 35 to 5. 63 acres. 
There are about 
1 
thirty residential dwellings within one quarter of a mile, with 
a mixture of farm residential and residential uses within one 
half mile. 
C. 
There are no intensive farm operations on adjacent lands, with 
the closest commercial farm a quarter to the mile to the north 
being a dairy farm. 
No letter of concern or objection were 
received by reason of the notice letter. 
D. 
The property has agricultural soils rated from Class IIw to 
Class IVw. The proposed homesite is 
to be in an open meadow, 
with Class IIw Newberg Fine Sandy Loam soil. 
E. 
This site is within the floodway of the Rogue River. 
Any 
construction will need to 
comply with the 
Flood Hazard 
Ordinance. 
F. 
The County Watermaster's Office indicated that this part of the 
property does not have irrigation rights. 
G. 
The property receives a 406 residential assessment. 
H. 
The owners should sign a disclosure statement that recognizes 
that the farm use in the area has priority over the residential 
uses. 
II I. FINDINGS: 
A. 
The property is 
zoned EF, 
which is consistent with the 
Josephine County Comprehensive Plan. 
B. 
The parcel in question was created by Warranty Deed 271/643 in 
1970, and is a valid parcel. 
c. The approval of the dwelling will be compatible with farm 
practices in the area. 
It will not be adjacent to any farm 
activities, and the existing farm operations in the vicinity 
are buffered by both distance and intervening land holdings. 
The intervening uses include the Rogue River to the south, 
Lower River Road to the north, residences to the east and west, 
Garcia Subdivision to the northeast, and Country Estates Mobile 
Home Park to the east. 
D. 
The nature of the farm practices in the area show no potential 
for conflict with the location of the proposed residence. 
The 
residence will be located on the southern end of the parcel 
near other residential dwellings, 
which will maximize the 
distance to existing commercial farms. 
E. 
The land use pattern for the immediate area is clearly small 
parcel-residential. 
2 
F. 
David A. 
Bassett, 
Consul ting Engineer, 
has issued a letter 
dated June 2, 1993, stating that the house will have "no impact 
or rise on the 100 year Base Flood Elevation." 
G. 
The lack of irrigation, parcel configuration, plus small parcel 
size and adjacent residences, make this lot unsuitable for farm 
use. 
H. The property does not require declassification due to the 
residential assessment. 
I. 
The disclosure statement has been signed. 
IV. 
CONCLUSION: 
A. 
Based upon the preceding evidence and findings, 
the Planning 
Director concludes that the application for a non-farm dwelling 
complies with the requirements of Josephine County and State 
law pertaining to such matters. 
V. 
DECISION: 
Therefore, the Planning Director grants a permit to Buck N. and 
Lindi Lu Baker for the placement of a residence not in conjunction 
with farm use on property heretofore described.

for Conditional Uses in the Exclusive Farm District.

shall not be consider~d a nuisance condition in an Exclusive Farm 
District, provided that such actions are consistent with the Oregon 
Revised Statutes 215.203 to 215.273 and the Oregon Forest 
Practices Act and do not extend beyond the boundaries of the 
Exclusive Farm District. 
Nothing is this regulation is intended to 
interfere with normal agricultural or forest practices that might

-Subject property: 
4735 Lower River Road 
Grants Pass, Oregon. 
We are neighboring property owners residing at 4961 Lower River Rd. Grants Pass, Oregon 
We have never received any notification of the current building plans at 4735 Lower River Rd. 
We are writing to express our concerns regarding the ongoing development occurring at 
4735 Lower River Rd., (LRR). 
Background. 
The subject properties that make up our community is on a private road off of lower River Road. 
It consists of four home sites. One of the home sites was the site of a home that had burned years and 
years ago that home site has been split into three parcels. 
One site, that consists of two parcels is being developed as one home site, will be developed at a 
future undisclosed time. 
The other single parcel is being developed currently, with two homes. 
This pushes our community to a total of seven homes. It again. 
CONCERNS: 
Inspections during building is absolutely necessary to hold accountable to regulations. 
Overbuilding in a floodplain where agricultural and rural properties may be converted to denser 
residential use. 
When agricultural or farm properties are developed at higher residential densities, the resulting 
changes can affect traffic, privacy, drainage, floodplain conditions, agricultural operations, and the 
overall rural character of the neighborhood. 
It is important that development decisions carefully consider whether proposed densities and uses are 
consistent with the existing character and applicable land use protections. 
Concerns-we are down River from this property so anything that washes out from a flood situation will 
come towards us. The large shop building is built inconsistent with regulations having the doors of the 
large metal shop not in the correct direction for a flood situation. 
When we moved to this neighborhood in 2019 -the property at 4735 Lower River Rd. consisted of a 
single-family residence and one large metal shop building. 
Since then an additional dwelling described as an ALS (with non permitted items-full kitchen facilities, 
short term rental use),has been constructed resulting in a total of three substantial structures on the 
property. 
Current-We understand that another approximately 2800 square-foot home is now being proposed or 
considered. 
A well has been dug. 
Plans are made, but, the proposal was denied. 
Now, soils are being tested at the site to prove poor agricultural use. The site is being made to appear 
useless for agricultural use, again to help prove a home site is best use. 
The soil on this site is no different than most of the soil in this beautiful agricultural zone -Sandy, 
Rocky, River bed soil the most sought after, fertile soil anywhere. 
Concern- we are convinced that following the initial denial, legal or procedural strategies, are being 
used to circumvent the substance of that decision, and to ultimately obtain approval through a modified 
or renewed application. 
Concern-is that if approved, another request could eventually follow for an additional ALS unit 
associated with that new home effectively creating a small housing development. 
As neighboring property owners, we respectfully ask: 
At what point does continued expansion exceed what is permitted under agricultural land use 
regulations? 
Are accessory dwelling units, ALS units allowed (with full kitchen facilities) in addition to multiple 
primary residences on this property? 
What limits exist to prevent incremental overdevelopment of ea parcel? 
Please clarify what protections are in place to preserve, productive, agricultural soils, existing 
farm operations, space, (home density)and the rural character of lower River Road. 
Please identify opportunities for neighboring property owners to receive notice. Review 
proposals and provide meaningful public comment before decisions are made. 
We respectfully ask that any future land used decisions, recognize the value of this area and 
protect the qualities that make lower River Road and the Rogue River corridor, a special place to 
live.
